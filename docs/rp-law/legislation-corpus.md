TITLE I—BILL
SEC. 101. PREAMBLE.
The purpose of this bill is to clearly define and establish how future bills are drafted and outlines the specific usage of following definitions in order to standardize practices across chambers.
        SEC. 102. REPEAL OF CONFLICTING LEGISLATION
- Repeal of the Congressional Codification Act.

        SEC. 104. IMPLEMENTATION
- Implementation Date.
- Transfer of Administrative Functions.
- Enforcement.

Subtitle B—Repeal of Conflicting Legislation
SEC. 111. CONTENT.
The purpose of this subtitle is to repeal conflicting or outdated legislation.

SEC. 112. REPEAL OF THE CONGRESSIONAL CODIFICATION ACT
Upon the enactment of this bill, H.R. 0144 “Congressional Codification Act” shall be repealed in its entirety, and any provisions therein shall no longer have any legal effect.

Subtitle C—Implementation
SEC. 131. CONTENT.
The purpose of this subtitle is to establish a clear date and means of implementation and how it will be enforced.

SEC. 132. IMPLEMENTATION DATE
This Act shall take effect immediately upon passage. All future bills must comply with the standards herein, and the templates provided by the originating chambers leadership.

SEC. 133. TRANSFER OF ADMINISTRATIVE FUNCTIONS
Provisions for administrative functions, such as the definition for bill structure and format, shall be transferred to the internal policies established within their respective congressional chambers. Chamber leadership shall hold the authority to update such internal policies as necessary.

SEC. 134. ENFORCEMENT
The chamber leadership of the originating bill shall be responsible for compliance. Non-compliant bills shall be returned to their sponsor for revision. Only passing compliant bills may be sent forward.

CONGRESSIONAL LAW PROPOSALS
H.R. 0354 "War & Peace Powers Act"

Section I: Short Title

-
- This bill shall be titled the “War & Peace Powers Act”, it may be cited as such during formal reference

- It may be referred to by code, or common acronym ‘WPPA’

Section II. Contents

-
- The following Congressional resolution limits the Presidential Office’s ability to initiate or escalate military actions abroad.

- War may only be declared with a majority Congressional approval, with Ownership and/or presidential authorization if the opposite wishes to initiate a campaign against an enemy group

- The Office must be notified at least 48 hours before campaign commencement into hostilities or situations of imminent hostilities, and armed forces are prohibited from remaining for longer than 60 days

- This period can be extended for a maximum of 30 days if the Presidential Office determines it is necessary to complete safe withdrawal of troops (w/o consultation)

- Any military engagement beyond the initial 60-day period or 30-day extension must be ratified by the Congress where the POTUS seeks explicit authorization for continued involvement

- Requests submitted pursuant to Subsection 2.1.1&2 must be transmitted to the standing Speaker of The House for announcement of voting within the Office

- The same terms apply to any ultimatum to be delivered to a hostile force; the Office must be notified

- Congress will have the authority to terminate the use of armed forces in hostilities by passing a concurrent resolution without Presidential approval necessary if deemed unbeneficial to CUSA

- The President may veto a concurrent resolution passed by Congress to terminate the use of force - the Office however can override the veto with a ⅔ vote.

- Although the President is Commander in Chief of all divisions, thereby with authority to issue orders; only the Congress may ratify a declaration of war, set and provide appropriate military funding

- The following resolution limits the Presidential Office’s individual ability to negotiate terms of peace.

- The Office must be notified 48 hours in advance before terms of peace are delivered to the opposition or national representatives are sent to agree to terms abroad

- To declare a ceasefire, the Presidential Office must consult the Office per Subsection 2.3.1, and gain majority approval & Ownership authorization.

- The Presidential Office may draft terms of peace to be reviewed by Congress, and transmitted to the Speaker of The House for announcement of internal voting.

- Surrender of all armed forces may only be ordered in an absolute emergency, not requiring Congressional or Ownership approval.

- The following resolution limits the Presidential Office’s individual ability to form alliances with other Groups or Militaries.

- The Office must be notified 72 hours in advance to review the terms of an alliance, requiring majority, judicial (Supreme Court of Justice), and Ownership approval.

- All proposed long-term alliances (defined as over 60 days in length) will abide by the above terms

Section III. Reasoning

-
- Its inception will ensure that the constitutional powers of Congress & the POTUS are carefully balanced to prevent unilateral initiation of hostilities & unnecessary involvement

- To allow the Office to assert its role in military decision-making, such that a President may not initiate potentially damaging war campaigns without bipartisan approval

- To prevent any incompetence in the Presidential Office to have far-reaching consequences

Section IV. Waivers

-
- This may be waived only by the Ownership team in rare exceptions where war, peace, or alliance is necessary beyond significant doubt

- In the case of temporary alliance (under 60 days), the Office requires notification at least 36 hours before the set announcement time for the announcement of internal voting by the Speaker of The House where a majority & Ownership approval is required

Section I: Short Title

-
- This bill shall be titled the “Personnel Firearms Safety & Accountability Act”, it may be cited as such during formal reference

- It may be referred to by code, or common acronym ‘PFSAA’

Section II. Contents

-
- To ensure the safe and appropriate usage of firearms within the United States Military, guidelines and sanctions for failure to adhere to these standards have been written below:

- It shall be prohibited for any military personnel to point a firearm at another person unless the use of lethal force is required to protect life, property, or to carry out responsibility of lawful order.

- It shall be prohibited for any military personnel to use their firearms as a means of intimidation, coercion, ridicule, harassment, or threat.

- It shall be prohibited for any military personnel to resolve personal conflict or dispute with firearms.

- Formal duels to decide the outcome of a scenario or argument shall be prohibited.

- It shall be prohibited for any military personnel (especially divisional superiors) to utilise firearms as a method of punishment or discipline.

- Any military personnel found in violation of this bill may be subject to criminal prosecution under the Code of Justice or other applicable laws and face suspension, demotion, termination, etc. by the court.

- Military personnel who have had these rights violated have the right to file a complaint(s), sue, or seek appropriate redress via civil lawsuit.

- Minor violations where judicial action is not required or where the victim has decided it unnecessary may be handled individually by the agents of the Military Education Department or divisional HICOM to discipline a perpetrator without further escalation.

Section III. Reasoning

-
- To secure safe and righteous usage of firearms by military personnel by law and prevent unnecessary use of lethal force.

- To impose punitive measures upon violators and provide a safe environment for military personnel in regards to arms.

- To prohibit acts of needless violence and aggression through establishing guidelines and sanctions for misuse.


Section IV. Waivers

-
- This bill may only be waived by the Chairman in rare exceptions.

RESOLUTION
To establish a Congressional Library named “The Library of Congress”.
Resolved by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,
SECTION 1. DEFINITIONS.
- The “Executive Branch” is defined as the following:
- The Department of Justice or “DoJ.”
- The Department of Defense or “DoD.”
-  The Department of the Navy or “DoN.”
-  The Department of the Army or “DoA.”
-  The Department of Homeland Security, or “DHS.”
-  The Executive Office of the President of the United States, which includes:
- The President of the United States or “PoTUS”
- Vice President of the United States or “VPoTUS”
- Department Secretaries.
- White House.
-  Any other Federal Department or Agency that falls under the Executive Office or its respective Executive Departments.
- The “Judicial Branch” is defined as the Supreme Court of the United States.
- “Divisional Command” is defined as any unit of command that falls under the Branches defined in Section 1.
- The “Legislative Branch” is defined as the following:
- The House of Representatives or “HoR.”
- The United States Senate or “USS.”
SECTION 2. PROVISIONS.
- There is hereby established a Congressional Library, to be known as the “Library of Congress,” which shall serve as the official archival repository of publicly available materials of the Federal Government.
- The Library shall archive publicly available documents and/or compositions from all entities defined in Section 1.
- Congress shall be responsible for the collection and administration of materials from the entities defined in Section 1.
-  The Legislative Branch shall be administered separately by each respective Chamber.
- The Library shall be organized into the following categories:
-  Each Chamber will be in charge of managing its own category.
- The Executive Branch will be managed by the Joint Oversight Committee.

TITLE I—GENERAL PROVISIONS
SEC. 101. DEFINITIONS.
- ‘Personal features’ or ‘Features’ shall refer to an individual's gender, identified gender, religion, sexual orientation, race, nationality, physical appearance, disabilities, and remaining protected articles outlined in Section 102.
- ‘Provisions’ shall refer to the contents of this Act regarding discrimination types, definition of discrimination and applicability of discrimination.
SEC. 102. DISCRIMINATION TYPES.
- Discrimination types officially prohibited by law are as follows:
- Discrimination on the basis of gender or identified gender.
- Discrimination on the basis of religion.
- Discrimination on the basis of sexual orientation.
- Discrimination on the basis of race, ethnicity or nationality.
- Discrimination on the basis of physical appearance or disability.
- Discrimination on the basis of familial situation, employment, income or social class.
- Discrimination on the basis of political view.
- This does not apply to political decisions, and appointments as they heavily depend on the individual’s political views, plans, and more.
SEC. 103. DEFINITION OF DISCRIMINATION.
- Discrimination is the act of directly and intentionally excluding, performing unlawful actions on, or intentionally engaging in derogatory, defamatory or violent acts against an individual solely or primarily based on their traits (defined in section 102).
- Discrimination applies only when the individual targeted has suffered tangible or measurable harm from the event. This includes, but is not limited to, being handed stricter punishments (in comparison to others for similar offences) by a member of High Command due to your traits.
- Acts of discrimination must be proven with sufficient evidence.
- Individuals must be proved to have acted in a way in which the nature has contradicted provisions in Section 102 and Section 103.
- Behavior, where an individual directly harasses, in any form, insults (related to aforementioned classifications), noticeably decreases quality of respect or treatment of an individual solely on the basis of their features, is not tolerated, and breaches outlined provisions within this Act.
- Individuals may not receive extra benefits over others due to their features, over others tangibly and measurably proven more capable or competent (this applies to cases such as appointments to a senior rank, where an internal promotion would have sufficed).
- Individuals may not receive differentiated treatment solely due to their traits in section 102.
SEC. 104. ENFORCEMENT.
- Violations may be reported to divisional leadership. Leadership must take such reports seriously and failure to handle and apply internal punishments may be prosecuted or punished.
- If a violation is already being prosecuted in a court of law or if a case concerning the violation has already adjourned, divisional leadership may not handle or apply any punishment to the reported violation unless it is an order of the court.
- If a person wishes to pursue a divisional report against a violation they must waive from their right to prosecute the violation before divisional leadership applies any punishment to the accused individual. This does not apply if leadership decides to leave it un-punished or deems the reported action to not be a violation of this Act.
- The ruling of any court takes priority over the decision of leadership and may result in rescission or restitution.
- Leadership will not be held liable if they use their best judgement and deem a report to not be in violation of this Act, even if the court later rules the reported action to be discriminatory, as long as leadership has taken the report seriously and have properly reviewed it. Leadership shall only be held liable if there’s a clear refusal or failure to look into the report and concern themselves with it.
- Violations of this Act may only be punished in accordance with the standards set in the code of justice.

Subtitle B—Statement Limitations
SEC. 121. COURT STATEMENTS.
- A court case shall have 3 separate statements for each side, each starting with the prosecutor—:
- Opening Statement.—The Prosecution introduces and explains the charges and accusations to the court. The Defense gets a chance to respond to the allegations.
- Cross Examination.—Both sides of the trial get to present their evidence and witnesses in this statement. Each witness questioned by either party may be questioned by the other immediately after, unrelated to who's presenting their statement.
- Closing Statement.—Both sides summarize and conclude their case and make their final remarks to the allegations.
SEC. 122. TIME CONSTRAINTS.
- Each opening statement may not exceed 15 minutes.
- Each cross examination may not exceed 1 hour.
- A cross examination on a witness immediately after the opposing party, outside of one's own cross examination phase may not extend 5 minutes.
- In cases with under 10 articles each side may only have an accumulated time of 5 minutes per article for each cross examination.
- Each closing statement may not exceed 15 minutes.
- Any dilatory practices by either side to force the opposite into a compromise by abusing time constraints is not permitted.
- The American Bar Association must reprimand Attorneys intentionally delaying or prolonging court statements for the above purposes.
- The presiding Judge may force a side into ending their statement if they exceed the time constraints.
- Failure or refusal to comply with this may result in contempt of the court.
- Any case violating the listed time constraints may be investigated by the American Bar Association to determine whether a punishment is due and whether the presiding Judge has failed to ensure compliance with the time constraints.
- An accepted recess is not counted towards the listed time constraints.
SEC. 123. STATEMENT ELAPSE.
- If any statement exceeds the listed time limit, the judge or ruling party must terminate the statement.
- If in a case the judge or the ruling party allows statements to go over the time constraint, it may be seen as grounds for a mistrial.
- The American Bar Association has the right to investigate and punish the individuals responsible.

Subtitle C—Admissible Evidence
SEC. 131. CLASSIFIED EVIDENCE.
- A prosecutor or defendant that utilizes evidence labeled “Classified” that pertains to the case and wins will not be punished for leaking.
- A prosecutor or defendant that utilizes evidence labeled “Classified” that pertains to the case and loses will undergo a review conducted by a third party as to whether the classified evidence was relevant to the case and reasonably used.
- The third party conducting the review shall be at minimum a Federal Court. With a seated Federal Judge.
- If it was found to be irrelevant or used in a neglectful manner, while not being unclassified, the party who first presented the evidence to the court shall be subject to punishment in accordance with the set regulations for leaking.
SEC. 132. ILLEGAL EVIDENCE.
- Any evidence obtained through an illegal process, by committing a crime, or by infringing constitutional rights, for the purpose of obtaining said evidence is inadmissible, and may not be used for any future or present charges, convictions, or punishments.
- This does not include conduct that is incidental, unrelated, or not connected to the method used to obtain the evidence.

SEC. 141. CASE ASSIGNMENT.

- The rotation must be strictly confidential and pre-determined.
- The list must be updated after every rotation.
- There must be a finalized rotation list for the next rotation before the current rotation is implemented meaning the Department of Justice leadership must plan the next rotation list ahead before implementing a new rotation. The next rotation must be final and may not be changed once the current rotation is implemented.
- The rotation must be final and may not be changed afterwards.
- All rotations must be kept confidential to the Department of Justice leadership and the Chief Justice. The United States Congress may demand disclosure through a formal subpoena, if it’s necessary to confirm proper implementation and to confirm that cases are assigned correctly.
- The rotation can be waived for cases where the assigned judge would have conflict of interest, if the assigned judge recuses himself, if the assigned judge is on a leave of absence, if the assigned judge is suspended, or if there is any other reason for the assigned judge to not be able to participate in the trial.
- The Department of Justice leadership and the Chief Justice may not knowingly assign a case to a judge with conflict of interest and must to the best of their abilities, ensure that the assigned judge does not have a conflict.
- Waiving the rotation refers to skipping a judge on the rotation and instead assigning it to the next judge on the rotation who meets the necessary criterias.
- If a judge has to be skipped due to any of the aforementioned reasons in this section, he shall be the first consideration in the next case.
- Rotations shall be implemented in a way that respects the different classifications of trials. Judges shall have a choice in whether they want to be included in the rotation of a lower court or not.
SEC. 142. CASE ACCEPTANCE.
- All charges must be reviewed and approved by the presiding judge for the case before the case is accepted. Approved charges must have substantial evidence backing them.
- Unapproved charges must be removed from the case before the trial may proceed.
- Charges added while the case is in progress must be approved by the presiding judge.
- In order for a case to be accepted the prosecutor must provide at minimum 3 counts of charges supported by substantial evidence that the defendant allegedly violated.
- Multiple counts of a single charge or multiple charges that collectively total 3 separate counts may also be accepted by the presiding judge
- Both the defendant and prosecutor must be verified members of the “CUSA Official” main server in order for the case to be accepted.
- Both parties must join the official Department of Justice server in order for the case to proceed. Banned members must be unbanned temporarily for the duration of their trial.
SEC. 143. COUNTERSUIT PROHIBITION.
Any person being sued may not initiate a trial against their current prosecutor until the case comes to a conclusion.
SEC. 144. HEARING.
- Any and all trials may only be heard in the court house of the CUSA game or in a designated special ticket in the DoJ server.
- A discord hearing may only be conducted in a trial if the ruling party determines that either of the parties is not able to attend an in-game hearing of the trial.
- There must be a designated hearing ticket for the trial hearing which shall only be open for the duration of the hearing and must be closed if the court is not in session.
- A transcript of the hearing ticket must be provided to both parties of the trial within 24 hours of adjournment.
- Before the court is in session all both parties and their respective legal counsel or consultant must be added to the hearing ticket.
- Witnesses may be restricted entrance to a court hearing until their testimony is required if deemed necessary by the presiding judge.
- In all trial hearings before any party or witness makes any statement on the record, they must be sworn in by the presiding judge to tell the truth.
- All parties and witnesses before they make any statement on the record shall be asked to take the following oath: “Do you swear to tell the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth?”.
- Any statement made without this oath is not considered to be on the record and it may result in the case being classed as a mistrial.
- Violating this oath may result in criminal charges of perjury or other disciplinary measures for Attorneys employed by the Department of Justice. A clear violation of this oath may also result in the case being classed as a mistrial.
SEC. 145. RECESS.
- Both the prosecution and defense are able to use a recess during their cross examination.
- Both the prosecution and defense are each able to use only one recess during a trial. Recesses have a limit of 10 minutes, the time will be decided by the judge, however it can be suggested by the requesting side.
- A request for a recess can be accepted or denied by the presiding Judge. The Judge may not ignore the request for a recess.

SEC. 152. ACCEPTANCE TIME FRAMES.
- Any motion submitted while the court is in active session must be reviewed by the presiding judge within 30 minutes of its filing.
- This requirement shall apply only to motions filed by a party during that party’s designated statement.
- Where a motion is filed outside the submitting party’s designated statement, the presiding judge must review it within 45 minutes of its filing.
- A motion to rejoin must be reviewed and either granted or denied within 10 minutes of submission, and regardless of which party’s turn is active at the time of filing.
- The given time frames may be waived by the presiding judge under extreme or exceptional circumstances.
- In such cases, the time limit may be extended by up to 12 hours from the time the motion was filed.
- The submitting party may request proof of  these extreme circumstances from the presiding judge.

Subtitle F—Attorneys and Legal Officers.
SEC. 161. DEFINITION.
- A legal counsel refers to a bar licensed individual who may provide legal advice or representation to a client to ensure compliance with laws and regulations.
- A legal consultant is an individual who may provide legal advice to a client.
SEC. 162. REPRESENTATION.
- Only a legal counsel may provide direct representation in a court of law.
- Citizens shall only have 1 legal counsel or 1 legal consultant employed, for any legal matter, at a time.
- All citizens have the right to be represented by a legal counsel in any legal matter, including but not limited to interrogations, hearings, depositions, and more.
- All citizens have the right to have a legal counsel or consultant present in any legal matter or to consult with them about any legal matter, including but not limited to interrogations, hearings, depositions, and more.
- All legal counsels and consultants must perform to the best of their abilities, in the interest of their client. The failure / inability to do so may result in criminal charges for misrepresentation, sabotage, breach of contract, or other criminal charges, as well as a permanent disbarment or other disciplinary measures through the Department of Justice and the American Bar Association.
- Any counsel or consultant who is aware that he is not able to perform to the best of their abilities must inform their client and advise reconsideration in counsel or consultation.
- No counsel or consultant shall represent or consult in cases or matters where they have a personal stake or can not act in the best interest of their client.
SEC. 163. PRIVILEGE.
- Any private communication or communication conducted in a reasonable expectation of privacy between a client and their legal counsel or consultant for the purpose of seeking legal advice  is strictly confidential and inadmissible in court.
- This does not protect neglectful communication conducted in the presence of a third party.
- It is strictly illegal to intentionally attempt to secretly surveillance a private conversation between a client and their legal counsel or consultant.
- Breach of this privilege may be seen as violation of privacy, espionage, illegal investigation, or deprivation of rights under color of law.
- Any conversation between a client and their legal counsel or consultant which is reasonably believed to be conducted in private is protected from surveillance and protected with privilege, meaning any statement or admission in such a conversation is inadmissible.
- Any counsel or consultant violating privilege may face permanent disbarment and potential charges such as violation of privacy, espionage, illegal investigation, deprivation of rights under color of law, or dereliction of duty.

Constitution of Clockwork’s United States Army

We, the people of the Clockwork's United States Army (CUSA), united in our shared faith in justice, cooperation, and prosperity, enact and adopt this Constitution to establish a peaceful and prosperous society. Thankful for our strength and diversity, we vow to create a more perfect union, provide a common defense, advance the general welfare, and secure the rights and liberties of all the people of our governed and virtual world.

This Constitution establishes a government of three equal branches: the Legislative branch, the Executive branch, and the Judicial branch, capable of being the people's voice, to impartially enforcing our laws, and to encourage the concepts of justice and order. We honor the dedication of our founders and members past and present and are pledged to making this system better through amendments to serve our community's changing needs. Through this enduring relationship, we seek to build a legacy of unity, integrity, and respect for each other by all CUSA residents.
ARTICLE I
The Legislative Branch.
§ 1 - Establishment and Powers
- The Legislative Branch of the Clockwork’s United States Army (CUSA), established under Article I, consists of a bicameral Congress made up of the House of Representatives and the Senate. Congress holds the exclusive power to enact legislation, approve Presidential appointments, and conduct investigations, including oversight on the government. Its core function is to represent the people by drafting, debating, and passing laws that affect their daily lives.
1.1. The House of Representatives holds the exclusive authority to bring forth an impeachment resolution against government officials as outlined by this constitution.
1.2. The Senate holds the exclusive authority to confirm presidential nominees, and to convict and deliberate upon impeachment trials.
- Legislation becomes official once passed by the House of Representatives and Senate with a simple-majority and signed by the President. If the President vetoes a proposal, Congress may override the veto with a ⅔ super-majority in the House of Representatives and the Senate. Congressional legislation can be subject to judicial review and interception if found in violation of the Constitution. Congressional legislation can regulate the executive, judicial, and legislative branches provided that they do not practically alter their form, powers, or functions unless by the power of an amendment to the Constitution.
- Each House may determine the Rules of its Proceedings, punish its Members for disorderly Behaviour, and, with the Concurrence of two thirds, expel a Member.

§ 2 - Composition and Leadership
- The Congress officers, members of both the Senate and the House of Representatives, shall be elected by their chambers except for the following:
1.1. The Vice President of the United States shall serve as the President of the Senate but shall not vote unless it is to break a tie.
- The Speaker of the House of Representatives shall be elected by a majority vote of the members of the House. Elections shall be held every three months. The Speaker may be reelected an unlimited number of times, provided they remain fit to hold office as determined by the House.
- The President Pro Tempore of the Senate shall be elected by a majority vote of the members of the Senate. Elections shall be held every three months. The President Pro Tempore may be reelected an unlimited number of times, provided they remain fit to hold office as determined by the Senate.
- To be eligible to run for a leadership position within a chamber, a candidate must be a current member of that chamber.
4.2. An exception to this clause would be the Vice President, who shall be appointed by the President to serve as the President of the Senate.
§ 3 - Transparency
- All legislative processes, laws passed, and actions of the United States Congress shall be public and accessible to all citizens of CUSA. Congress shall not conduct proceedings in secret or pass laws through secret channels, except where necessary for the protection of national security, ongoing investigations, or other compelling interests as defined by law. Congress may withhold disclosure of non-binding resolutions, internal deliberations, or procedural actions without legal effect, so long as doing so does not hinder public accountability. Such non-public proceedings shall be limited in scope, subject to oversight, and classified information shall be declassified as soon as conditions permit. This ensures citizens have the opportunity to examine legislative processes and hold their representatives accountable while safeguarding vital interests.
§ 4 - Impeachment and Removal
- The House of Representatives shall have the sole authority to bring articles of impeachment against the President, Vice President, and all other officials of the Government, including members of the Executive, Legislative, and Judicial Branches, for treason, abuse of power, corruption, incompetency, or other serious crimes. Impeachment by the House requires a simple majority vote.
- The Senate shall have the sole authority to try all impeachments. Conviction and removal from office require a two-thirds (⅔) majority vote. Upon conviction, the Senate may also disqualify the individual from holding future public office. Impeachment trials are political in nature and are not subject to judicial appeal.
- If the President is impeached, the Chief Justice of the Supreme Court shall preside over the Senate trial.
3.1. If the Chief Justice has a conflict of interest, the Deputy Chief Justice shall preside.
3.1.1. If the Deputy Chief Justice is also disqualified, the duty to preside shall pass to the longest-serving Associate Justice. If unavailable or disqualified, it shall pass in order of seniority to the next longest-serving Justice, until a qualified Justice is found.
3.2. For all other impeachments, the President of the Senate shall preside. In the absence of the President of the Senate, or in the case of an impeachment of the Vice President, the President Pro Tempore of the Senate shall preside.
3.2.1. If the presiding officer has a conflict of interest, the Senate may, by a majority vote, remove them from the presiding role and appoint the next eligible member to preside.
§ 5 - Congressional Subpoena Powers
- Congress may subpoena any American citizen, including cabinet members, agency heads, and officers of the Executive and Judicial branches, to appear and testify or give information and documents relevant to execution of laws, administration of federal programs, and alleged misconduct within the executive or judicial branches.
1.1. Congressional Oversight Hearings may also request testimony from the President, Vice President, or Executive Office of the President staff.
1.1.1. While these officials may decline to testify on constitutional or legal grounds, such refusals may be reviewed by the Supreme Court in exceptional cases demonstrating a reasonable need for testimony.
- Hearings shall be conducted in closed session when testimony or materials may violate an individual’s reasonable expectation of privacy, risk compromising national security, or ongoing investigative proceedings.
2.1. In all other cases, Congress shall retain discretion to determine whether a hearing is public, sealed, or closed, and whether a summary or transcript shall be released to the public.
2.2. Congressional subpoena power extends to the production of documents, records, other relevant material, or other sworn evidence, under oath, whether or not a hearing is scheduled.
2.3. Witnesses subpoenaed must receive advance notice of the hearing's subject matter, and may be accompanied by legal counsel or advisors. All testimony shall be given under oath and recorded as part of the Congressional Record unless otherwise restricted for national security reasons.
2.3.1. A written subpoena must be delivered at least one week prior to the scheduled hearing and must include the date, time, and location of the hearing, the general subject matter, a list of any requested documents or records.
2.3.1.1. Emergency subpoenas may only be issued in exceptional cases where testimony or the production of sworn materials is necessary to meet urgent legislative purposes. In such cases, the subpoena can be issued up to 24 hours in advance.
- Congress must exercise its subpoena power for legitimate oversight or legislative purposes, and not to harass or unduly burden executive operations. Subpoenas may be challenged in the Supreme Court if they are overly broad, issued in bad faith, not relevant to legislative or oversight authority, or if the testimony may potentially harm or compromise the national security of CUSA.
3.1. National security concerns must be specific and do not automatically void a subpoena or hearing. Congress retains oversight authority, subject to Supreme Court review if challenged.
3.2. If a subpoena is invalidated, Congress may reissue it on new grounds or appeal the Supreme Court’s decision. Congress shall avoid repetitive or excessive summons that unduly interfere with an official’s duties.
- Congress may, as part of its oversight powers, investigate ethical misconduct, abuse of authority, or dereliction of duty by government officials, by the relevant committees from both chambers, and recommend disciplinary action to the President or the Ownership Team when necessary.
- All Congressional subpoenas carry the force of law. Individuals or entities must comply unless granted relief by the Supreme Court. Unjustified refusal or failure to comply may result in legal or procedural action as permitted by law.
§ 6 - Incompatibility
- No one can be a member of Congress (either the Senate or the House of Representatives) and at the same time hold a job in the federal government under the Executive or Judicial branches. Additionally, any individual holding an office under the authority of the United States (executive government or any other civil positions excluding the United States Congress) may not simultaneously serve as a Senator or Representative during their tenure in that office.
ARTICLE II
The Executive Branch.
§ 1 - Executive Authority
Executive power will be vested in the President of the United States, as Government and Clockwork's United States Army Head, acting as the Chief Executive of the Executive Branch, and the Vice President, who will be second in command among the executive officers and President when the latter is not available.
§ 2 - Presidential Powers and Duties
- The President of the United States has the authority to:
1.1. Appoint and remove members of the Cabinet.
1.2. Issue Executive Orders.
1.2.1. An Executive Order may also be superseded by any subsequently enacted legislation that directly contradicts or overrides it.
1.3. Call emergency sessions of Congress during a time of crisis.
1.4. Grant pardons and reprieves for federal offenses, except in the case of impeachment.
1.5. Veto a legislation enacted by congress so long the President has a reasonable cause to veto.
1.5.1. A legislation that’s been rejected by a Presidential Veto may be returned to congress, alongside the reasoning for the veto, for a revote. If the second re-vote of the bill passes a super-majority (⅔) within Congress, it can be signed into law without the signature of the President.
1.6. The President can declare war and sign war treaties with the consent from Congress, which will require a majority vote from both the House of Representatives and Senate.
- The President of the United States will have the mandated-powers to, so long that it aligns with the law, and does not violate the constitution or any other legal authority:
2.1. Enforce and administer all laws passed by Congress.
2.2. Serve as Commander-in-Chief of all the military forces, except as otherwise restricted.
2.3. Represent the United States and the White House in all matters diplomatically.
- The President will be required to:
3.1. Deliver a State of the Union speech no less than once per term, within the first three months of the term.
3.2. Insist on loyal observance of all laws.
3.3. Maintain communication with the Judicial and Legislative branches to ensure balance of powers.
§ 3 - Vice Presidential Duties
- The Vice President shall:
1.1. Assist the President in all such executive functions as are assigned to them under their agreement or by law.
1.2. Preside over the Senate with the power to cast tie-breaking votes.
1.3. Serve as Acting President when the President resigns, is impeached, or is incapacitated.
1.4. Act for the President temporarily whenever the President cannot take over responsibilities.
§ 4 - The White House and the Executive Office of the President
- The White House is the official workplace of the President of the United States.
- The Executive Office of the President of the United States is the central office of the White House that supports the work of the President.
- The Staff of the Executive Office of the President serve by the appointment and at the pleasure of the President of the United States. White House staff cannot hold any direct control or influence over government functions. The office does not require the advice nor the consent of the United States Senate.
3.1. The White House Chief of Staff is the highest ranking member of the Executive Office of the President. The White House Chief of Staff serves at the appointment and the pleasure of the President.
3.2. All White House staff positions, with the exception of the White House Chief of Staff, are not of a cabinet level.
§ 5 - Executive Departments
- The Executive Departments may be organized into branches, units, and divisions, each branch led by a Cabinet official appointed by the President.
- These divisions, departments and units shall be responsible for the administration of the White House's policies and laws and shall be subject to congressional supervision.
§ 6 - The Cabinet of the United States
- The primary role of the United States Cabinet is to act as the principal advisory body to the President of the United States.
- Beyond advising, cabinet members are responsible for managing the operations of their respective executive departments. This includes overseeing government agencies, enforcing regulations, and administering programs that affect the daily lives of Americans.
- The President shall nominate, and with the advice and consent of the Senate, appoint the Attorney General (as a member of the U.S. Cabinet), alongside the Secretary of Justice, who, while not a cabinet member, serves as key officials within the Department of Justice and reports directly to the Attorney General. For other executive department heads serving in the Cabinet, the President may appoint them without the requirement of Senate consent unless otherwise provided by law, subject to judicial review for consistency with this Constitution’s separation of powers, and approval from the Ownership Team.
§ 7 - Prohibition of Dual Service
- No person holding any office or employment under the Executive Branch of the United States shall simultaneously serve in any capacity within the Legislative or Judicial Branches that involves participation in legislative functions or judicial deliberations.
1.1.  No member of the Legislative Branch shall simultaneously hold any office or employment within the Executive or Judicial Branches.
1.2. No person serving in any capacity within the Judicial Branch that involves judicial deliberations or legal judgment shall simultaneously hold any office or employment within the Executive or Legislative Branches.
§ 8 - Presidential Term Duration and Limitations
- The President of the United States shall serve a term of six months. No person shall serve as President for more than two terms (consecutively or not), whether by succession or appointment.
1.1. Any person who has served two terms shall be permanently ineligible to hold the office of President of the United States again.
ARTICLE III
The Judicial Branch.
§ 1 - Power of the Judiciary
The Judicial Branch shall have the sole authority to interpret and apply the laws and the Constitution. It shall function with dedication to justice, constitutional integrity, and due process. While maintaining its autonomy in judicial rulings, the Judiciary,  including the Supreme Court. The Judicial Branch will operate in coordination with the Department of Justice, and will remain subject to its oversight.
§ 2 - The Supreme Court
- The Supreme Court shall be the highest court. It shall have:
1.1. Original jurisdiction in cases involving interpretation of this Constitution, disputes between branches of government, and matters of significant national importance as defined by law.
1.2. Appellate jurisdiction over all other legal matters under the laws of the United States, subject to exceptions and regulations as may be prescribed by Congress.

-  The Supreme Court shall consist of:
2.1. A Chief Justice, who shall preside over the Court and oversee its administration.
2.2. A Deputy Chief Justice, appointed at the discretion of the Chief Justice, to assist in administrative duties. The position may be filled or left vacant as the Chief Justice sees fit.
2.3. A number of Associate Justices as established by congressional legislation, passed by Congress and signed into law. All Associate Justices shall equally participate in deliberations and decisions of the Court.
§ 3 - Appointment and Tenure
- All Justices must graduate from a Law School and be nominated by the President alongside the Attorney General, with the review by the Chief Justice for legal qualifications, followed by a vote of the Supreme Court.
1.1. The Chief Justice shall be appointed by the President, with the advice and consent of the Senate.
1.2. Justices shall hold office during good behavior and remain in office until resignation, removal due to inactivity, or impeachment on the grounds of misconduct or incapacity.
§ 4 - Lower Courts
The Supreme Court shall have the authority to establish and oversee lower federal and local courts to hear civil, criminal, and administrative cases, with the approval from the Ownership Team. These courts shall be subject to the authority of the Supreme Court and bound by its legal precedents.
§ 5 - Court-Martial
- A Court-Martial is a formal legal process used to prosecute military personnel or government officials for offenses such as treason, desertion, gross insubordination, or violations of the Constitution.
1.1. A Court-Martial may be initiated through a written request by either the Attorney General or the Secretary of Defense, followed by a majority vote in Congress, via initiation through Congress independently, or via a petition submitted directly to the Supreme Court, which must be approved by a majority vote of the Justices.
1.1.1. Court-Martials may only be held when regular courts are unable to handle the offense due to its nature, or when the accused holds a military, intelligence, or national security position.
1.2. All Supreme Court Justices shall participate in Court-Martial proceedings with impartiality, fairness, and transparency.
1.3. The Chief Justice shall preside over Court-Martial sessions unless unavailable or disqualified.
1.3.1. A Justice may be removed from a Court-Martial panel by a two-thirds (⅔) vote of the Supreme Court, upon presentation of evidence indicating bias, conflict of interest, or misconduct. The Justice under review may not vote on their own removal.
1.4. The accused shall be entitled to legal counsel, the right to call witnesses, and a fair hearing. A majority vote of participating Justices shall determine the outcome.
1.5. Decisions from a Court-Martial are binding but may be appealed under exceptional circumstances such as judicial misconduct or denial of due process.
- Court-Martial proceedings must begin within 14 days of initiation, with the accused given at least one week’s notice and access to evidence. Reasonable requests for delay to ensure a fair defense may be granted by a majority vote of the Supreme Court but should not cause unnecessary postponement.
- Court-Martials shall not be initiated for purely political reasons or as a tool of political retaliation. Any such attempts shall be subject to judicial review and invalidation.
§ 6 - Judicial Powers
- The Judiciary shall exercise the following powers:
1.1. Conduct judicial review of legislation that has force of law and executive orders through petitions and raise of judicial questions to ensure conformity with the Constitution.
1.2. Resolve legal disputes and interpret the meaning and application of statutory law.
1.3. Preside over cases that arise under federal law or the Constitution.
1.4. Issue legally binding decisions and enforceable court orders consistent with due process.
1.5. Serve as the final and binding authority on the interpretation of the Constitution, with all constitutional interpretations subject to their definitive review and ruling.
1.6. Exercise subpoena power to support judicial proceedings.
1.6.1. The Judiciary may issue subpoenas for testimony, documents, or other evidence necessary for resolving cases or conducting constitutional review.
1.6.2. Subpoenas may only be issued upon finding of relevance and necessity, in accordance with due process.
1.6.3. Willful failure to comply with a lawful subpoena may result in sanctions as provided by law.
1.6.4. No person shall be obligated to give testimony against themselves contrary to constitutional protections.
1.6.5. Anyone subject to a subpoena may challenge its relevance, scope, or validity through appropriate judicial procedures, such as claims of privilege, by appeal, or other procedures as allowed by law.
§ 7 - Judicial Conduct and Discipline
- Judges must maintain impartiality, ethical conduct, and political neutrality in the performance of their duties.
1.1. Any Justice or federal judge who engages in misconduct may be subject to investigations and impeachment.
1.2. The Chief Justice and all Associate Justices of the Supreme Court are subject to the same impeachment process, which must be based on verifiable misconduct or incapacity.
§ 8 - Right to Appeal
- Every individual subject to judgment in any court within the jurisdiction of the United States of Clockwork’s United States Army shall have the right to appeal.
1.1. The appellate process shall be conducted transparently and follow established legal procedures.
- Right to Appeal
2.1. Any individual found guilty in a court of law has the right to appeal their judgment within 72 hours of conviction, unless otherwise directed by judicial order.
- Appeal Court Jurisdiction
3.1. Appeals shall be handled by the Supreme Court, unless the original case was adjudicated by the Supreme Court itself. In such cases, a special panel may be formed with the consent of both the Chief Justice and the President.
- Reasons for Appeal
4.1. Valid reasons for appeal include:
4.1.1. Abrogation of rights during trial: This includes any violation of the appellant’s fundamental legal rights as guaranteed by law or the constitution. Such violations must have had a material impact on the outcome of the trial.
4.1.2. New evidence: The discovery of substantive new evidence that was not available or could not reasonably have been obtained at the time of trial. This evidence must be credible, relevant, and likely to have altered the verdict or sentence if it had been presented during the original proceedings.
4.1.3. Procedural error: Significant errors in the application or interpretation of legal procedures or court rules during the trial process. The procedural error must be shown to have potentially affected the fairness or integrity of the trial.
4.1.4. Judicial bias or misconduct: Demonstrable instances where the judge exhibited actual bias, prejudice, or engaged in unethical behavior that compromised the impartiality of the proceedings. This may include inappropriate comments, conflicts of interest, or actions that suggest favoritism or hostility toward any party in the case.
- Filing an Appeal
5.1. The appeals shall be made through a formal written application to the Supreme Court.
5.2. The application shall be accompanied by reason and/or evidence in support of the appeal.
5.3. The Supreme Court shall have 48 hours to approve or reject the appeal for hearing.
- Appeal Hearing Procedure
6.1. Once an appeal is formally accepted, the case will undergo a review process. This will be handled in a way that ensures fairness, transparency, and that both parties have an opportunity to share their perspectives.
6.1.1. The review will include a full reassessment of the original case materials. This includes the complete trial transcript, witness testimonies, and any evidence previously submitted and considered. These documents will form the basis of the appeal review.
6.2. Either party may ask to submit new evidence or statements that weren’t part of the original case. Any such requests must be approved by the reviewing judge or panel, who will decide if the new material is relevant, appropriate, and potentially significant to the outcome.
- Possible Results, the appellate court can:
7.1. Affirm original ruling: The appellate court may determine that the trial was conducted properly and that the original verdict and sentence were legally sound. In such cases, the appeal is denied, and the decision of the lower court stands without modification.
7.2. Reform the sentence: If the court finds that the sentence imposed was inappropriate or not in accordance with the law, it may modify or reduce the sentence while upholding the original verdict. This may occur in cases where the punishment was deemed excessive or inconsistent with statutory guidelines.
7.3. The appellate court may conclude that legal or procedural errors, judicial misconduct, or newly discovered evidence rendered the trial outcome invalid. In such instances, the court may vacate the original verdict entirely, which may result in the charges being dismissed or further legal action being taken.
7.4. Order a retrial: If the appellate court finds that the trial was flawed but believes the matter still warrants judicial resolution, the court may order a new trial. This allows the case to be reconsidered entirely, before a different judge or jury.
- Double Jeopardy Protection: an individual who has been acquitted or convicted of a criminal offense shall not be subjected to a second prosecution for the same charge. This protection upholds the constitutional principle of finality in criminal proceedings and safeguards individuals from repeated prosecutions for the same alleged conduct. Exceptions to this protection may apply in the following limited circumstances:
8.1. Mistrial: If the original trial was declared a mistrial due to procedural irregularities, a jury in a legal case that is unable to reach a unanimous or required majority decision on a verdict, or other legal issues that prevented a conclusive verdict, retrial may be permitted without violating double jeopardy protections.
8.2. Proven Fraud or Misrepresentation: If it is demonstrated that the conviction or acquittal was obtained through fraudulent means, such as perjury, fabrication of evidence, or intentional misconduct by a party, the court may authorize a retrial in the interest of justice.
8.3. New Evidence of Gross Judicial Misconduct: In rare and exceptional cases, where compelling new evidence reveals substantial misconduct by a judge or key court officials-such as bribery, coercion, or deliberate miscarriage of justice-that directly influenced the outcome of the original trial, a retrial may be permitted despite a prior acquittal or conviction.
§ 9 - Legal Immunity
Legal immunities as granted by law to public officials do not protect against court-martials or removal through impeachment. Impeachment remains fully permissible for criminal conduct, abuse of power, or other serious offenses, regardless of a public official’s protected status.
§ 10 - Judicial Accountability and Standards for Tenure
Supreme Court Justices, including the Chief Justice, Deputy Chief Justice, and Associate Justices, shall hold office during good behavior, as defined by judicial conduct and performance standards. However, any Justice who consistently fails to fulfill the minimum requirements of judicial responsibility may be subject to formal evaluation and disciplinary action.
- Standards for judicial activity must be publicly defined and approved by the Judicial Overseers.
- If a Justice is found, through verifiable evidence, to be in violation of these activity standards (for example, prolonged unexcused inactivity or failure to engage in required judicial functions), the Judicial Overseers are authorized to initiate disciplinary action. Disciplinary measures may include formal warnings, issuance of activity strikes, temporary suspension, or formal removal from office.
§ 11 - Judicial Transparency
To uphold public trust and ensure accountability, the Supreme Court is required to maintain transparency in all legal proceedings and rulings, except where national or organizational security requires confidentiality.
- All court rulings, legal interpretations, and judicially enacted policies must be made publicly available within 48 hours, unless they contain classified information.
- The Court must maintain a comprehensive and publicly accessible legal archive that includes, at minimum:
2.1. All court decisions, including majority, concurring, and dissenting opinions.
2.2. All legal provisions and amendments codified in the Code of Justice.
2.3. A complete record of appeals filed and their outcomes, including any modifications to original rulings.
- In cases where rulings involve sensitive or classified content, such decisions must still be formally documented and preserved in a sealed archive. These records must be clearly labeled with a Judicial Seal and may only be accessed by authorized personnel in accordance with the security policy established by the Judicial Overseers and the Chief Justice.
§ 12 - Judicial Interception of an Executive Order
- Federal judges of the United States may petition the Supreme Court to review any Executive Order issued by the President that they find to be in conflict with this Constitution or pre-existing law. Likewise, Justices of the Supreme Court may raise judicial questions as outlined by this Constitution.
1.1. The Supreme Court shall only take up such petitions or judicial questions (if raised) when there is a clear and necessary constitutional question. It shall not review Executive Orders unless a petition is submitted by a federal judge or the issue arises in a case properly before the Court.
1.1.1. An immediate temporary injunction shall be issued, as part of the Judicial Interception of the Executive Order, if approved and taken up by the Supreme Court, blocking the enforcement of the challenged Executive Order pending constitutional review.
1.1.1.1. The Supreme Court shall conduct the review in full (active, with the exception of justices on an approved Leave of Absence, or justices with an excused absence by the Chief Justice) presence in a public legal review, whether in an in-game or Discord court hearing, of the matter within 48 hours of the Judicial Interception being authorized.
1.1.1.2. If the Supreme Court is unable to assemble within the outlined time, the deadline may be extended once, by no more than 120 hours, and only for valid reasons.
1.1.1.3. In the case the judge petitioning the Executive Order in the Supreme Court is a Justice of the Supreme Court of the United States, the same Justice is to be removed from the bench during the judicial review of the Executive Order.
1.1.1.4. A Justice of the Supreme Court may also raise a Judicial Question of Constitutionality concerning an Executive Order, which shall be treated as a formal petition and follow the same review process.
1.1.1.4.1. In these cases, the Justice who raises the question shall be temporarily recused from the review unless the remaining Justices unanimously agree that the participation of the petitioning Justice does not compromise impartiality.
- If the Supreme Court fails to conduct the legal review within the allowed time, the Judicial Interception shall be automatically lifted, and the blocked Executive Order reinstated unless a new petition is filed and accepted.
2.1. Upon initiation of the Judicial Interception, the Supreme Court shall formally notify the President that the Executive Order is under constitutional review. All standard legal procedures of the Supreme Court are to be temporarily adjusted to prioritize this matter.
2.2. This process shall ensure that judicial review of Executive Orders occurs only when required to resolve substantial constitutional or legal concerns, and not as a routine or discretionary action by the Supreme Court.
2.3. The entire procedure of the Judicial Interception must be officially recorded.
- Before a Judicial Interception can be officially approved, the Supreme Court must hold a public hearing, either:
3.1. In a Discord trial, where the case, reasoning, and implications of the order are discussed and reviewed.
3.2. During an in-game trial, where the case, reasoning, and implications of the order are discussed and reviewed.
- Following the hearing, a majority vote of the Supreme Court Justices shall be required to confirm the Judicial Interception within 48 hours of its proposal (unless with an approved extension, as established under this Constitution), whether to approve or deny.
- The entire procedure of the Judicial Interception must be officially recorded.
§ 13 - Judicial Performance and Evaluation
- A system will be established to evaluate judges performance and ensure justice is administered effectively.
1.1. The evaluation system will be created with input from the Judicial Overseers, the Chief Justice, and members of the Supreme Court to ensure fairness and clarity.
1.2. Judges will be evaluated based on clear and objective standards such as timeliness, quality of rulings, and professional conduct. The system will protect judicial independence and job security.
1.3. Results from judicial evaluations shall be used constructively to inform judicial responsibilities, identify areas for professional development, and support continuous improvement. Evaluations may guide training opportunities, resource allocation, and, where appropriate, recommendations for corrective actions in line with judicial policies.
§ 14 - Judicial Authority and Constitutional Oversight of the Supreme Court
- The United States Supreme Court's primary duty includes reviewing documents produced by CUSA organizations, excluding the Department of Homeland Security, the Secret Service, the Naval Criminal Investigative Services, the Department of State, and Congress, To ensure their constitutionality and legality. The Supreme Court shall:
1.1. Review Department of Justice Documentation.
1.1.1. Review and provide advisory proposals to documents directly owned and legally controlled by the Department of Justice and (or) The Supreme Court of the United States, specifically concerning their legal implications and constitutional compliance, the Supreme Court does not require a formal request from the Department of Justice and may offer proposals at any given time.
1.1.2. This excludes the documents under the American Bar Association, the Education department, the U.S. Marshals, and the general documents such as the Department of Justice handbook.
1.2. Reconsider Previous Supreme Court Rulings: Review and reconsider previous rulings in exceptional circumstances where a clear error of law or significant change in constitutional understanding is demonstrated, following a formal petition process.
- The Supreme Court and its justices shall and will:
2.1. Exercise privileges and duties responsibly and diligently.
2.2. Provide clear, concise written reasoning for decisions and advisory opinions.
2.3. Act in a timely manner, upholding the integrity of CUSA's legal framework.
§ 15 - Judicial Interception of Legislation
- Federal judges of the United States may petition the Supreme Court to review any official act of Congress, law, or resolution that they find to be in conflict with this Constitution or pre-existing law. Likewise, Justices of the Supreme Court may raise judicial questions as outlined by this Constitution.
1.1. The Supreme Court shall only take up such petitions or judicial questions (if raised) when there is a clear and necessary constitutional question. It shall not review laws passed by Congress unless a petition is submitted by a federal judge or the issue arises in a case properly before the Court.
1.1.1. An immediate temporary injunction shall be issued, as part of the Judicial Interception of the Legislation, if approved and taken by the Supreme Court, blocking the enforcement of the challenged congressional bill pending constitutional review.

1.1.2.1. If the Supreme Court is unable to assemble within the outlined time, the deadline may be extended once, by no more than 120 hours, and only for valid reasons.
1.1.2.2. In the case the judge petitioning the congressional bill in the Supreme Court is a Justice of the Supreme Court of the United States, the same Justice is to be removed from the bench during the judicial review of the bill.
1.1.2.3. A Justice of the Supreme Court may also raise a Judicial Question of Constitutionality concerning legislation, which shall be treated as a formal petition and follow the same review process.
1.1.2.4. In these cases, the Justice who raises the question shall be temporarily suspended from the review unless the remaining Justices unanimously agree that the participation of the petitioning Justice does not compromise impartiality.

ARTICLE IV
Amendments.
§ 1 - Purpose and Scope
- Amendments or revisions to this Constitution, when adopted through the proper amendment process, are official and binding changes that may override or modify existing provisions. However, their authority is subject to the following limitations:
1.1. Supersession and Integration: Any new amendment may take precedence over existing provisions. Superseded provisions shall remain part of the constitutional record for historical and legal clarity, unless expressly repealed.
- Amending Foundational Articles: Amendments that propose changes to Articles I through IV may be adopted, but must meet the following conditions:
2.1. They must be approved by a supermajority (⅔) of the voting in Congress.
2.2. They must include a statement of necessity and constitutional justification, demonstrating why such a change is essential to the continued fairness, functionality, or justice of the organization.
- Preserving Constitutional Integrity: Even when altering foundational Articles, amendments must uphold the principles of separation of powers, due process, and equal representation. No amendment shall be valid if it seeks to abolish or render powerless any entire branch of government without providing for a fair and functional replacement.
 § 2 - Amendment Procedure
 The amendment procedure of this Constitution shall be as follows:
- Initiation and Discussion:
1.1. The proposal for an amendment shall be initiated for discussion in a meeting of the President, the Supreme Court, and the United States Congress collectively.
1.1.1. The meeting shall be made open to the public at large or to the public invited by any one of the three parties.
- Vote in Congress:
2.1. Congress will proceed to vote on the finalized amendment after the session. A ⅔ majority vote is required in both the Senate and the House of Representatives before the amendment can move ahead.
- Presidential Action:
3.1. The amendment will then be presented to the President for signing.
3.2. The President may sign the amendment into law or veto it, with a written statement of reasons for the veto. In the event of a veto, the amendment may be resubmitted after reconsideration, with the President's objections stated, and again for a vote by Congress.
- Veto and Implementation:
4.1. In case the President vetoes the amendment, the veto can be overridden by Congress by a ¾ supermajority vote in both chambers of Congress.
4.1.1. Upon successful override or Presidential ratification, the amendment will take effect.
- Supremacy of Amendments:
5.1. Any amendment adopted under this Article shall hold equal authority to the Constitution and may modify, override, or repeal any prior provision of law, code, or constitutional clause, except where otherwise restricted by Article 4 or explicitly non-amendable sections.
- Public Proclamation:
6.1. The President shall cause public proclamation of the adopted amendment and provide it to all citizens of CUSA, upon which the amendment shall be incorporated into the Constitution.
§ 3 - Judicial Supervision
The Supreme Court may review the procedural validity of proposed constitutional amendments before or during their ratification to ensure compliance with the requirements set in this Constitution. Once an amendment has been lawfully adopted in accordance with these procedures, it shall become part of this Constitution and shall not be subject to annulment by the Supreme Court on procedural or substantive grounds. Congress shall not circumvent procedural rulings by resubmitting identical amendments without addressing the Court’s concerns.
 § 4 - Record Keeping
Every amendment proposal, meeting minutes, Congress votes, judicial review, and Presidential action will be recorded and made public so that the amendment process is open. Citizens will have the right to access such records in order to hold their government accountable.
ARTICLE V
The Freedom of the People.
§ 1 -  Legislative Authority and limits
The Legislative Branch shall have the authority to enact laws applicable to all citizens of the United States, foreign officials subject to U.S. jurisdiction, officials of the United States Government, and courts established pursuant to law. However, Congress shall not enact any law that limits or violates the fundamental rights and freedoms guaranteed to the people, including but not limited to freedom of speech, religion, assembly, or the right to a fair trial, and all other fundamental rights as established in Article 6 (VI) - the bill of rights.
§ 2 - Executive Authority and Due Process
The Executive Branch shall faithfully execute the laws of the United States and ensure the protection of the rights and liberties guaranteed to all persons. No individual shall be deprived of life, liberty, property, taxes, valuables, or duties without due process of law. No person shall be held to answer for a capital crime without indictment by a Grand Jury, nor shall any person be tried twice for the same offense, except where the original adjudication was procured through fraud or other improper means.
§ 3 - Judicial Protection of Rights
The Judicial Branch shall uphold and protect the fundamental rights of all individuals within its jurisdiction. Every citizen accused of a crime shall receive a speedy and public trial by an impartial jury, be informed of the nature and cause of the accusation, confront the witnesses against them, obtain compulsory process to secure witnesses in their favor, and be provided with assistance of counsel for their defense.
§ 4 - Wartime Jurisdiction
When the Executive Branch, with authorization by a majority vote of Congress, enters into a treaty declaring a state of war, the Department of Justice shall retain legal jurisdiction over Fort Tedder and all other locations where active military personnel operate under the Code of Justice. Individuals engaged in active wartime service shall remain subject to this provision until a formal treaty of peace is signed by the President of the United States.
ARTICLE VI
 Bill of Rights.
§ 1 - Right of Speech and Expression
Every CUSA citizen and employee shall have the fundamental right to freely express their thoughts, ideas, and opinions, including on government property and in official capacities. This right is essential to a free and open society and supports democratic participation. However, this freedom is subject to reasonable limitations where speech:
- Incites violence or rebellion: Speech that directly encourages or provokes imminent acts of violence, insurrection, or rebellion against the government or public order is prohibited, as it poses a clear and present danger to the safety and stability of the community.
- Is hate speech, harassment, or threats: Expressions that target individuals or groups based on race, ethnicity, gender, religion, sexual orientation, disability, or other protected characteristics in a manner intended to intimidate, threaten, or harass are restricted to protect the rights and dignity of all persons.
- Speech that reveals, discloses, or undermines the security protocols of government operations, or that unlawfully exposes classified or sensitive information, is prohibited to preserve security, privacy, and effective governance.
§ 2 - Right to a Fair Trial
Every person charged with a violation of CUSA law is entitled to the following fundamental rights to ensure a fair and just legal process:
- Presumption of Innocence: Every individual accused of an offense shall be presumed innocent until proven guilty.
1.1. The burden of proof lies with the prosecution, which must establish guilt beyond a reasonable doubt.
- Right to be informed of charges: The accused shall be promptly and clearly informed of the specific charges brought against them, including the nature and cause of the accusation.
2.1. The accused shall have timely access to all evidence held by the prosecution that is relevant to the charges, including both favorable and unfavorable material, as part of a fair discovery process.
- Right to a fair and just trial: The accused has the right to a trial that is conducted fairly, impartially, and openly, ensuring transparency and accountability while safeguarding the integrity of the judicial process.
- Right for legal representation: Every accused individual has the right to be represented by legal counsel of their choice.
4.1. If the accused is unable to find representation, the government is required to appoint qualified legal representation at no cost.
- Right to present a defense: The accused may produce evidence, call and cross-examine witnesses, and present any other relevant material in their defense to ensure a comprehensive examination of the case.
- Right to appeal: Following the conclusion of the trial, the accused has the right to appeal the verdict or sentence to a higher court or tribunal for review, in accordance with established appellate procedures.
- Right to an impartial judge and jury: The accused shall have the right to be tried before an impartial judge and may choose to have the trial conducted either by a judge alone or by a jury composed of seven members randomly selected from the community.
§ 3 - Double Jeopardy Protection
No person shall be prosecuted or convicted more than once for the same offense by the same court or governmental authority. This protection ensures finality in legal proceedings and prevents harassment through repeated trials.
- A person may be retried for the same offense only under limited circumstances as outlined under this constitution.
§ 4 - Right of Privacy
All CUSA members are entitled to the right to privacy concerning their personal communications and documents. This right protects individuals from unauthorized access or surveillance, subject only to lawful exceptions.
- Personal messages: People of CUSA have the right to keep their personal messages confidential and free from unwarranted intrusion.
- Documents: The privacy of personal documents, whether digital or physical, shall be respected and safeguarded.
- Private group chats, unless there is a valid warrant from a legal or military court procedure: Private group communications are protected under this right, except when access is authorized by a valid warrant issued through a proper court procedure based on established legal standards.
§ 5 - Right against Unreasonable Punishment
Punishments imposed shall be proportionate to the severity of the crime committed. Under no circumstances shall any punishment be cruel, excessive, inhumane, or degrading. The principles of justice and human dignity must guide all sentencing decisions.
§ 6 - Right to Bear Arms (Where applicable)
Every citizen has the right to keep and bear arms for lawful purposes, including personal defense and participation in sanctioned activities. This right shall not be infringed, but it may be subject to reasonable restrictions to ensure public safety, prevent misuse, and maintain order.
§ 7 - Right of Peaceful Protest
The public has the right to peacefully protest or express opposition to laws and governmental decisions. This right is protected as long as the protest does not incite rebellion or violence, nor obstruct or interfere with the lawful operations of the armed forces.
- Protests are prohibited on the property of divisional or federal government areas and facilities, where security and order must be maintained without disruption.
§ 8 - Right to Habeas Corpus
- Every person has the right to challenge their detention or imprisonment before a court.Any individual who is detained shall have prompt access to judicial review, and if the detention is found to be unlawful, the court shall order their immediate release.
1.1. This right can be suspended in cases where the safety of the public clearly demands it.

§ 9 - Protection Against Unreasonable Searches and Seizures
All people of CUSA are protected from unwarranted searches and seizures of their person, property, or communications.
- Warrants: searches or seizures require a valid warrant issued by a court. The warrant must be based on probable cause and must clearly describe what is being searched and why.
- Digital Privacy: this protection includes digital data, devices, and communications.
- Emergency exceptions: searches without a warrant are only allowed in urgent situations where immediate action is necessary to prevent harm or protect public safety.
- Illegally obtained evidence: evidence obtained through unlawful searches cannot be used in court or disciplinary actions, except under rare, legally defined exceptions.

DEFINITIONS
- Executive Branch

1.1. The Executive Branch is responsible for implementing and enforcing laws. It is led by the President of CUSA, who serves as both the head of state and head of government. This branch also includes the Vice President, Cabinet members, and federal government agencies.
1.1.1. The primary role of the Executive Branch is to enforce laws, oversee the military, and manage the day-to-day operations of the federal government.
- Legislative Branch

2.1. The Legislative Branch of CUSA is responsible for making laws. It consists of Congress, which is divided into two chambers: the Senate (upper chamber) and the House of Representatives (lower chamber).
2.1.1. The primary function of the Legislative Branch is to propose, draft, debate, and enact legislation for the benefit of CUSA and its members.
- Judicial Branch

3.1. The Judicial Branch interprets laws and ensures they are applied fairly and constitutionally. It consists of the Supreme Court and other lower courts, including federal and local courts.
3.1.1. The Judicial Branch reviews laws and executive actions to determine their constitutionality and resolves disputes involving laws, individuals, or the government.
- United States Congress

4.1. The United States Congress comprises the Senate and the House of Representatives. Both chambers can propose legislation, which must be approved by both before being sent to the President for approval or veto.
4.1.1. The United States Senate (upper chamber) is one of the two chambers of Congress, responsible for legislating. It is composed of Senators who represent the members of CUSA.
4.1.1.1. The Senate has the exclusive power to confirm some presidential appointments and to conduct impeachment trials initiated by the House of Representatives. It has the authority to convict and remove federal officials from office.
4.1.2. The House of Representatives (lower chamber) is one of the two chambers of Congress and shares the responsibility of legislating. It is composed of Representatives who represent the members of CUSA.
4.1.2.1. The House has the exclusive power to initiate impeachment proceedings. It works together with the Senate to pass legislation, which must be approved by both chambers before becoming law.
- Presidential appointments

5.1. Presidential appointments refer to individuals nominated by the President to serve in key government positions, including in the executive branch, judiciary, and federal agencies. Some appointments require Senate confirmation.
- Legislative Powers

6.1. Legislative powers refer to the constitutional authority granted to Congress to propose, debate, and enact laws. This power allows Congress to govern the nation through binding legislation.
- Legislative Bill

7.1. A legislative bill is a proposed law introduced in either the House or the Senate. It must pass both chambers and be signed by the President to become law.

- Presidential Veto
8.1. A presidential veto is the President’s constitutional power to reject a bill passed by both chambers of Congress. The vetoed bill is returned to Congress with an explanation. Congress can override the veto with a two-thirds vote in both the House and the Senate. If not overridden, the bill does not become law.
- Constitutional Amendment

9.1. A constitutional amendment is a formal change to the Constitution of CUSA. It can be proposed by the President, the Supreme Court, or by Congress, and may pass by a ⅔ majority in both the House and the Senate.
- Impeachment

10.1. Impeachment is a formal process initiated exclusively in the House of Representatives to bring charges against government officials for serious offenses such as treason, bribery, or other high crimes and misdemeanors.
- Judicial Interception

11.1. Judicial Interception is a formal process by which the Supreme Court, upon petition from a federal judge, temporarily blocks the enforcement of a congressional law or executive order believed to conflict with the Constitution. This triggers an expedited public review by the full Court within a strict time frame. If the Court fails to act in time, the block is lifted automatically. Judicial Interception ensures urgent constitutional issues are reviewed swiftly, objectively, and only when necessary.
-  Discord Trial

12.1. A Discord trial, similar to a regular in-game trial, is a judicial hearing, review or a legal process where a judge (and sometimes a jury) hears evidence and arguments to decide if someone is guilty or not, or to settle a legal dispute, in Discord. A discord-trial should take place in a private or public channel, in consideration to the classification of the trial, in the Department of Justice Server, in which only the concerning parties to the legal dispute may communicate.
- Civil Positions

13.1. Civil positions are non-military roles within the government. They include positions such as federal judges, cabinet members, and other appointed officials in the executive and judicial branches. Civil officers are subject to processes such as appointment and impeachment under the Constitution.
- Executive Officers

14.1. Executive officers are individuals within the Executive Branch who lead or manage federal departments, agencies, divisions, or programs.
- Judicial Overseers

15.1. Judicial Overseers are officials within the Department of Justice with the authority to oversee the Judiciary. These officials are: the Secretary of Justice and the Attorney General, as the Judicial Branch is subject to oversight of the Department of Justice, as established under this constitution.
- The Supreme Court of the United States

16.1. The Supreme Court of the United States is the highest court in the federal judiciary. It has ultimate appellate jurisdiction and the authority to interpret the Constitution and federal laws.
- Petition

17.1. A petition is a formal written request submitted to a court, asking it to take action or issue a ruling on a specific legal matter.
- Appellate Court

18.1. An appellate court, or court of appeals, reviews the decisions of lower courts to determine whether legal errors occurred that may have affected the outcome. It does not conduct new trials but evaluates the application of the law in prior proceedings.
18.1.1. Appellate review is the process through which a higher court examines the proceedings and decisions of a lower court to determine if they complied with the law. It addresses legal or procedural errors, not factual disputes.
- Judicial Review

19.1. Judicial review is a procedure in which the Supreme Court interprets whether executive orders or passed laws are inline with the constitution and law, and to decide whether such government actions are lawful and constitutional.
- Judicial Question

20.1. A Judicial Question is a formal questioning of the legality or the constitutionality of a certain governmental action, such as an executive order or signed bill, that can be raised by a justice of the Supreme Court that asks the Supreme Court to judicially review said action.
20.1.1. A judicial question is considered to be parallel to a petition, and thus cannot override it.
- Public Office / Public Officials

21.1. A public office is a position within the government, where an individual is officially entrusted with the authority and responsibility to act on behalf of the public. It involves the exercise of governmental power or duties, whether through decision-making, enforcement, administration, or representation.
21.1.1. Holding a public office means serving the public interest under legal and ethical obligations, regardless of whether the position is elected or appointed.

## [CUSA] Code of Justice

Tab 1
Code of Justice
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,
ARTICLE 1. DEFINITIONS.
- Law Enforcement.—The Military Police and the Military Education Department are considered law enforcement.
- Federal Agent.—Agent of the Naval Criminal Investigative Service, Secret Service or Homeland Security.
- Government Agent.—Any person acting under official government authority, including law enforcement, federal agents, individuals acting under lawful authority of government officials/entities, high ranked military officials and government officials.
- Ruling party.—The individual or group that has authority to deliberate over conviction or acquittal of charges.
ARTICLE 2. JUDICIAL SENTENCING.
- No judge may be influenced or look for consultation during a case, except if the influence or consult originates from a superior court.
- Any influence or consultation must relate directly to the case that the judge is presiding over and may be used solely to advise or assist the judge in determining a verdict with their own independent judgment.
- Judges may issue sentences and other orders, following the listed conditions and requirements in this code, upon determining whether an accused is guilty or not per trial proceedings.
- Judges may order rescission or restitution following this code after adjudication.
- Judges may order the rescission of contracts or agreements, as part of their sentencing or order, at the conclusion of a trial, that included fraud, coercion or other illegal means as a measure to restore balance, order, and justice.
- Judges may grant specific performance following this code after adjudication.
- Judges may grant specific performance, as part of their sentencing or order, at the conclusion of a trial, to restore a state or situation which was lost or disturbed through illegal actions as a measure to restore balance, order, and justice.
- Exile, demotion, and suspension sentences may not result in the removal from a government position.
- Being convicted in court for violations of this code does not hinder any impeachment from taking place for the offense, as impeachments are a question of whether an official should remain in office and not whether an official should be punished.
- A guilty conviction from this code may be used as a reason to pursue articles of impeachment.
- There may be no sentencing that could remove or cause the removal of a government official from their position.
- A court sentence may be appealed to a court higher than the one that issued it.
- The higher court may decide to change the sentence following the listed conditions and requirements and other lawful appeal proceedings.
- This may not change the initial verdict unless the verdict itself is being appealed.
- The result of a sentencing appeal may only replace the initial sentence with a lighter sentence or affirm the sentence issued by the lower court.
- No sentencing or its summation may restrict an individual for more than 5 days on discord.
ARTICLE 3. COUNTS.
- People subject to this code may be prosecuted or charged with multiple counts of offenses.
- This means that multiple offenses would not result in just 1 charge of the Article but may result in multiple charges.
- Each count is to be considered separately and is to be differentiated from one another throughout a trial and its proceedings.
- The ruling party, whether jury or judge, must consider and evaluate each count separately, including the sentencing of each count.
ARTICLE 4. STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS.
- Violations of this code may only be prosecuted for a period of 3 months after the initial commitment of the crime.
- Charges outside of the statute of limitations may not be added to any court trial.
- Violations may still be prosecuted if the case was initiated before the statute of limitations expired.
ARTICLE 5. PLEAS.
- The accused in a trial may plead guilty, not guilty, or nolo contendere.
- Guilty.—The accused pleads guilty, meaning they will not be tried any further for the accusation and will receive a guilty sentence for it by the presiding judge. Any guilty sentence through a guilty plea is still subject to regular appeals. This plea can be withdrawn if it can be proven that at the moment of the plea—
- the accused was not properly informed of their rights or the charges,
- the accused was not in the appropriate mental state to make the decision,
- the accused was illegally coerced into pleading guilty; or
- a request or motion to withdraw the plea was made before any guilty sentence was issued or executed.
- Not guilty.—The accused pleads not guilty, meaning the proceedings will continue, and the accused may decide to change their plea until the trial reaches a conclusion.
- Nolo contendere.—This means the accused does not contest the charges, meaning the case immediately moves to the sentencing. This plea can be withdrawn if it can be proven that at the moment of the plea—
- the accused was not properly informed of their rights or the charges,
- the accused was not in the appropriate mental state to make the decision,
- the accused was illegally coerced into the plea; or
- a request or motion to withdraw the plea was made before any sentencing or punishment to it, was issued, or executed.
- Parties of a trial may negotiate plea deals, in which the accused agrees to a plea for a concession.
- All plea deals and their terms must be approved by the presiding judge, both sides of the trial, and must comply with this code.
- The judge must ensure that the terms are fair and must verbally warn in case he notices unfair or unjust terms and may deny the proposed bargain.
- An approved plea deal is treated as an official and valid contract.
ARTICLE 6. SEVERABILITY.
If any title, subtitle, article, or clause of this code is struck down for being unconstitutional or any other reason, the rest of this code shall remain unaffected and continue to be enforced and followed.
ARTICLE 7. APPREHENSION.
- Law Enforcement and Federal Agents may apprehend and arrest individuals that they believe to have violated articles of this code.
- Apprehension pursuant to this article is not subject to the burden of proof and evidence.
- Departments and divisions retain the right to apprehend anyone on their respective property or any other property they’ve been granted jurisdiction over, to apprehend violators of the law or other property rules and may directly hand them over to law enforcement.
- This article can not be used or enforced for violations which are being prosecuted in court.
- Valid reasons for apprehension are doing a lawful arrest, issuing a warning or following divisional procedure such as checking the individual's profile. It is explicitly illegal to detain in ways that cause harm, such as waiting for others to aim their guns before releasing the detainee.
- All Citizens shall be informed of the reason for their arrest and the length of their arrest before being arrested.
ARTICLE 8. RESTRAINT OF CHARGED CITIZENS.
- The ruling party of a case may order the temporary suspension of an accused, from all duties.
- There may be no permanent replacement made during the suspension or as a result of the suspension.
- There must be compelling evidence of serious accusations related to the duties that the accused is getting suspended from.
- A higher court may overrule the decision of the ruling party.
ARTICLE 9. ENTRAPMENT.
- Entrapment shall constitute the acquittal from charges of a person accused of a crime. The burden to prove entrapment lies with the defendant.
- Entrapment is when a government agent induces the commitment of a crime, through luring or persuasion. It must be indubitable that the crime would not have been committed if it had not been for the involvement of a government agent.
- The affected individual must not have had any predisposition to commit the crime. Entrapment does not apply if the defendant was ready to commit the crime prior to any involvement of a government agent.
- Private citizens can not cause entrapment unless they act under governmental directives, authority, or control.
ARTICLE 10. RIGHTS OF THE CITIZENS.
- All Citizens shall have the rights to—
- Be informed of the reason and evidence of any punishment issued to them, regardless of classification,
- Freely express themselves within legal boundaries of law,
- Freely choose and exercise their religion unless otherwise provided by law,
- Request declassification of documents,
- Think freely and express their thoughts under the freedom of speech,
- Refuse following unethical or unlawful commands,
- Be protected from self-incrimination by invoking their right to remain silent.
- Any action to exercise these rights is protected by law.
ARTICLE 11. EX-POST FACTO CRIMINALIZATION.
- No person shall be subject to a punishment or criminal liability for an act that was not defined as an offense or crime at the time it was committed.
- No person shall be subject to a penalty that was not defined at the time the underlying offense for the penalty was committed.
- No penalty may be retroactively increased in severity, duration, or type unless it is proven that a more severe offense was committed than the one that the penalty was first issued for.
- Criminal law and any rule that may result in punishment or criminal liability must be accessible to those subject to it for a penalty to be imposed.
- No accused shall be put into a worse position through retroactive legislation, retroactive interpretation, or retroactive administration.

TITLE I—LOCAL ARTICLES
ARTICLE 1-0-1. JURISDICTION.
- Violations of this Title may be prosecuted in a local court, presided by a local judge or a judge from a higher court.
- Cases involving only articles from this title are classed as local trials.
- Any licensed attorney may act as a legal counsel in local trials.
ARTICLE 1-0-2. NUISANCE.
- Consistent irritation, inconvenience, or disturbance of other people is considered a nuisance. It gets in the way of comfort, tranquility, or the ability to go about daily tasks without getting annoyed.
- Punishable by up to a 1-day game ban, up to a 1-rank demotion or up to a 1-day discord mute.
ARTICLE 1-0-3. DISTURBING THE PEACE.
- An act of loud, violent, or disruptive behavior that disturbs the peace and disrupts the normal calm of a community is known as disturbing the peace. This is regarded as a criminal offense since it jeopardizes public safety and order and may involve fighting, loud parties, or other disruptive behaviors.
- Punishable by up to a 3-day ban, up to a 1-rank demotion or up to a 1-day discord mute.
ARTICLE 1-0-4. HARASSMENT.
- The persistent and targeted, unwanted behavior that is causing fear or emotional distress.
- It must be blatantly obvious to the human mind that the related behavior is unwanted, there must have been clear signs that it is unwanted, or the dignity of the person must have been violated.
- Punishable by up to a 3-rank demotion in either USM or in the related division, up to a 5-day discord mute, up to a 1-day game ban or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 1-0-5. FALSE ACCUSATIONS.
- When someone is accused of a crime, they did not commit without solid proof or evidence, it is known as a false accusation. Such charges frequently result in unjust treatment or punishment and can damage a person's relationships, reputation, and emotional health.
- The false accusation must have been made knowingly with the intent to deceive or cause harm.
- Punishable by up to a 1-week suspension from the related division, up to a 2-rank demotion, up to a 5-day discord mute or up to a 6-day game ban.
ARTICLE 1-0-6. DISCRIMINATION.
- When someone is treated unfairly or unequally due to characteristics such as race, gender, religion, age, or disability, that is discrimination.
- Any violation of the Anti Discrimination Act is considered a violation of this article.
- Punishable by up to a 3-rank demotion, up to a 1-week ban, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 1-0-7 UNJUST ENRICHMENT.
- Gaining an advantage at the expense of others without any valid reason.
- The recipient may not keep the benefit.
- The court may order restitution, restoring the position of the injured party through the return of the unjust benefit or through equal or just compensation to the injured party.
ARTICLE 1-0-8. BREACH OF CONTRACT.
- A legally binding contract is broken when one party does not fulfill their end of the bargain. The harmed party may receive compensation, and the non-compliant party may receive legal consequences as a result.
- Punishable by up to a 1-week suspension or an exile from the related division
- The court may:
- Order a rescission of the contract to restore the original position of all involved parties or
- grant specific performance so that the original contract is properly executed.
- The court may not order rescission and grant specific performance at the same time but must decide whether the contract will remain valid and be executed properly or whether it is completely invalidated and a rescission is required.
- The court must make careful consideration between the 2 options of rescission and specific performance.
- A rescission should be considered or made if the breach destroys the basis of the contract or makes the contract meaningless.
- A specific performance should be considered or made when the breaching party can still perform to uphold the contract, and the non-breaching party insists on enforcing the contract.
ARTICLE 1-0-9. RETALIATION FOR PROTECTED ACTIVITY.
- Any act committed with the intent to coerce, intimidate, or adversely affect an individual on account of engaging in protected conduct.
- The retaliation / action in response does not have to be a crime in itself.
- Protected activities refer to actions protected or allowed by law.
- Punishable by up to a 1-week ban, up to a 2-rank demotion, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 1-0-10. FALSE PERSONATION.
- Falsely representing oneself to be someone else to deceive, defraud, or otherwise cause harm.
- Punishable by up to a 3-day ban from the game or discord.
ARTICLE 1-0-11. OPENLY CARRYING A FIREARM.
- The visible possession or display of a firearm in a location or circumstance where such action is restricted or forbidden by law.
- Punishable by up to a 120-second arrest.
ARTICLE 1-0-12 THREATS TO KILL.
- A verbal, written, or implied expression of intent to cause the death of someone.
- Punishable by up to a 240-second arrest, up to a 2-day game ban or up to a 3-day discord mute.
ARTICLE 1-0-13. REFUSING ORDERS OF ENFORCEMENT OFFICERS.
- The deliberate failure or refusal to comply with lawful orders issued by authorized enforcement officers in the performance of their duties.
- Punishable by up to a 1-day ban or up to a 360-second arrest.
ARTICLE 1-0-14. DELAYING ACTIVITIES OF ENFORCEMENT OFFICERS.
- Any intentional act that obstructs, impedes, or causes unnecessary delay in the lawful performance of an enforcement officer’s duties.
- Punishable by up to a 2-day ban or up to a 240-second arrest.
ARTICLE 1-0-15. DISCHARGING A FIREARM ON FEDERAL OR MILITARY PROPERTY.
- The act of firing a firearm within the boundaries of federal or military property, except if explicitly authorized by law or regulation.
- Discharging a firearm in self-defense does not violate this article.
- Can be charged with up to a 280-second arrest.
ARTICLE 1-0-16. RESISTING ARREST.
- The intentional and physical prevention or obstruction of a law enforcement officer from committing a lawful arrest or detainment.
- Punishable by up to a 300-second arrest, up to a 3-day ban, or a 1-rank demotion.
ARTICLE 1-0-17. TRESPASSING ON MILITARY PROPERTY.
- The illegal entrance to military property.
- Punishable by up to a 150-second arrest or up to a 3-day ban.
ARTICLE 1-0-18. TRESPASSING ON FEDERAL PROPERTY.
- The illegal entrance to property of the federal government.
- Punishable by up to a 400-second arrest or up to a 7-day ban.
ARTICLE 1-0-19. FALSE IMPRISONMENT.
- The wrongful arrest or detainment of someone.
- Punishable by up to a 500-second arrest, a strike and up to a 7-day suspension in the related division/department, up to a 5-day ban or a 1-rank demotion in the relevant division/department.
ARTICLE 1-0-20. RIOTING.
- An assembly of 3 or more people causing a violent disturbance of the peace.
- Punishable by up to a 400-second arrest or a 7 day suspension in the related division / department.
ARTICLE 1-0-21. ASSAULT.
- Placing an individual into fear of receiving physical harm whether through a physical attempt to inflict harm, a verbal or written threat to inflict harm or other means.
- There must be a reasonable belief that the threat will be carried out.
- Punishable by up to a 120-second arrest, a 2-day suspension from all duties with the exception of government related duties or a 1-day ban.
ARTICLE 1-0-22. BATTERY.
- The intentional, physical harm without any prior consent.
- Punishable by up to a 260-second arrest or a 1-day ban.
ARTICLE 1-0-23. ASSAULT ON AN ENFORCEMENT OFFICER.
- Committing assault on an enforcement officer.
- Punishable by up to a 240-second arrest, a 2-day suspension from all duties with the exception of government related duties or a 2-day ban.
ARTICLE 1-0-24. BATTERY ON AN ENFORCEMENT OFFICER.
- Committing battery on an enforcement officer.
- Punishable by up to a 400-second arrest or a 2-day ban.
ARTICLE 1-0-25. CRIMINAL NEGLIGENCE.
- The act of disregard to the safety or lives of others, causing damage due to carelessness and negligent behavior.
- Punishable by up to a 200-second arrest.
ARTICLE 1-0-26. MASS MURDER.
- The act of killing multiple individuals during a single event or location.
- Punishable by up to a 2-day ban, up to a 600-second arrest, or up to a 2-rank demotion with up to a 1-month rank lock.
ARTICLE 1-0-27. TERRORISM.
- Unlawful violence or threats to coerce or intimidate for political, ideological, or religious purposes.
- Punishable by up to a 2-day ban, up to a 500-second arrest, or up to a 2-rank demotion with up to a 1-month rank lock.
ARTICLE 1-0-28. ATTEMPTED MURDER.
- Intending to murder someone without success.
- A physical action towards carrying out the intention must have been made.
- Punishable by up to a 1-day ban, or up to a 180-second arrest.
ARTICLE 1-0-29. THIRD-DEGREE MURDER.
- Third Degree Murder is when someone kills someone without premeditation/intention through an act that shows extreme recklessness or indifference to the well-being of other lives.
- The act must be of a nature where causing death is blatantly obvious.
- Punishable by up to a 1-day ban, or up to 450-second arrest.
ARTICLE 1-0-30. MANSLAUGHTER.
- Involuntary Manslaughter: The act of killing through reckless or negligent actions without the intent to kill.
- The act must have been in a careless or thoughtless nature.
- Punishable by up to a 1-day ban, or up to a 350-second jail sentence.
- Voluntary Manslaughter: The act of killing in the heat of the moment when suddenly provoked to do so.
- Punishable by up to a 2-day ban, or up to a 600-second arrest.
ARTICLE 1-0-31. SECOND-DEGREE MURDER.
- Second-degree murder is the act of intentionally killing another individual without premeditation in advance.
- Punishable by up to a 2-day ban.
ARTICLE 1-0-32.  FIRST-DEGREE MURDER.
- First degree murder is the act of intentional killing, while premeditated and pre-planned before the act.
- Punishable by up to a 3-day ban.
ARTICLE 1-0-33. POLITICAL ASSASSINATION.
- Political assassination is the act of intentionally killing a public or government/political official, such as a government leader, for ideological, political, or religious reasons.
- Punishable by up to a 10-day ban, up to a 400-second arrest, or up to a 2-rank demotion.

TITLE II—FEDERAL ARTICLES
ARTICLE 2-0-1. JURISDICTION.
- Violations of this Title may only be heard in a federal court or higher, presided by a federal Judge, a Supreme Court Justice, or a bench of Supreme Court Justices.
- Any case involving any article from this title is classed as a federal trial and may not involve representation by a local licensed attorney.
- Federal licensed attorneys may act as legal counsel in any court.
ARTICLE 2-0-2. MISREPRESENTATION.
- A false statement made with the purpose to induce someone to an action with false knowledge. Undeniable harm must have been caused as the result of the deception.
- Misrepresentation can be categorized into 3 degrees. The ruling party of a trial must determine under which degree a charge of misrepresentation falls if the above clause applies:
- Innocent.—The accused genuinely believed it to be true.
- The court may order rescission and/or restitution of the contract or decision, related to the charge.
- Negligent.—The accused carelessly gave false information without checking the facts.
- Punishable by up to a 3-day discord mute or up to a 2-rank demotion.
- The court may order rescission and/or restitution of the contract or decision, related to the charge.
- Fraudulent.—The accused deliberately and knowingly lied.
- Punishable by up to a 7-day ban as well as a blacklist from the related division or an exile from the related division with up to a 7-day ban.
- The court may order rescission and/or restitution of the contract or decision, related to the charge.
ARTICLE 2-0-3. CONSPIRACY.
- An agreement between 2 or more people to commit a crime. May be conducted both formally and informally.
- Punishable by up to a 5-day ban, up to a 2-rank demotion, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-4. VIOLATION OF PRIVACY.
- Violating the reasonable expectation of privacy or the constitutional right to privacy whether through government intrusion or not.
- Punishable by up to a 21-day ban or up to a 3 rank demotion.
ARTICLE 2-0-5. ESPIONAGE.
- Spying on divisions, departments, units, governmental institutions, or federal agencies in order to obtain classified information for illegal purposes. This includes but is not limited to documents, photos, or other intelligence.
- Punishable by up to a 12-day ban and an exile from the related division, a 2-day ban and a 3-day discord mute, or a 9-day ban and a 3-rank demotion from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-6. LIBEL.
- The act of publishing or spreading false information/statements about another person that may damage their reputation, credibility, or rank within CUSA.
- Punishable by up to a 2-day game ban and up to a 3-day discord mute; up to a 9 day game ban and a 3-rank demotion; or an exile from the related division and up to a 12-day ban.
ARTICLE 2-0-7. SABOTAGE.
- The act of hampering, deliberating, subverting, or hurting the efforts of another, usually for personal benefit.
- Punishable by up to a 4-day ban, up to a 3-rank demotion in the related division, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-8. ACCESSORY AFTER THE FACT.
- Any intentional act of aiding or assisting a criminal to prevent them from receiving a penalty for committing a crime.
- Punishable by up to one-half of the punishment to the principal crime.
ARTICLE 2-0-9. ACCESSORY BEFORE THE FACT.
- Any intentional act of assisting, aiding or encouraging a criminal act before it happens.
- This may also result in being charged with the principal crime.
- Punishable by up to the full sentence of the principal crime.
ARTICLE 2-0-10 RETALIATION.
- Committing a crime with retaliatory intent.
- Retaliatory intent is defined as the intention to punish someone or harm someone for revenge.
- Punishable by up to a 4-day ban, up to a 3-rank demotion in the related division, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-11. ABUSE OF TOOLS.
- Using in-game tools illegally, for illegal purposes, or unreasonably, without following the given guidelines and laws. Including but not limited to cuffs, taser, pepper spray, authorization tool, and more.
- Punishable by up to a 4-day ban, an exile from the related division, up to a 2-rank demotion, or up to a 5-day suspension.
ARTICLE 2-0-12. HATE CRIME.
- Committing a crime choreographed based on intents against race, religion, or other personally identifiable traits.
- A separate crime must have been committed that this charge directly refers to.
- Punishable by up to a 7-day ban, up to a 3-rank demotion, an exile from the related division, or a blacklist from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-13. INSUBORDINATION.
- Intentionally refusing to follow legal and reasonable orders given by a figure of authority appointed above them.
- Punishable by up to an 8-day suspension from the related division, up to a 7-day ban from the game, up to a 3-rank demotion from the related division or the USM group, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-14. VEXATIOUS LITIGATION.
- The action of repeatedly or intentionally initiating legal action (lawsuits, motions, or complaints), not for a legal purpose, but to harass, exhaust resources, cause harm, or intimidate the opposing party.
- Punishable by up to a 3-day ban, a 2-rank demotion from the related division, or a 3-day discord mute.
ARTICLE 2-0-15. WITNESS TAMPERING.
- Improperly or unauthorizedly interacting with a witness.
- The interaction must have had the potential to affect the witnesses’ impartiality.
- Punishable by up to a 2-week ban, or up to a 3-rank demotion.
ARTICLE 2-0-16. EVIDENCE TAMPERING.
- The alteration, destruction, hiding, or fabrication of evidence with the attempt to obstruct or interfere with legal proceedings or investigations.
- Punishable by up to a 2-week ban, or up to a 3-rank demotion.
ARTICLE 2-0-17. JURY TAMPERING.
- The illegal attempt to influence a Juror, or unauthorized or improper interaction with a Juror, outside formal proceedings.
- Punishable by up to a 2-week ban, or up to a 3-rank demotion.
ARTICLE 2-0-18. PERJURY.
- The act of violating or falsifying an oath to state the truth, either in writing or in speech when concerning a proceeding. This is also classed as lying under oath.
- Punishable by up to a 2-week ban from the game, an exile from all divisions, or a demotion in all divisions up to to the lowest rank.
ARTICLE 2-0-19. FORGERY/FALSIFICATION OF DOCUMENTS.
- The act of altering or tampering with evidence, documents, messages, or any other type of alterable material in order to justify wrongdoing, frame someone, escape charges or Justice, or to cause an unwarranted action.
- Punishable by up to a 2-week ban, up to a 3-rank demotion, or up to a 2-month blacklist.
ARTICLE 2-0-20. COERCION.
- The act of using forceful or intimidating action to force an individual to act in an involuntary manner.
- Punishable by up to a 7-day ban or a 7-day ban along with a 1-rank demotion from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-21. BRIBERY.
- The act of offering, giving, accepting, or soliciting any valuable incentive with the intent to persuade an individual to commit an unlawful or improper act.
- There must be a malicious or corrupt intent.
- Punishable by up to a 15-day ban, a demotion to E5 along with up to a 2-week rank lock, or an exile from the related division along with up to a 5-day discord mute.
ARTICLE 2-0-22. MISUSE OF CLASSIFIED INFORMATION.
- Leaking confidential information you have been entrusted with, to those who do not possess the authority to access the contents of such information.
- Punishable by up to a 10-day suspension, up to a 5-rank demotion along with up to a 3-day ban, an exile from the related division, or a 90-day blacklist from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-23. ILLEGAL INVESTIGATION.
- An investigation conducted without any legal authority or while violating laws such as legal procedures or rights.
- Only the DHS, the NCIS and the United States Congress may conduct investigations.
- Divisional leadership may conduct internal investigations within their division with approval from the respective O10 or above. If the investigation involves outside factors it must be handed to the DHS immediately.
- Punishable by up to a 10-day suspension from the related division, up to a 4-rank demotion or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-24. DEPRIVATION OF RIGHTS UNDER COLOR OF LAW.
- Whoever, under color of any law, statute, ordinance, regulation, or custom, willfully subjects any person to the deprivation of rights, privileges, or immunities secured or protected by the Constitution or laws of the United States, or subjects them to different punishments or penalties based on race, color, or citizenship, commits a federal offense.
- Punishable by up to a 7-day suspension, demotion to a rank of your choosing, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-25. DERELICTION OF DUTY.
- Dereliction of duty is the intentional or negligent failure to fulfill one's duties properly.
- Punishable by up to a 2-rank demotion, up to a 3-week suspension, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-26. MALFEASANCE.
- Malfeasance is intentional dishonest, fraudulent, or improper conduct, or intentional misuse of power by high-ranked or public officials.
- Punishable by up to a 2-week suspension, a demotion up to the lowest rank in the related division, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-27. NEPOTISM.
- A form of malfeasance done to benefit friends or family through favoritism or undue advantages.
- Punishable by up to a 2-rank demotion, up to a 2-week suspension from the related division along with up to a 3-rank demotion in the related division, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-28. OBSTRUCTION OF JUSTICE.
- The deliberate and illegal interference with government or judicial proceedings in the pursuit of justice.
- Punishable by up to a 2-week ban or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-29. CONTEMPT OF COURT.
- Contempt of the Court is when a person is causing the court to take longer on deciding verdicts or such other manner by being disrespectful or interruptive.
- Punishable by up to a 2-week ban from the game, or a 2-rank demotion.
- In cases of direct contempt, in front of the presiding judge, the presiding judge of the case may hold the violator in contempt and impose immediate punishment to it, which must be enforced once the case reaches a conclusion.
ARTICLE 2-0-30. CONTEMPT OF CONGRESS.
- Contempt of Congress is the disobedience or interference to lawful Congressional orders or mechanisms. This can include but is not limited to the refusal to comply with subpoenas, the disruption of Congressional proceedings or the interference with witnesses, documents or evidence relevant to Congressional proceedings or inquiries.
- Upon referral by the President of the Senate or the Speaker of the House, the Attorney General must ensure that violations are prosecuted in the name of the United States through the US Attorney’s Office.
- Punishable by up to a 3-week suspension from the related division, up to a 2-rank demotion, or up to a 2-week ban from the game.
ARTICLE 2-0-31. TREASON.
- Committing acts of war against the United States or intentionally harboring / aiding a traitor or an enemy of the United States.
- Punishable by a removal from up to all divisions.
ARTICLE 2-0-32. BREACHING NATIONAL SECURITY.
- The unauthorized access, disclosure, or manipulation of sensitive data.
- Punishable by up to a 1-week suspension from the related division, 2-week ban, or an exile from all divisions along with a 1-week ban.

SUBCHAPTER 1: GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 01: Definitions
- “Attorney General” refers to the head of the Department of Justice.
- “Chief of Staff” refers to the head of all Staff members in CUSA
- “President” refers to the current President of CUSA
- “Chairman and Vice Chairman” refer to the two highest ranks and owners of CUSA
- “Divisional Commanding Officer” refers to the head of a division in CUSA
- “Local Justice” refers to a Local Judge
- “Federal Justice” refers to a Federal Judge
- “Enforcement Officer” refers to all members of enforcement divisions; MED, MP, DoJ, SS and DHS.

Article 02: Persons Subject to USCJ
- All members of the “[CUSA] United States Military” owned by RenewedJourney are subject to this document of the US Code.
- All personnel within groups that are allied to the “[CUSA] United States Military '' owned by RenewedJourney are also subject to this document of the US Code.

Article 03: Citizen’s Right to a Trial
- All members of CUSA are granted a right to a fair and just trial before a punishment or verdict is delivered based on accusations and evidence.
- Only the Attorney General, the Chairman, the Vice Chairman, and the President of the United States may bypass this protocol before directly punishing military personnel.
- All members of CUSA  also retain the right to appeal a punishment delivered with or without a trial, with the only exception to this being the punishments issued by the President of the United States.

Article 04: Territorial Applicability of the USCJ

SUBCHAPTER 4: TRIAL JURISDICTION

Article-12: Classification of a Trial
- The 4 types of Court Trial in CUSA are:
- State Trial (State/Local Judge)
- Federal Trial (Federal Judge)
- Supreme Court Trial (Supreme Court)
- Federal Agency Jurisdiction

- State Trial
- A state trial consists of a singular Local Judge and an 8 person jury.
- This can be changed if the defendant or Plaintiff wishes to go to Bench Trial.
- When found guilty by the bench the defendant may go to retrial with a Jury.

b) Federal Trial
- A Federal Trial consists of a singular Federal Judge or a 12 person jury.
- Can be changed if the Defendant wishes to go to Bench Trial.
- If found guilty by the bench the defendant may not go to retrial with a Jury, the sentence still stands.

c) Supreme Court Trial
- A Supreme Court Trial consists of up to 12 Supreme Court Justices of the United States on the bench.
- Will never be judged by a jury only by the bench.
- All verdicts are final.
- Must be approved by the President and the Defendant unless the President is the Defendant.

d) Federal Agency Procedure
- Federal Agencies such as the Secret Service and The Department of Homeland Security have their own procedures to deal with certain legal issues.
- As long as these procedures do not violate any articles under the Code Of Justice it is classed as a Non-Judicial Punishment. (See Article 11)

Article-14: Procedure Before Trial
- For a Local Case, a Local Judge must be found, date and time of trial confirmed as well as the assignment of attorneys for those that wish to have one. A jury will also have to be summoned if the defendant/plaintiff should choose for one to be present.
- For a Federal Case, a Federal Judge must be found, date and time of trial confirmed, attorneys for those that wish to be represented by one and a 12 person jury if requested by the defendant.
(Juror’s will be replaced immediately if it is found that they’ve failed to appear for the trial. The appointed Judge may prosecute the Juror if necessary as well.)
- For a Supreme Court Justice Case, the Attorney General must be present, a time and date confirmed as well as an attorney for the defendant.

Article-17: Unlawful Command Influence
- Unlawful Command Influence is where a member of the military, usually a high ranking one, uses or appears to use their authority to influence the goings of military judicial proceedings.

Article-18: Challenges / Objections
- Challenges are things such as motion to dismiss and other things that will challenge the ongoings of court.

Article-19: Oaths
- In the early stages of the beginning of a trial, the prosecutor/claimant and defendant/plaintiff must be asked: “Do you swear to tell the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth?” and they need to respond with something along the lines of: “I swear to tell the truth, the whole truth and nothing but the truth so help me God.” Not staying true to this oath will result in perjury charges. (See article 65)

Article-20: Statute of Limitations
- The statute of limitations is the time limit for filing charges against the defendant.
- Any applicable evidence will remain valid up to a year after a criminal offense has occurred.

Article-21: Double Jeopardy
- Double jeopardy is the prosecution or punishment of a person twice for the same offense. If these offenses were at different times this is not applied.

Article-22: Pleas of the accused
- The defendant must state whether they plead guilty or not guilty when asked in court.
- Pleading guilty to an offense means that you have accepted you have committed that offense.
- Pleading not guilty means that you do not agree with the allegations against you.

Article-23: Court Procedure
- During court there are five separate sections being, Opening Statement followed by Cross Examination, Closing Statement, Final Ruling, and Dismissal.
- The Opening Statement consists of a presentation of the defendant’s crimes from the prosecution. The defense team presents a statement to instill doubt into either the seated judge or the jury.
- Cross Examination consists of a presentation of witness statement’s or other forms of evidence such as photo proof or audio recordings. (Clarification: Both teams can partake in this stage)
- Closing Statement consists of a statement provided by the prosecution, mainly covering how their evidence has been shown to implicate guilt. However the defense is also able to make a statement on how their arguments disprove guilt.
- Final Ruling occurs at the end of the trial and is the given sentence by the judge if the defendant has plead not guilty, and is proven guilty. If the defendant pleads guilty they are to be provided the punishment listed in the plea agreement. And if the bench finds the defendant not guilty, no punishment is delivered.
- The final stage is Dismissal, it is simply where the punishment is enacted and everyone in the stands is asked to leave the court as the trial has ended.
- Note: Objections, Motions and Challenges are allowed to be given during, Opening Statement, Cross Examination and if necessary the closing statement.

Article-24: Refusal to attend court
- Deliberately missing court times is evading justice and is committing contempt of the court. (see article 25).

Article-25: Contempt
- Contempt of court or often referred to as contempt is the offense of being disobedient to or disrespectful to a court of law.

Article-26: Depositions
- A deposition is a sworn out-of-court testimony given by a witness that is relevant to a case. Depositions are usually done before court by a representing side's attorney.

Article-27: Votings and Rulings
- A ruling is a decision made by the judge, normally on the sentence. Rulings can be appealed under some circumstances.
- A voting is how the jury makes their decision in a group and decides what will go forward to the court.

Article-28: Votes required for conviction, sentencing, and other proceedings
- In a jury, the majority will rule and their decision will be kept in mind when the judge(s) are making decisions.

Article-29: Findings and Sentencing.
- Judges and the Jury can look over findings and make a non-biased well informed decision on the discipline required for the charges. If the findings of a case are too little or of little use then the judge/jury/side judges will make decisions whether to discipline at all.

Article-30: Plea Agreements
- A plea agreement, also known as a plea bargain, is an agreement in criminal law proceedings, whereby the prosecutor provides a concession to the defendant in exchange for a plea of guilty or nolo contendere. This concession is often a reduced sentencing. Nolo contendere is a plea by which a defendant in a criminal prosecution accepts conviction but pleads neither guilty or not guilty. Presented during the Opening Statement.

Article-31: Record of Trial
- The trials prosecutor, defendant, judge/side judges and attorneys will be recorded alongside the verdict.

Manslaughter is an unlawful killing that doesn't involve malice aforethought—intent to seriously harm or kill, or extreme, reckless disregard for life. Thus, while manslaughter is a serious crime, the punishment for it is generally less than that for murder.

Article-6d: Legality and applicability of USCJ amendments and fixes
Amendments to the United States Code of Justice (USCJ) can only be enacted through legislation passed by Congress and signed into law by the President.
- The Amendment must pass both chambers of Congress by a majority vote.
- Once passed by Congress, the amendment is sent to the President for signature.
- If the President signs the amendment, it becomes law and amends the USCJ.
- If the President vetoes the amendment, it is returned to Congress.
- Congress can override the veto with a two-thirds majority vote in both the House and Senate, after which the amendment becomes law without the President’s signature.
- Once passed, the leadership of the Department of Justice is responsible for implementing the amendment in the USCJ.
- The Supreme Court is the only Government body which is able to strike down legislation if it is determined to be unlawful.
- Once all is completed and the amendment is approved by the Supreme Court and Congress and all is given the green light, you may then give the document to a Department of Justice HI-COM with proof of approval for that amendment to be put into action.
- Amendments may be vetoed by the President if it's approved by both Congress and the Supreme Court.
- A two-thirds majority vote is required to override the President's veto on the amendment.

SUBCHAPTER 2: APPREHENSION AND RESTRAINT

Article-07: Apprehension
- Apprehension shall be defined as taking an individual who has violated the US Code into custody.
- Anyone authorised to apprehend someone may do so under the reasonable belief that an offence has been committed, and the individual apprehended has committed it.
- The only personnel who are authorised to apprehend someone are enforcement divisions and units; Military Education Department, the Military Police, the Specialized Protection Unit and the Marine Corps Military Police. Enforcement divisions and units are exempt from providing proof of the reason for the arrest.
- The Department of Homeland Security and the Secret Service are allowed to apprehend someone if an offence has been committed in their guarded areas (DHS base, Headquarters), The Military Police and Military Education Department are not allowed to go in the Department of Homeland Security’s base, and only may enter the Headquarters if their request to apprehend someone inside of it is approved.
- Apprehension is not applicable if the context of the violation is being tried upon in court.
- Valid reasons for apprehension are doing a lawful arrest, issuing a warning or following divisional procedure such as checking the individual's profile. It is explicitly illegal to detain in ways that cause harm, such as waiting for others to aim their guns before releasing the detainee.

Article-08: Restraint of Citizen Charged
1. Any person subject to this chapter, and awaiting trial, may be ordered into arrest or confinement. There is no mandatory procedure nor protocol for this to take place.
2. This shall not be applicable if the person is accused of a charge that warrants only a Local case.
3. The seated Judge may rule that the defendant may be temporarily suspended from his/her position.
4. The seated Judge may enact this article without the prosecution requesting it.
5. The suspended person's position may not, under any circumstances, be permanently taken by another member.
6. When and if a person is ordered into arrest or confinement, the following steps must be taken by the apprehender.

- The individual getting apprehended is to be informed of his rights.
- The individual is to be informed of the reason for his apprehension

7. This article shall only be used when clear evidence is presented to support the suspension, and the reason for the suspension must be directly relevant to the case.
8. This article may also be enacted when the defendant's duties interfere with the timing of the trial.
9. The Judge shall not abuse this permission for personal benefit and shall always firstly think whether the suspension of the defendant is helpful for the current case or if it connects the case in any way. For example suspending a defendant from his divisional duties because he may interfere with the court proceeding with his duties.

Article-09: Legal Authority of Enforcement divisions:

SUBCHAPTER 4: TRIAL JURISDICTION
Article-11: Classification of a Trial
- The 3 types of Court Trial in CUSA are:
a. State Trial (Local Judges)
- b. Federal Trial (Federal Judges)
- c. Supreme Court Trial (Supreme Court Justices)
- Any and all trials are strictly prohibited to happen outside of the main game, all allowed locations for a trial to take place will be publicly stated by the Department of Justice. In all cases, trials will be open to the public. However, the presiding judge may choose a closed trial for only authorised personnel if there are compelling justifications for it, and such decisions are subject to review by the Supreme Court.
   a) Local Trial
- A Local trial consists of a singular Local Judge along with a Co-Judge if seen fit by the standing Judge and/or 7 Jurors. This can be changed if the defendant wishes for a bench or jury trial. When found guilty by the bench (Judge, Co-judge) the defendant may go to retrial with a Jury.
   b) Federal Trial
- A Federal Trial consists of a singular Federal Judge with a Co-Judge if seen fit by the standing Judge or 7 Jurors. Can be changed if the Defendant wishes to go to Bench Trial. If found guilty by the bench (Judge, Co-judge) the defendant may not go to retrial with a Jury, the sentence still stands.
c) Supreme Court Trial
- A Supreme Court Trial consists of up to 9 Supreme Court Justices of the United States on the bench, or a singular Supreme Court Justice member. Supreme Court cases will never be judged by a jury only by the bench. All verdicts are final unless the Attorney General, President, or Ownership team says otherwise in an official announcement to the Department of Justice and/or the CUSA public. The verdict of a trial of which is defined as a case of which must/or will be handled by a member of the Supreme Court must be approved by the Chief Justice before punishment of the verdict may proceed.
  d) Federal Agency Procedure
- Federal Agencies such as the Secret Service and The Department of Homeland Security have their own procedures to deal with certain legal issues. As long as these procedures do not violate any articles under the Code of Justice it is classed as a Non-Judicial Punishment.

Article-12: Charges
- When one is requesting a court case, a list of the articles a defendant is being charged with must be established.
- The list shall be reviewed by a Judge alongside the evidence to determine the validity of the said request. Reasoning: This is in conjunction with the redefinition of Article 5, where the label of Judge being "Legal Officer" is being removed.

Article-13: Proceedings before Trial
- For a Local Case, a Local Judge or Federal Judge must be found, date and time of trial confirmed as well as the assignment of attorneys for those that wish to have one. A body of jurors will also have to be summoned if the defendant wishes for one.
- For a Federal Case, a Federal Judge must be found, date and time of trial confirmed, attorneys for those that wish to be represented by one and a 6 person jury if requested by the defendant.
(Juror’s will be replaced immediately if it is found that they’ve failed to appear for the trial. The appointed Judge may prosecute the replaced Juror if necesarry as it could be labelled as Contempt. )
- For a Supreme Court Justice Case, a time and date confirmed as well as an attorney for the defendant. If the defendant and/or prosecution wishes for an attorney they will be provided for one, and if the defendant wishes for a bench, the Supreme Court member who accepted the case will request 2 or more members of the Supreme Court to be alongside him on the bench.

Article-14: Prohibition of Self Incrimination
- Article V of the United States Constitution protects the accused/witness from being forced to incriminate themselves in a crime. However, staying silent without a prior exclamation of exercising this right, the right is not invoked.

Article-15: Duties of Counsel
- The trial counsel shall be prosecuting for either the Plaintiff or on behalf of the United States Government.
- The Defense counsel shall be defending the defendant.
- The Prosecuting counsel shall be the standing attorney for the prosecution.
- Both attorneys are expected to perform to their best ability, failing to do so will result in actions taken by the American Bar Association with punishments accordingly.

Article-16: Accessory Before the Fact
- Accessory Before the Fact shall be defined as the conspiracy, aid, or influence of another individual committing a crime, without attendance at the setting of incidence.
- An accessory shall be subject to the punishment of the crime they assisted in.

Article-17: Accessory After the Fact
- A person who knowingly and purposely assists in covering up a crime committed by another individual or the criminal’s escape from law enforcement or punishment is guilty of being an accessory of that crime after the fact.
- Not working with or assisting law enforcement to catch a criminal is not considered assistance under Clause 1.
- An accessory shall be subject to the punishment of the crime they assisted in.

SUBCHAPTER 5: TRIAL PROCEDURE

Article-18: Unlawful Command influence
- Unlawful Command Influence is where a member of the military, usually a high ranking officer or Government official, uses or appears to use their authority to influence the goings of military judicial proceedings when they have not obtained any prior approval from a member of the Department of Justice's High Command.
Article-19: Challenges / Objections
- Challenges are motions made (such as motion to dismiss and other motions) that will challenge the ongoings of court.
Article-20: Oaths
- In the beginning stages of a trial, the prosecutor/claimant and defendant/plaintiff must be asked: “Do you swear to tell the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth?” by the seated judge. Both defendant and plaintiff must respond with a statement that is along the lines of: “I swear to tell the truth, the whole truth and nothing but the truth so help me God.”
- Failure to abide by this oath during the duration of the trial will result in perjury charges along with the case being deemed a mistrial.
Article-21: Statute of Limitations
- The statute of limitations is the time limit for filing charges against the defendant.
- Charges that are older than 4 months may not be used during the hearing of a court case.
Article-22: Double Jeopardy
- Double Jeopardy is the illegal punishment of a person twice for the same offence.
- If multiple of the same criminal offenses are committed in two separate instances with different evidence, double jeopardy does not apply, as it is not the same offence.
- Federal Articles may be prosecuted for, even if divisionally punished, without violating Double Jeopardy as they are severe actions that violate the societal fabric of CUSA.
Article-23: Prohibition of Ex Post Facto Punishment
- No person may be judicially punished for an action defined by an article in the CoJ when at the time it was committed, there was no article or article definition that deemed that action as illegal.
Article-24: Pleas of the accused
- The defendant must state whether they plead guilty or not guilty.
- Pleading guilty to an offence means that you have admitted your guilt for that offence. (See Article-32 for more information).
- Pleading not guilty means that you do not agree with the allegations against you and the case will proceed as normal.
Article-25: Court Procedure
- During court there are five separate sections being, Opening Statement followed by the Evidence Statement, Closing Statement, Final Verdict, and Dismissal.
- The Opening Statement consists of a presentation of the defendant’s crimes from the prosecution. The defence team presents a statement to instil doubt into either the seated judge or the jury.
- The Evidence Statement consists of a presentation of witness statement’s or other forms of evidence such as photo proof or audio/video recordings, both sides can partake in this statement.
- Closing Statement consists of a statement provided by the prosecution, mainly covering how their evidence has been shown to implicate guilt. However, the defence is also able to make a statement on how their arguments disprove guilt.
- The verdict is administered at the end of the trial and is the given sentence by the judge if the defendant has plead not guilty, and is proven guilty. If the defendant pleads guilty they are to be provided the punishment listed in the plea agreement. And if the bench finds the defendant not guilty, no punishment is delivered.
- The final stage is Dismissal, it is simply where the punishment is enacted and everyone in the stands is asked to leave the court as the trial has ended.
Article-26a: Objections & Motions
- Objections and Motions are allowed to be given during every statement.
- Objections and motions may be approved or denied depending on the opinion of the seated judge.
- If one of the parties in a case feels as if they were mistreated, they may ask the judge to reconsider by using the Objections and Motions Reversal Act.
- If the OMRA is denied, the party may go to the Supreme Court for a final ruling on it.
Article-26b: Countersuing
- Countersuing defines situations such as, if you were the defendant in a case, and you request a court case against your prosecutor while being sued, it would be considered countersuing, that would be against this article and it would be illegal.
- If the defendant of the initial case is found not guilty of all charges, they may sue the prosecutor referencing this case without violating Countersuit.
Article-27: Refusal to attend Court
- Deliberately missing court hearings is evading justice and is committing contempt of the court. (see article 40).
- Avoiding contact with the prosecution and/or judge purposely throughout a 72 hour timeframe and/or leaving the Discord server or group.

Article-28: Contempt
- Contempt is the offence of being disobedient to or disrespectful to a court of law.
- Committing contempt could lead to legal charges.
Article-29: Depositions
- A deposition is a sworn out-of-court testimony given by a witness that is relevant to a case. Depositions are usually done before court by a representing side's attorney.
- A deposition is required during summary judgements and must be presented to the judge along with the prosecutor's or defendant's statement if there are witnesses involved in the case.
Article-30: Votings and Rulings
- A ruling is a decision made by the judge or jury, normally on the sentence. Rulings can be appealed under some circumstances of which may include, mistrial, bias, and so on.
- A voting is how the jury makes their decision in a group and decides what will go forward to the court.
Article-31: Votes required for conviction, sentencing, and other proceedings
- In a jury, the majority will rule (More than 50%) and their decision will be kept in mind when the judge(s) are making decisions.

Article-32: Plea agreements
- A plea agreement, also known as a plea bargain is an agreement in criminal law proceedings, whereby the prosecutor provides a concession to the defendant in exchange for a plea of guilty or nolo contendere. This concession is often a reduced sentencing. Nolo contendere is a plea by which a defendant in a criminal prosecution accepts conviction but pleads neither guilty or not guilty.
- If the defendant wishes for a plea deal, they will usually be sentenced with lesser punishments once the verdict is reached.
- During a plea deal, no decided punishments between them shall exceed a 2 week ban, 3 weeks rank lock, a blacklist of any sorts, or any other punishment that exceeds the reasoning of their trial.
- A plea agreement may include agreements where additional violations of the Code of Justice will result in an already set punishment. This can also be restricted to only applying to violations related to duties in a specific division. The agreement can still not result in a blacklist.

Article-33: Record of trial
         1. The trials prosecutor, defendant, judge/side judges and attorneys will be recorded alongside the verdict.

Article-34: Time Constraints
1.) A case with both sides’ 3 statements may not elapse beyond a maximum duration of 3 hours.
2.) Any opening or closing statement may not elapse beyond a maximum duration of 15 minutes. Each side’s evidence statement may last up to an hour or 5 minutes of explanation per article for shorter cases (under 10 articles).
3.) Deliberate extension of a case by either side to force the opposite into compromise by time constraint shall be prohibited. -An attorney found with intention to prolong a court for their side’s benefit may be punished appropriately by the American Bar Association. -A case the judge or ruling party allows to elapse beyond the above time constraints will be deemed a mistrial. The Bar Association therefore has the right to investigate the case, and if necessary, punish responsible individuals.
4.) The judge or ruling party has the right to terminate any side’s statements if they exceed the given time limits.
5.) An accepted recess will not count toward the time constraint.

SUBCHAPTER 6 : LOCAL TRIAL ARTICLES

Article-35: Aggravated Assault
- Aggravated Assault is a serious and violent form of assault. The defendant should have had the intent to cause serious bodily harm to another person to be charged with this. Weapons are sometimes included.
Article-36: Assault
- Assault is physically trying to inflict harm, threatening to inflict harm, or otherwise placing an individual in fear of physical harm.
- When oral or verbal, assault is the act of stating a specific physical threat of harm or death to the listener where the listener has a reasonable belief that such threat will be carried out.

- Assaulting a MED or MP member is the same crime as assault, but the assaulted is an enforcement member. Sentences could range from simple punishments to harsher punishments, of which could include arrests or warnings.
- This does not apply to any rank-bought personnel of their respective division.

- Resisting Arrest is a criminal charge against an individual who has committed at least one of the following acts:
a) Fleeing a division that has the power of detaining and punishing an individual via warning or arresting them, for example a MED or MP member is attempting to detain and warn/arrest you and you are fleeing from them.
b) Threatening an enforcement officer with physical violence while being arrested.
c) Physically struggling to free oneself from being restrained.
d) Attacking an enforcement officer while being arrested (assault).

- Carrying a firearm partly concealed eg. down is legal for USM and divisions. It is not permitted for civilians.

- Failure to obey lawful orders from an Enforcement Officer in situations wherein that officer has jurisdiction.
Article-43: Escaping from Lawful Custody
- Escaping from lawful custody means breaking free from an enforcement officer's legal control or from a division with cuff jurisdictions, often after being arrested or detained by law enforcement.

Article-44: False Imprisonment

Article-45: False Information

Some examples could be, falsely informing an Enforcement Division officer/agent, of a false arrest time if they ask you as they may have forgotten the arrest time themselves, of which would cause False Imprisonment. Or providing False Information about an investigation that could possibly make the investigation be delayed.

Article-47: Manslaughter

SUBCHAPTER 7 : FEDERAL TRIAL ARTICLES

- Nepotism is favouritism over friends and family normally for a position/rank, of which is illegal and otherwise known as false obtainment of a position/rank. Other forms of nepotism could include, letting someone in a division without a tryout as they are a family member or friend, picking a friend or family member over someone else, like removing the warning for them because it’s your friend or family member.

Article-67: Political Assassination
- Political Assassination is the planned killing of a politician, or killing prompted by political motives.
- Attempting to ruin the political career of a person via fake evidence, or other unjust means, whether or not it is successful.

Article-68: Conspiracy
- An agreement between two or more persons to commit an offence at some point in the future. It must be agreed upon by the persons involved in order to be considered as conspiracy.
- For example: Conspiring to Murder by plotting or planning to kill an individual.

Article-69: Sabotage
- Sabotage is the act of intentionally disrupting or interfering with someone else's efforts, usually to gain an advantage.

Article-70: Espionage
- Also known as spying, Espionage is the act of obtaining secret or confidential information from undisclosed sources. A person who commits espionage is known as a spy. Government authorised espionage is legal in investigations where needed.

Article-71: Falsification of Documents
- Falsification of Documents is altering messages, documents, evidence, or other alterable material to justify wrongdoing, mislead another to cause an unwarranted action, escape due justice, or place someone in unnecessary danger of injustice.
- If a fabricated document is provided in court, the standing attorney for the person(s) who fabricated the document must provide that information to the judge otherwise it will result in a mistrial and the removal of the attorney.

Article-72 Forgery
- Forgery is the action of copying, or imitating instruments, usually governmental documents, to be used to make people think they are genuine.

Article-73: Discharging a Firearm on Federal Property
- Discharging a Firearm is unlawful, with some exceptions, however firing on federal property is punished further as it puts Federal Agents at risk.

Article-74: Illegal Investigation
- Conducting an investigation is crucial for cases and arrests, among other things. They are to be conducted by the primary investigative team in CUSA, The Department of Homeland Security. Only HLS, Ethics and NCIS can conduct investigations. Any investigation taken by a division outside of Homeland Security, Ethics or the NCIS is considered unlawful. In order for an investigation to be considered legal, it must be approved by the HLS Chief of Staff, and/or the Secretary of Homeland Security, and the President of The United States of America. Investigations without probable and justifiable cause is also illegal.
- Governmental investigations must be approved with the Secretary of Homeland Security’s, the President of The United States’ explicit permission or the Congress Ethics.
- Divisions are authorised to proceed with internal investigations if they have substantial reasoning and proof. It must be approved by their HI-COM, or division head, the President of The United States, the Attorney General, or the Department of Homeland Security Head.

Article-75: Abuse of Power
- Using rank or authority, or threatening the use of such authority to influence people in unlawful ways. For example, demoting someone for simply not liking them.

Article-76: Abuse of Position
- Using the power of one’s position you have been trusted with, in order to benefit one’s self.

Article-77: Political Corruption
- Using political power to influence the ongoings of the world to benefit yourself, not the majority of the population.

Article-78: Illegal Removal by the President
- Removing Congress or personnel with the cabinet rank without proper reason and justifying it is illegal. Taking away Congress or Cabinet and replacing them with friends/family is also Nepotism, and can lead to impeachment.

Article-79: Treason
- Treason is the act of attacking a state authority or one’s own country that they owe allegiance to. This can also be summarised as betraying one’s own country, usually for the benefit of an enemy. In CUSA, betraying the community of CUSA by selling or leaking classified information to potential enemies, can be considered Treason.

Article-80: Conspiracy to Commit Acts of Terrorism
- Conspiring, an act of two or more parties agreeing to commit an unlawful act, but in this instance, it is to inflict terror. Raiding the base with the intent to cause fear and spread hate is an example.

Article-81: Bribery
- The act of offering, giving, receiving, or soliciting anything of value to corrupt a person or influence a person in unlawful ways. This can be anything such as ranks, money, or power.

Article-82: Perjury
- The intentional act of swearing a false oath or falsifying an affirmation to tell the truth in writing or spoken matters concerning a proceeding. This is also known as lying under oath.

Article-83: Group Admin Abuse
- Group Admin Abuse is using permissions in groups to alter anything without good intent or permission. For example: Timmy was a Captain in Marines, he is bad and his division leader demotes him. Bob then changes all of the members ranks that he could out of rage. This is an example of GAA.
- The head of a division/department may appoint their assistant head/co-leader of their division/department without any prior approval and if they do so, are protected under law for that one action. (They are not given immunity, it’s that they cannot be sued for the appointment/demotion of their assistant head)

Article-84: Game Admin Abuse
- Game Admin Abuse is the wrongful use of commands/permissions for unlawful acts. For example, Stuart uses the ;m command to get all the citizens to join a raid. This is illegal.
- This action should be handled with staff, this will only appear in a case if it’s accompanied by another article.
- An individual may not be prosecuted for this article if the conducted rank alter is reasonable and/or has evidence to back it up.

Article-85: Discord Admin Abuse
- Using the power trusted in you in a discord server for wrongdoings such as mass-banning people' for no apparent reason is an instance of DAA.

Article-86: Impeachment
- Impeachment is the process by which a legislative board charges a public officer and removes them of their role.
- If someone who is able to be impeached is being sued, it is not counted as impeachment unless the prosecution specifically adds Article-86 to their list.
- Only the House of Representatives may bring up charges of impeachment.

Article-87: Incompetency
- Incompetence is the inability to do things successfully, responsibly and sensibly.

Article-88: Insubordination
- Insubordination is the act of disobeying your superiors lawful commands with purpose.
- This article does not apply if the command is unethical, unlawful, or against divisional or departmental rules.

Article-89: False Accusations
- Accusing someone of things they have not done or unsupported by facts.
- Can be paired with Article-42.

Article-90: Trespassing on Federal Property
- Definition of Federal Property- Private Property that is under the use and ownership of the United States Government and affiliated departments. These include “The Government Building”, “Homeland Security Headquarters” and “The Courthouse”.
- An individual is not allowed to enter the priorly mentioned places unless a specific event is occurring that allows the general population to enter.
- Definition of Trespassing- Trespassing is under CUSA law, meaning it is a misdemeanour in most cases, however when one trespasses on a Federal Property it becomes a criminal offence. Trespassing is the act of deliberately and illegally entering private property without the expressed permission of the property owner and/or operator or a personnel superior.
- The entering of Federal Property without permission is illegal and will be prosecuted. Permission can be given under few circumstances such as “whitelisting” where permission is pre given by the Federal Government e.g. The President. Other circumstances include invitation by a person holding the power to allow that such as The President or Vice President along with other high ranking government officials. Another circumstance would be to attend a special event. (ex. A court trial)
- Enforcement Officers and divisional personnel stationed on the premises may use handcuffs to remove trespassers from the premises of Federal Property without warning.
- Enforcement Officers can’t arrest someone that trespassed in the HLS Headquarter.

Article-91: Coercion
      1.    Coercion involves compelling a party to act in an involuntary manner by the use of threats, including threats to use force against someone.
It involves a set of forceful actions which violate the free will of an individual in order to induce a desired response. These actions may include extortion or blackmail. Example: Threatening someone to declassify divisional documents under penalty of being demoted.

Article-92: Extortion
- Any person who unlawfully communicates threats to another person or uses actual force with the intention thereby to obtain anything of value or any acquittance, or advantage is guilty of extortion. Extortion can be a result of blackmail.
- For example, taking someone hostage and demanding money. Another example: The commanding officer of a division resorted to extortion,  unlawfully threatening a lower-ranking individual with demotion unless they handed over the desired item or information.
Article-93: Blackmail
- The action, treated as a criminal offence, of demanding payment or another benefit from someone in return for not revealing compromising or damaging information about them, or taking some form of threatening action to oneself.
Article-94 Impersonation
-  Impersonation is the act of imitating persons and pretending to be them for unlawful purposes, to benefit themselves, or to influence another into taking action that they would not have taken if there was no impersonation.
Article 95: Entrapment
        1. Luring an individual who was not otherwise predisposed into committing a crime in order for this person to be punished. For example, a divisional member telling someone to step inside their base then arresting them for trespassing. Telling someone to commit an unlawful act with the intent of reporting or prosecuting the individual for that offense would also invoke this article.
Article 96: Misuse of Classified Information
        1.  Leaking classified information that you have been entrusted with, by sharing it to individuals who have no access to it/are not supposed to have or allowed to have access to it.
Article 97: Deprivation of Rights Under Color of Law
        1.  People act Under Color of Law when they wield powers invested in them by divisions or departments. Such as MED personnel on patrol duty, or DoJ judges presiding over a case.
Willfully depriving a person of a right or privilege protected by the
Constitution or the Code of Justice while acting Under Color of Law is strictly prohibited by this article.
Article 98: Witness Tampering
        1.  The act of pressuring, threatening, or inciting fear in a witness to force them to refrain from testifying or speaking about an act that they have witnessed, to force them into testifying untruthfully, or to change the outcome of the case.

Article-99: Malicious Prosecution
- The act of initiating a case or using the influence of a case against someone with no evidence or completely irrelevant evidence to the litigant's knowledge, or to warrant threats, harassment, damages, or unjust leverage while there is no proper reason for a lawsuit.

Article-100: Assaulting a Government Official
- Assaulting a Government Official is an act of assaulting or harming a government official. This is similar to assault and aggravated assault except that it is done towards a government official.

Article-101: Contempt of Congress
- Contempt of Congress is the disobedience or interference to lawful Congressional orders or mechanisms. This can include but is not limited to the refusal to comply with subpoenas, the disruption of Congressional proceedings or the interference with witnesses, documents or evidence relevant to Congressional proceedings or inquiries.
- Upon referral by the President of the Senate or the Speaker of the House, the Attorney General must ensure that violations are prosecuted in the name of the United States through the US Attorney’s Office.

CONGRESSIONAL LAW PROPOSALS
H.R. 1208 "Countersuit Prevention Act"

Section I: Short Title

-
- This bill shall be titled the “Countersuit Prevention Act”, it may be cited as such during formal reference

- It may be referred to by code, or common acronym ‘CPA’

- The formal acronym for ‘Code of Justice’ is recognised as ‘CoJ’

Section II. Contents

-
- ‘Countersuit’ will be added to the CoJ under Subchapter 6: Trial Procedure as an article of definition.

- This article will specify how community members being sued at one given period of time may not request a court case against their prosecutor during this time.

- They will be required to attend the initial case as a defendant and wait till conclusion until they may countersue the prosecutor in a separate case.

- A request for a second, separate case against the prosecutor must not be accepted if it includes any reference, link, or evidence to a previous case.

- If the defendant of the initial case is found not guilty of all charges, they may sue the prosecutor referencing this case without violating Countersuit.

- Subarticle 2.2.2 is not applicable when a prosecutor who violates an article mid-trial, (e.g. perjury by lying under oath) and the defendant may countersue for this offense.

- A member with immunity may retain it if they are currently suing the individual attempting to remove their immunity (via suing them)

Section III. Reasoning

-
- To limit cases of ‘False Accusations’ and ‘Defamation of Character’ where an individual attempts to avenge a former defendant for a previous lawsuit.

- To prevent immune officials from being unable to carry out their duties for the duration of 2 active cases

- To support organisation of cases and requests as countersuits are often cluttered & difficult to manage

- In previous instances, attempting prosecutors have decided to drop their charges to fearing the chance of being countersued with the same evidence used


Section IV. Waivers

-
- This bill may be waived by the Ownership Team in rare exceptions.

- This bill may be waived by the standing Attorney General if they deem the severity of the case, and given evidence, too much to withhold the case any longer than necessary.

- Countersuit may be waived if the case involves (with valid reason) Article 86 – Impeachment

## H.R. 1707 "Court Statement Limitation Act"

Source: https://docs.google.com/document/d/17XVXPHHtR_-YBP3svXLfMBfAp0HpJEF9g5CMAK4f13k/edit

CONGRESSIONAL LAW PROPOSALS
H.R. 1707 "Court Statement Limitation Act"

Section I: Short Title

-
- This bill shall be titled the “Court Statement Limitation Act”, it may be cited as such during formal reference

- It may be referred to by code, or common acronym ‘CSLA’

Section II. Contents

-
- The below terms shall apply to all cases conducted in a court of law by the Department of Justice or its licensed representatives

- A case, with both sides’ 3 statements may not elapse beyond a maximum duration of 3 hours.

- Any opening or closing statement may not elapse beyond a maximum duration of 15 minutes.

- Each side’s evidence statement may last up to an hour, or 5 minutes of explanation per article for shorter cases (under 10 articles)

- Deliberate extension of a case by either side of a suit to force the opposite into compromise by time constraint shall be prohibited.

- An attorney found with intention to prolong a court for their side’s benefit may be punished appropriately by the American Bar Association.

- A case the judge or ruling party allows to elapse beyond the above time constraints will be deemed a mistrial. The Bar Association therefore has the right to investigate the case, and if necessary, punish responsible individuals.

- The judge or ruling party has the right to terminate any side’s statements if they exceed the given time limits.

- An accepted recess will not count toward the time constraint.

- ‘Time Constraints’ will be added to the Code of Justice under Subchapter 5: Trial Procedure as an article of definition.

- The article will specify the above terms and restraints.

Section III. Reasoning

-
- To limit the intentional prolonging of statements to force the opposite side into compromise or mistrial by repeated procedural delay – each side may not have the time in their schedule to attend rescheduled cases.

- To control the duration of a statement, a feature that has previously been exploited by members for case postponement.

- To promote efficiency in the legal system and ensure timely justice, cases are resolved promptly without being needlessly extended.


Section IV. Waivers

-
- This bill may be waived by the Group Manager, Founder of CUSA, or Ownership Team in rare exceptions

- A majority Supreme Court vote may waive the limitations or Attorney General decision on specific instances

TITLE I—GENERAL PROVISIONS
SEC. 101. DEFINITIONS.
- Branches are defined as the following:
- “Executive Branch” is defined as the Executive Office of the President of the United States and White House.
- “Supreme Court of the United States” is defined as the Chief Justice, Deputy Chief Justice(s), and Associate Justice(s).
-  “Department of Justice High Command” is defined as the Deputy Secretary of Justice, Secretary of Justice, and Attorney General of the United States.
-  “United States Congress” is defined as the Deputy President Pro Tempore, President Pro Tempore, Speaker Pro Tempore, Speaker of the United States House of Representatives, Senate & House Minority & Majority Leader & Whip, Representatives, and Senators.
- “Government meeting” refers to a monthly meeting between the branches defined in SEC. 101. DEFINITIONS. (a).
-  “White House Office of Governmental Affairs” is defined as the organiser(s) and presider(s) of the government meeting.
SEC. 102. KEY CONCEPT.
- Accordingly, governmental meetings will be held on the first Saturday or Sunday of each month rather than weekly.

Subtitle A—Preparation
SEC. 111. ATTENDANCE.
- A minimum of one (1) member from each branch must be present in the government meeting.
- All members of the Judicial Branch, Legislative Branch, and Executive Branch, are expected to attend government meetings when they are available.
- Attendance & times must be determined one (1) week prior to the meeting unless replacements are found.
SEC. 112. CANCELLATION.
- A monthly meeting may only be cancelled with valid reasoning presented and a ⅔ majority vote from all parties involved set to attend.
SEC. 113. FAILURE TO ATTEND.
- If any of the aforementioned parties are unable to attend, they must inform the White House Office of Governmental Affairs prior to 24 hours until the scheduled meeting.
- In this instance, a replacement for the absentee must be found. If no one from a branch is able to attend, the meeting will be rescheduled.
- An exemption to informing prior to 24 hours is for last minute emergencies that justify failure to attend. The meeting will go on without them if this occurs.

Subtitle B—Meeting
SEC. 121. LOCATION.
- The meeting must be held in the HQ meeting room on any server. Relevant divisions, sub divisions, units, and/or any authorised organisation(s) may be deployed to guard.
SEC. 122. DISCUSSION.
- In the meeting, the following topics must be discussed:
- Branch performances.
- Executive Branch Proposals & Focuses.
- Legislative Branch Proposals & Focuses.
- Judicial Branch Proposals & Focuses.
- Supreme Court Actions & Plannings.
- Updates on the Department of Justice.
- Legislative Agendas.
- Branch Collaboration.
- Subsection (a) and the subparagraphs (a) contained may be waived by the President of the United States only if other important matters related to the three (3) government branches are required to be discussed.
- If the President is unable to attend, each branch's respective High Command personnel will be able to decide on important matters they need to discuss and if they want to waive the subparagraphs above that are associated with their branch.
- In the case that the High Command members of the three (3) government branches need to waive the subparagraphs listed in subsection (a), more than ½ of those High Command members must agree to do so. One member of High Command may not solely have the power to do so.

Subtitle C—Conclusion
SEC. 131. RECORD.
- Within 48 hours of the conclusion of the meeting, the White House must publish an Electronic Version of the Meeting Transcript & Records to the public.
- Any confidential information must be removed in the document prior to publication while an unedited version may be released for the branches.

Subtitle D—Waivers
SEC. 141. WAIVERS.
- The Revised Government Meeting Act may be waived if granted permission by the Ownership Team.
- The government meeting may be cancelled if CUSA is in an active state of war.
