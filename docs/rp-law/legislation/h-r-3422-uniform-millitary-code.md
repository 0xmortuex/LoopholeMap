A BILL
To consolidate military access, pursuit, and trespassing regulations into a single enforceable Uniform Military Code, ensuring clarity, due process, and consistency with executive directives and in-game regulations.
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,
SECTION. 1. DEFINITIONS.
- In this Act Federal property refers to the HQ, Bunker, radio tower, and the Court House.
- In this Act divisional property refers to the property / base in which a division operates. This includes the HQ for the Secret Service and the Court House for the DoJ.
- In this Act a suspect refers to any individual whose actions demand for an arrest or warning in accordance with CUSA’s set arresting guidelines.
- In this Act Crimson Raiders refers to any individual on the Crimson Raiders team.
- In this Act a Civilian is defined as any individual on the Citizens team.
SECTION. 2. APPLICABILITY.
- The provisions of this Act apply to all persons who enter, occupy or otherwise remain within the boundaries of the Fort Tedder military base.
- The only exception to this Act shall be the Department of Homeland Security and its on-duty agents while being on the Fort Tedder military base.
SECTION. 3. ENFORCEMENT.
- The Military Police and the Military Education Department shall have the authority to enforce the provisions of this act while on-duty.
- Violations of this Act may be prosecuted in the US courts if applicable and not otherwise punished.
- Divisions shall have the authority to enforce the provisions of this Act on their respective properties, if applicable.
- Divisional leadership must ensure that their personnel adheres to the provisions of this Act.

TITLE I—GENERAL PROVISIONS

Subtitle A—Base Access Regulations
SEC. 111. CIVILIAN ENTRANCE.
- Civilians may enter Fort grounds by:
- Entering through the base access door, while owning the base access gamepass.
- Entering through the Military Police Checkpoint, by being processed by the Military Police.
- MP personnel must ensure weapons are removed from visitors entering through the checkpoint.
SEC. 112. CIVILIAN RIGHTS.
- Civilians may not be deprived of their rights granted through the Constitution or other laws while being on military property.
- Civilians are only compelled to answer questions about identification and proof of access.
- Any arrest resulting from non-compliance is at the blame of the Civilian as long as proper arresting procedures were followed.
SEC. 113. CIVILIAN RESTRICTIONS.
- Civilians and divisional guests, with the exception to whitelisted individuals, may not enter any of the training grounds and may be arrested for trespassing if they enter:
- Parade Square.
- Shooting Range.
- Obstacle Course.
- Hangar.
- Classroom.
- Divisional training pads / grounds.
- Civilians must be warned once before being arrested for any offense in this subtitle, with the exceptions to violations involving the discharging of firearms.
- Civilians are prohibited from openly carrying or discharging a firearm on military grounds.
- Civilians with red nametags may be eliminated on sight by any military personnel.
- Civilians raiding, shooting or openly carrying a firearm inside the base, while not being part of the Crimson Raiders group or owning any of the CUSA raiding gamepasses may be arrested by federal agents, members of the Military Education Department or members of the Military Police depending on jurisdiction.

Subtitle B—Pursuit and Trespass Regulations
SEC. 121. TRESPASSING.
- Divisional leadership and oversight have discretion on who is or is not allowed on their property.
- For the HQ this is up to the leadership of the federal government.
- All divisions and departments have a legal jurisdiction to apprehend or arrest trespassers to their property.
SEC. 122. PURSUIT AUTHORITY.
- Any pursuer may, while in pursuit of a suspect which has violated a law or regulation enforced in the military base, may enter non-federal divisional properties to pursue and detain the suspect.
- Any divisional officer pursuing a suspect may enter federal property during their pursuit.
- Pursuit must be halted and pursuers may not enter a property, if the property is being guarded by active, authorized personnel which has the tools to detain the suspect until the suspect leaves the property.
- It is illegal to refuse to hand a suspect to the original pursuer.
- Personnel guarding the divisional property must, to the best of their abilities attempt to, apprehend the suspect.
- Members of the Military Education Department and Military Police are not affected by this.
- A pursuer must leave any entered divisional property immediately after apprehending the suspect.
- Any following arrest or measure must be taken after leaving the property.
SEC. 123. REASONABLE DOUBT EXCEPTION
If there’s reasonable doubt that personnel guarding a property will not succeed to apprehend the pursued suspect, the pursuer may enter the property to apprehend the suspect themselves or to assist in the apprehension.

Subtitle C—Gate Regulations
SEC. 131. GATE ACCESS.
- The Military Police has complete authority and jurisdiction at the Military Gate.
- The Military Police has no jurisdiction over federal agents or any government personnel on the premises of the Military Gate.
- Military Police personnel may restrict access to the Military Gate.
- Any USM personnel not meeting the requirements listed at the Military Gate are not allowed to enter the premises of the Military Gate.
- Violations are considered as trespassing and may be arrested by the Military Police for trespassing.
- Listed requirements refers to any line or sign at the Military Gate premises which gives a description of requirements to enter or cross an area.
- Military Police High Command is allowed on Gate premises regardless, even while off duty.
- Military Police High Command may whitelist or authorize individuals to enter Gate premises.
- Civilians are not allowed to return to the guarded premises of the Military Gate upon entering the Military Base.
- Violations are considered as trespassing and may be arrested by the Military Police for trespassing.
SEC. 132. DIVISIONAL PATROL.
- Any party of 3 or more divisional members led by an Officer (O1+) may be allowed outside the base to patrol; the Military Police may revoke their authority to do so if deemed interfering with their divisional duties
- Further internal divisional regulations to this may be determined by divisional leadership or oversight as they see fit.
SEC. 133. SAFETY REGULATIONS.
- Openly carrying a firearm, whether Civilian or Raider, on the premises of the Military Gate is illegal.
- Violators may be shot on sight by Military personnel.
- Military Police personnel may arrest violators as long as they are not part of the Crimson Raiders or not in the possession of any of the CUSA raiding gamepasses.
- Military Police personnel may order the vacancy of Gate premises for safety reasons.
SEC. 134. PURSUIT.
Military Police personnel may pursue suspects who committed an offense at the Military Gate in accordance with the regulations set forth in Subtitle B and may also leave the fort grounds to continue their pursuit of the suspect.

Subtitle D—Raiding Guidelines
SEC. 141. RAID AUTHORIZATION.
- Anyone on the Crimson Raiders team may raid.
- Raiding, rioting, or other usually unlawful activities, committed by Crimson Raiders, may not be prosecuted, arrested or otherwise held accountable unless it is allowed through this Subtitle or in violation of any provisions of this Subtitle.
SEC. 142. KILL ON SIGHT.
- Crimson Raiders are kill-on-sight targets.
- Members of military divisions must actively engage and battle Crimson Raiders on-sight.
SEC. 143. TRESPASSING.
- Crimson Raiders are immune from any trespassing violations during a raid in compliance with section 141 and section 143 of this Act.
- Crimson Raiders may not enter any buildings inside the military fort.
- Entering a building inside the military fort is considered as trespassing for Crimson Raiders.
- The exact nature of the trespass is equivalent to the usual nature of trespass on the property of the building.
SEC. 144. RAIDERS PROTECTIONS.
No individual may spawn camp on the Crimson Raiders spawn.
SEC. 145. EVENT RESTRICTIONS.
- Crimson Raiders may not disturb events.
- MED is authorized to detain any Crimson Raider deliberately disturbing an event to issue a warning.
- Persistent disturbance after being warned twice may lead to an arrest.

TITLE II—LEGAL JURISDICTION OF THE MILITARY

Subtitle A—Law Enforcement
SEC. 211. LEGAL JURISDICTION.
- The Military Education Department and its agents as well as the Military Police and its agents are considered as military law enforcement and may enforce laws and other military guidelines on military property.
- Military law enforcement may only enforce the laws on military property unless otherwise provided by law.
- Military regulations directed by the Department of Defense leadership must be publicly accessible to be enforced by the military law enforcement. Such regulations may be enforced by military law enforcement on all on-duty military personnel unless otherwise provided by law.
- Department of Defense leadership may impose restrictions to the jurisdiction of military law enforcement.
SEC. 212. RESTRICTIONS.
Law enforcement may not arrest or detain on-duty government personnel and has no jurisdiction over them.
SEC. 213. INTERFERENCE.
- Military law enforcement may not be obstructed or interfered with in the line of their duty except by higher ranked officers from their own chain of command.
- Obstructing military law enforcement may be seen as delaying activities of enforcement officers or refusing orders of enforcement officers.
- USM personnel are compelled to comply with military law enforcement or may be arrested or detained for non-compliance.
- Civilians under the jurisdiction of military law enforcement are compelled to comply with military law enforcement or may be arrested or detained for non-compliance.
- Civilians are under the jurisdiction of military law enforcement when they’re on any military property.
SEC. 214. ARRESTING PROCEDURES.
Military law enforcement may detain and arrest violators of the law or military guidelines in accordance with the arresting procedures that may be drafted and outlined by the Supreme Court.

Subtitle B—Defense Operations and Security
SEC. 221. AUTHORITY TO USE LETHAL FORCE.
- Any Civilian on military property, with a red nametag may be shot on sight by military personnel.
- Military personnel may use lethal force to defend military property or to prevent unauthorized access to the fort.
- Lethal force may include the firing of any firearm, the use of batons, or the use of tasers.
SEC. 222. TRESPASSING.
Trespassers to military property may be subject to lethal force or detainment and arrest.
