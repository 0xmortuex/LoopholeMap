A BILL
To effectuate in law and declare all acts of discrimination on the basis of personal features illegal.
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,

TITLE I—GENERAL PROVISIONS
SEC. 101. DEFINITIONS.
- ‘Personal features’ or ‘Features’ shall refer to an individual's gender, identified gender, religion, sexual orientation, race, nationality, physical appearance, disabilities, and remaining protected articles outlined in Section 102.
- ‘Provisions’ shall refer to the contents of this Act regarding discrimination types, definition of discrimination and applicability of discrimination.
SEC. 102. DISCRIMINATION TYPES.
- Discrimination types officially prohibited by law are as follows:
- Discrimination on the basis of gender or identified gender.
- Discrimination on the basis of religion.
- Discrimination on the basis of sexual orientation.
- Discrimination on the basis of race, ethnicity or nationality.
- Discrimination on the basis of physical appearance or disability.
- Discrimination on the basis of familial situation, employment, income or social class.
- Discrimination on the basis of political view.
- This does not apply to political decisions, and appointments as they heavily depend on the individual’s political views, plans, and more.
SEC. 103. DEFINITION OF DISCRIMINATION.
- Discrimination is the act of directly and intentionally excluding, performing unlawful actions on, or intentionally engaging in derogatory, defamatory or violent acts against an individual solely or primarily based on their traits (defined in section 102).
- Discrimination applies only when the individual targeted has suffered tangible or measurable harm from the event. This includes, but is not limited to, being handed stricter punishments (in comparison to others for similar offences) by a member of High Command due to your traits.
- Acts of discrimination must be proven with sufficient evidence.
- Individuals must be proved to have acted in a way in which the nature has contradicted provisions in Section 102 and Section 103.
- Behavior, where an individual directly harasses, in any form, insults (related to aforementioned classifications), noticeably decreases quality of respect or treatment of an individual solely on the basis of their features, is not tolerated, and breaches outlined provisions within this Act.
- Individuals may not receive extra benefits over others due to their features, over others tangibly and measurably proven more capable or competent (this applies to cases such as appointments to a senior rank, where an internal promotion would have sufficed).
- Individuals may not receive differentiated treatment solely due to their traits in section 102.
SEC. 104. ENFORCEMENT.
- Violations may be reported to divisional leadership. Leadership must take such reports seriously and failure to handle and apply internal punishments may be prosecuted or punished.
- If a violation is already being prosecuted in a court of law or if a case concerning the violation has already adjourned, divisional leadership may not handle or apply any punishment to the reported violation unless it is an order of the court.
- If a person wishes to pursue a divisional report against a violation they must waive from their right to prosecute the violation before divisional leadership applies any punishment to the accused individual. This does not apply if leadership decides to leave it un-punished or deems the reported action to not be a violation of this Act.
- The ruling of any court takes priority over the decision of leadership and may result in rescission or restitution.
- Leadership will not be held liable if they use their best judgement and deem a report to not be in violation of this Act, even if the court later rules the reported action to be discriminatory, as long as leadership has taken the report seriously and have properly reviewed it. Leadership shall only be held liable if there’s a clear refusal or failure to look into the report and concern themselves with it.
- Violations of this Act may only be punished in accordance with the standards set in the code of justice.
