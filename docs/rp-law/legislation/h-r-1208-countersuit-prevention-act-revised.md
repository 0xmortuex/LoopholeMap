CONGRESSIONAL LAW PROPOSALS
H.R. 1208 "Countersuit Prevention Act"

Section I: Short Title

-
- This bill shall be titled the “Countersuit Prevention Act”, it may be cited as such during formal reference

- It may be referred to by code, or common acronym ‘CPA’

- The formal acronym for ‘Code of Justice’ is recognised as ‘CoJ’

Section II. Contents

-
- ‘Countersuit’ will be added to the CoJ under Subchapter 6: Trial Procedure as an article of definition.

- This article will specify how community members being sued at one given period of time may not request a court case against their prosecutor during this time.

- They will be required to attend the initial case as a defendant and wait till conclusion until they may countersue the prosecutor in a separate case.

- A request for a second, separate case against the prosecutor must not be accepted if it includes any reference, link, or evidence to a previous case.

- If the defendant of the initial case is found not guilty of all charges, they may sue the prosecutor referencing this case without violating Countersuit.

- Subarticle 2.2.2 is not applicable when a prosecutor who violates an article mid-trial, (e.g. perjury by lying under oath) and the defendant may countersue for this offense.

- A member with immunity may retain it if they are currently suing the individual attempting to remove their immunity (via suing them)

Section III. Reasoning

-
- To limit cases of ‘False Accusations’ and ‘Defamation of Character’ where an individual attempts to avenge a former defendant for a previous lawsuit.

- To prevent immune officials from being unable to carry out their duties for the duration of 2 active cases

- To support organisation of cases and requests as countersuits are often cluttered & difficult to manage

- In previous instances, attempting prosecutors have decided to drop their charges to fearing the chance of being countersued with the same evidence used


Section IV. Waivers

-
- This bill may be waived by the Ownership Team in rare exceptions.

- This bill may be waived by the standing Attorney General if they deem the severity of the case, and given evidence, too much to withhold the case any longer than necessary.

- Countersuit may be waived if the case involves (with valid reason) Article 86 – Impeachment
