Code of Justice
Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,
ARTICLE 1. DEFINITIONS.
- Law Enforcement.—The Military Police and the Military Education Department are considered law enforcement.
- Federal Agent.—Agent of the Naval Criminal Investigative Service, Secret Service or Homeland Security.
- Government Agent.—Any person acting under official government authority, including law enforcement, federal agents, individuals acting under lawful authority of government officials/entities, high ranked military officials and government officials.
- Ruling party.—The individual or group that has authority to deliberate over conviction or acquittal of charges.
ARTICLE 2. JUDICIAL SENTENCING.
- No judge may be influenced or look for consultation during a case, except if the influence or consult originates from a superior court.
- Any influence or consultation must relate directly to the case that the judge is presiding over and may be used solely to advise or assist the judge in determining a verdict with their own independent judgment.
- Judges may issue sentences and other orders, following the listed conditions and requirements in this code, upon determining whether an accused is guilty or not per trial proceedings.
- Judges may order rescission or restitution following this code after adjudication.
- Judges may order the rescission of contracts or agreements, as part of their sentencing or order, at the conclusion of a trial, that included fraud, coercion or other illegal means as a measure to restore balance, order, and justice.
- Judges may grant specific performance following this code after adjudication.
- Judges may grant specific performance, as part of their sentencing or order, at the conclusion of a trial, to restore a state or situation which was lost or disturbed through illegal actions as a measure to restore balance, order, and justice.
- Exile, demotion, and suspension sentences may not result in the removal from a government position.
- Being convicted in court for violations of this code does not hinder any impeachment from taking place for the offense, as impeachments are a question of whether an official should remain in office and not whether an official should be punished.
- A guilty conviction from this code may be used as a reason to pursue articles of impeachment.
- There may be no sentencing that could remove or cause the removal of a government official from their position.
- A court sentence may be appealed to a court higher than the one that issued it.
- The higher court may decide to change the sentence following the listed conditions and requirements and other lawful appeal proceedings.
- This may not change the initial verdict unless the verdict itself is being appealed.
- The result of a sentencing appeal may only replace the initial sentence with a lighter sentence or affirm the sentence issued by the lower court.
- No sentencing or its summation may restrict an individual for more than 5 days on discord.
ARTICLE 3. COUNTS.
- People subject to this code may be prosecuted or charged with multiple counts of offenses.
- This means that multiple offenses would not result in just 1 charge of the Article but may result in multiple charges.
- Each count is to be considered separately and is to be differentiated from one another throughout a trial and its proceedings.
- The ruling party, whether jury or judge, must consider and evaluate each count separately, including the sentencing of each count.
ARTICLE 4. STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS.
- Violations of this code may only be prosecuted for a period of 3 months after the initial commitment of the crime.
- Charges outside of the statute of limitations may not be added to any court trial.
- Violations may still be prosecuted if the case was initiated before the statute of limitations expired.
ARTICLE 5. PLEAS.
- The accused in a trial may plead guilty, not guilty, or nolo contendere.
- Guilty.—The accused pleads guilty, meaning they will not be tried any further for the accusation and will receive a guilty sentence for it by the presiding judge. Any guilty sentence through a guilty plea is still subject to regular appeals. This plea can be withdrawn if it can be proven that at the moment of the plea—
- the accused was not properly informed of their rights or the charges,
- the accused was not in the appropriate mental state to make the decision,
- the accused was illegally coerced into pleading guilty; or
- a request or motion to withdraw the plea was made before any guilty sentence was issued or executed.
- Not guilty.—The accused pleads not guilty, meaning the proceedings will continue, and the accused may decide to change their plea until the trial reaches a conclusion.
- Nolo contendere.—This means the accused does not contest the charges, meaning the case immediately moves to the sentencing. This plea can be withdrawn if it can be proven that at the moment of the plea—
- the accused was not properly informed of their rights or the charges,
- the accused was not in the appropriate mental state to make the decision,
- the accused was illegally coerced into the plea; or
- a request or motion to withdraw the plea was made before any sentencing or punishment to it, was issued, or executed.
- Parties of a trial may negotiate plea deals, in which the accused agrees to a plea for a concession.
- All plea deals and their terms must be approved by the presiding judge, both sides of the trial, and must comply with this code.
- The judge must ensure that the terms are fair and must verbally warn in case he notices unfair or unjust terms and may deny the proposed bargain.
- An approved plea deal is treated as an official and valid contract.
ARTICLE 6. SEVERABILITY.
If any title, subtitle, article, or clause of this code is struck down for being unconstitutional or any other reason, the rest of this code shall remain unaffected and continue to be enforced and followed.
ARTICLE 7. APPREHENSION.
- Law Enforcement and Federal Agents may apprehend and arrest individuals that they believe to have violated articles of this code.
- Apprehension pursuant to this article is not subject to the burden of proof and evidence.
- Departments and divisions retain the right to apprehend anyone on their respective property or any other property they’ve been granted jurisdiction over, to apprehend violators of the law or other property rules and may directly hand them over to law enforcement.
- This article can not be used or enforced for violations which are being prosecuted in court.
- Valid reasons for apprehension are doing a lawful arrest, issuing a warning or following divisional procedure such as checking the individual's profile. It is explicitly illegal to detain in ways that cause harm, such as waiting for others to aim their guns before releasing the detainee.
- All Citizens shall be informed of the reason for their arrest and the length of their arrest before being arrested.
ARTICLE 8. RESTRAINT OF CHARGED CITIZENS.
- The ruling party of a case may order the temporary suspension of an accused, from all duties.
- There may be no permanent replacement made during the suspension or as a result of the suspension.
- There must be compelling evidence of serious accusations related to the duties that the accused is getting suspended from.
- A higher court may overrule the decision of the ruling party.
ARTICLE 9. ENTRAPMENT.
- Entrapment shall constitute the acquittal from charges of a person accused of a crime. The burden to prove entrapment lies with the defendant.
- Entrapment is when a government agent induces the commitment of a crime, through luring or persuasion. It must be indubitable that the crime would not have been committed if it had not been for the involvement of a government agent.
- The affected individual must not have had any predisposition to commit the crime. Entrapment does not apply if the defendant was ready to commit the crime prior to any involvement of a government agent.
- Private citizens can not cause entrapment unless they act under governmental directives, authority, or control.
ARTICLE 10. RIGHTS OF THE CITIZENS.
- All Citizens shall have the rights to—
- Be informed of the reason and evidence of any punishment issued to them, regardless of classification,
- Freely express themselves within legal boundaries of law,
- Freely choose and exercise their religion unless otherwise provided by law,
- Request declassification of documents,
- Think freely and express their thoughts under the freedom of speech,
- Refuse following unethical or unlawful commands,
- Be protected from self-incrimination by invoking their right to remain silent.
- Any action to exercise these rights is protected by law.
ARTICLE 11. EX-POST FACTO CRIMINALIZATION.
- No person shall be subject to a punishment or criminal liability for an act that was not defined as an offense or crime at the time it was committed.
- No person shall be subject to a penalty that was not defined at the time the underlying offense for the penalty was committed.
- No penalty may be retroactively increased in severity, duration, or type unless it is proven that a more severe offense was committed than the one that the penalty was first issued for.
- Criminal law and any rule that may result in punishment or criminal liability must be accessible to those subject to it for a penalty to be imposed.
- No accused shall be put into a worse position through retroactive legislation, retroactive interpretation, or retroactive administration.

TITLE I—LOCAL ARTICLES
ARTICLE 1-0-1. JURISDICTION.
- Violations of this Title may be prosecuted in a local court, presided by a local judge or a judge from a higher court.
- Cases involving only articles from this title are classed as local trials.
- Any licensed attorney may act as a legal counsel in local trials.
ARTICLE 1-0-2. NUISANCE.
- Consistent irritation, inconvenience, or disturbance of other people is considered a nuisance. It gets in the way of comfort, tranquility, or the ability to go about daily tasks without getting annoyed.
- Punishable by up to a 1-day game ban, up to a 1-rank demotion or up to a 1-day discord mute.
ARTICLE 1-0-3. DISTURBING THE PEACE.
- An act of loud, violent, or disruptive behavior that disturbs the peace and disrupts the normal calm of a community is known as disturbing the peace. This is regarded as a criminal offense since it jeopardizes public safety and order and may involve fighting, loud parties, or other disruptive behaviors.
- Punishable by up to a 3-day ban, up to a 1-rank demotion or up to a 1-day discord mute.
ARTICLE 1-0-4. HARASSMENT.
- The persistent and targeted, unwanted behavior that is causing fear or emotional distress.
- It must be blatantly obvious to the human mind that the related behavior is unwanted, there must have been clear signs that it is unwanted, or the dignity of the person must have been violated.
- Punishable by up to a 3-rank demotion in either USM or in the related division, up to a 5-day discord mute, up to a 1-day game ban or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 1-0-5. FALSE ACCUSATIONS.
- When someone is accused of a crime, they did not commit without solid proof or evidence, it is known as a false accusation. Such charges frequently result in unjust treatment or punishment and can damage a person's relationships, reputation, and emotional health.
- The false accusation must have been made knowingly with the intent to deceive or cause harm.
- Punishable by up to a 1-week suspension from the related division, up to a 2-rank demotion, up to a 5-day discord mute or up to a 6-day game ban.
ARTICLE 1-0-6. DISCRIMINATION.
- When someone is treated unfairly or unequally due to characteristics such as race, gender, religion, age, or disability, that is discrimination.
- Any violation of the Anti Discrimination Act is considered a violation of this article.
- Punishable by up to a 3-rank demotion, up to a 1-week ban, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 1-0-7 UNJUST ENRICHMENT.
- Gaining an advantage at the expense of others without any valid reason.
- The recipient may not keep the benefit.
- The court may order restitution, restoring the position of the injured party through the return of the unjust benefit or through equal or just compensation to the injured party.
ARTICLE 1-0-8. BREACH OF CONTRACT.
- A legally binding contract is broken when one party does not fulfill their end of the bargain. The harmed party may receive compensation, and the non-compliant party may receive legal consequences as a result.
- Punishable by up to a 1-week suspension or an exile from the related division
- The court may:
- Order a rescission of the contract to restore the original position of all involved parties or
- grant specific performance so that the original contract is properly executed.
- The court may not order rescission and grant specific performance at the same time but must decide whether the contract will remain valid and be executed properly or whether it is completely invalidated and a rescission is required.
- The court must make careful consideration between the 2 options of rescission and specific performance.
- A rescission should be considered or made if the breach destroys the basis of the contract or makes the contract meaningless.
- A specific performance should be considered or made when the breaching party can still perform to uphold the contract, and the non-breaching party insists on enforcing the contract.
ARTICLE 1-0-9. RETALIATION FOR PROTECTED ACTIVITY.
- Any act committed with the intent to coerce, intimidate, or adversely affect an individual on account of engaging in protected conduct.
- The retaliation / action in response does not have to be a crime in itself.
- Protected activities refer to actions protected or allowed by law.
- Punishable by up to a 1-week ban, up to a 2-rank demotion, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 1-0-10. FALSE PERSONATION.
- Falsely representing oneself to be someone else to deceive, defraud, or otherwise cause harm.
- Punishable by up to a 3-day ban from the game or discord.
ARTICLE 1-0-11. OPENLY CARRYING A FIREARM.
- The visible possession or display of a firearm in a location or circumstance where such action is restricted or forbidden by law.
- Punishable by up to a 120-second arrest.
ARTICLE 1-0-12 THREATS TO KILL.
- A verbal, written, or implied expression of intent to cause the death of someone.
- Punishable by up to a 240-second arrest, up to a 2-day game ban or up to a 3-day discord mute.
ARTICLE 1-0-13. REFUSING ORDERS OF ENFORCEMENT OFFICERS.
- The deliberate failure or refusal to comply with lawful orders issued by authorized enforcement officers in the performance of their duties.
- Punishable by up to a 1-day ban or up to a 360-second arrest.
ARTICLE 1-0-14. DELAYING ACTIVITIES OF ENFORCEMENT OFFICERS.
- Any intentional act that obstructs, impedes, or causes unnecessary delay in the lawful performance of an enforcement officer’s duties.
- Punishable by up to a 2-day ban or up to a 240-second arrest.
ARTICLE 1-0-15. DISCHARGING A FIREARM ON FEDERAL OR MILITARY PROPERTY.
- The act of firing a firearm within the boundaries of federal or military property, except if explicitly authorized by law or regulation.
- Discharging a firearm in self-defense does not violate this article.
- Can be charged with up to a 280-second arrest.
ARTICLE 1-0-16. RESISTING ARREST.
- The intentional and physical prevention or obstruction of a law enforcement officer from committing a lawful arrest or detainment.
- Punishable by up to a 300-second arrest, up to a 3-day ban, or a 1-rank demotion.
ARTICLE 1-0-17. TRESPASSING ON MILITARY PROPERTY.
- The illegal entrance to military property.
- Punishable by up to a 150-second arrest or up to a 3-day ban.
ARTICLE 1-0-18. TRESPASSING ON FEDERAL PROPERTY.
- The illegal entrance to property of the federal government.
- Punishable by up to a 400-second arrest or up to a 7-day ban.
ARTICLE 1-0-19. FALSE IMPRISONMENT.
- The wrongful arrest or detainment of someone.
- Punishable by up to a 500-second arrest, a strike and up to a 7-day suspension in the related division/department, up to a 5-day ban or a 1-rank demotion in the relevant division/department.
ARTICLE 1-0-20. RIOTING.
- An assembly of 3 or more people causing a violent disturbance of the peace.
- Punishable by up to a 400-second arrest or a 7 day suspension in the related division / department.
ARTICLE 1-0-21. ASSAULT.
- Placing an individual into fear of receiving physical harm whether through a physical attempt to inflict harm, a verbal or written threat to inflict harm or other means.
- There must be a reasonable belief that the threat will be carried out.
- Punishable by up to a 120-second arrest, a 2-day suspension from all duties with the exception of government related duties or a 1-day ban.
ARTICLE 1-0-22. BATTERY.
- The intentional, physical harm without any prior consent.
- Punishable by up to a 260-second arrest or a 1-day ban.
ARTICLE 1-0-23. ASSAULT ON AN ENFORCEMENT OFFICER.
- Committing assault on an enforcement officer.
- Punishable by up to a 240-second arrest, a 2-day suspension from all duties with the exception of government related duties or a 2-day ban.
ARTICLE 1-0-24. BATTERY ON AN ENFORCEMENT OFFICER.
- Committing battery on an enforcement officer.
- Punishable by up to a 400-second arrest or a 2-day ban.
ARTICLE 1-0-25. CRIMINAL NEGLIGENCE.
- The act of disregard to the safety or lives of others, causing damage due to carelessness and negligent behavior.
- Punishable by up to a 200-second arrest.
ARTICLE 1-0-26. MASS MURDER.
- The act of killing multiple individuals during a single event or location.
- Punishable by up to a 2-day ban, up to a 600-second arrest, or up to a 2-rank demotion with up to a 1-month rank lock.
ARTICLE 1-0-27. TERRORISM.
- Unlawful violence or threats to coerce or intimidate for political, ideological, or religious purposes.
- Punishable by up to a 2-day ban, up to a 500-second arrest, or up to a 2-rank demotion with up to a 1-month rank lock.
ARTICLE 1-0-28. ATTEMPTED MURDER.
- Intending to murder someone without success.
- A physical action towards carrying out the intention must have been made.
- Punishable by up to a 1-day ban, or up to a 180-second arrest.
ARTICLE 1-0-29. THIRD-DEGREE MURDER.
- Third Degree Murder is when someone kills someone without premeditation/intention through an act that shows extreme recklessness or indifference to the well-being of other lives.
- The act must be of a nature where causing death is blatantly obvious.
- Punishable by up to a 1-day ban, or up to 450-second arrest.
ARTICLE 1-0-30. MANSLAUGHTER.
- Involuntary Manslaughter: The act of killing through reckless or negligent actions without the intent to kill.
- The act must have been in a careless or thoughtless nature.
- Punishable by up to a 1-day ban, or up to a 350-second jail sentence.
- Voluntary Manslaughter: The act of killing in the heat of the moment when suddenly provoked to do so.
- Punishable by up to a 2-day ban, or up to a 600-second arrest.
ARTICLE 1-0-31. SECOND-DEGREE MURDER.
- Second-degree murder is the act of intentionally killing another individual without premeditation in advance.
- Punishable by up to a 2-day ban.
ARTICLE 1-0-32.  FIRST-DEGREE MURDER.
- First degree murder is the act of intentional killing, while premeditated and pre-planned before the act.
- Punishable by up to a 3-day ban.
ARTICLE 1-0-33. POLITICAL ASSASSINATION.
- Political assassination is the act of intentionally killing a public or government/political official, such as a government leader, for ideological, political, or religious reasons.
- Punishable by up to a 10-day ban, up to a 400-second arrest, or up to a 2-rank demotion.

TITLE II—FEDERAL ARTICLES
ARTICLE 2-0-1. JURISDICTION.
- Violations of this Title may only be heard in a federal court or higher, presided by a federal Judge, a Supreme Court Justice, or a bench of Supreme Court Justices.
- Any case involving any article from this title is classed as a federal trial and may not involve representation by a local licensed attorney.
- Federal licensed attorneys may act as legal counsel in any court.
ARTICLE 2-0-2. MISREPRESENTATION.
- A false statement made with the purpose to induce someone to an action with false knowledge. Undeniable harm must have been caused as the result of the deception.
- Misrepresentation can be categorized into 3 degrees. The ruling party of a trial must determine under which degree a charge of misrepresentation falls if the above clause applies:
- Innocent.—The accused genuinely believed it to be true.
- The court may order rescission and/or restitution of the contract or decision, related to the charge.
- Negligent.—The accused carelessly gave false information without checking the facts.
- Punishable by up to a 3-day discord mute or up to a 2-rank demotion.
- The court may order rescission and/or restitution of the contract or decision, related to the charge.
- Fraudulent.—The accused deliberately and knowingly lied.
- Punishable by up to a 7-day ban as well as a blacklist from the related division or an exile from the related division with up to a 7-day ban.
- The court may order rescission and/or restitution of the contract or decision, related to the charge.
ARTICLE 2-0-3. CONSPIRACY.
- An agreement between 2 or more people to commit a crime. May be conducted both formally and informally.
- Punishable by up to a 5-day ban, up to a 2-rank demotion, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-4. VIOLATION OF PRIVACY.
- Violating the reasonable expectation of privacy or the constitutional right to privacy whether through government intrusion or not.
- Punishable by up to a 21-day ban or up to a 3 rank demotion.
ARTICLE 2-0-5. ESPIONAGE.
- Spying on divisions, departments, units, governmental institutions, or federal agencies in order to obtain classified information for illegal purposes. This includes but is not limited to documents, photos, or other intelligence.
- Punishable by up to a 12-day ban and an exile from the related division, a 2-day ban and a 3-day discord mute, or a 9-day ban and a 3-rank demotion from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-6. LIBEL.
- The act of publishing or spreading false information/statements about another person that may damage their reputation, credibility, or rank within CUSA.
- Punishable by up to a 2-day game ban and up to a 3-day discord mute; up to a 9 day game ban and a 3-rank demotion; or an exile from the related division and up to a 12-day ban.
ARTICLE 2-0-7. SABOTAGE.
- The act of hampering, deliberating, subverting, or hurting the efforts of another, usually for personal benefit.
- Punishable by up to a 4-day ban, up to a 3-rank demotion in the related division, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-8. ACCESSORY AFTER THE FACT.
- Any intentional act of aiding or assisting a criminal to prevent them from receiving a penalty for committing a crime.
- Punishable by up to one-half of the punishment to the principal crime.
ARTICLE 2-0-9. ACCESSORY BEFORE THE FACT.
- Any intentional act of assisting, aiding or encouraging a criminal act before it happens.
- This may also result in being charged with the principal crime.
- Punishable by up to the full sentence of the principal crime.
ARTICLE 2-0-10 RETALIATION.
- Committing a crime with retaliatory intent.
- Retaliatory intent is defined as the intention to punish someone or harm someone for revenge.
- Punishable by up to a 4-day ban, up to a 3-rank demotion in the related division, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-11. ABUSE OF TOOLS.
- Using in-game tools illegally, for illegal purposes, or unreasonably, without following the given guidelines and laws. Including but not limited to cuffs, taser, pepper spray, authorization tool, and more.
- Punishable by up to a 4-day ban, an exile from the related division, up to a 2-rank demotion, or up to a 5-day suspension.
ARTICLE 2-0-12. HATE CRIME.
- Committing a crime choreographed based on intents against race, religion, or other personally identifiable traits.
- A separate crime must have been committed that this charge directly refers to.
- Punishable by up to a 7-day ban, up to a 3-rank demotion, an exile from the related division, or a blacklist from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-13. INSUBORDINATION.
- Intentionally refusing to follow legal and reasonable orders given by a figure of authority appointed above them.
- Punishable by up to an 8-day suspension from the related division, up to a 7-day ban from the game, up to a 3-rank demotion from the related division or the USM group, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-14. VEXATIOUS LITIGATION.
- The action of repeatedly or intentionally initiating legal action (lawsuits, motions, or complaints), not for a legal purpose, but to harass, exhaust resources, cause harm, or intimidate the opposing party.
- Punishable by up to a 3-day ban, a 2-rank demotion from the related division, or a 3-day discord mute.
ARTICLE 2-0-15. WITNESS TAMPERING.
- Improperly or unauthorizedly interacting with a witness.
- The interaction must have had the potential to affect the witnesses’ impartiality.
- Punishable by up to a 2-week ban, or up to a 3-rank demotion.
ARTICLE 2-0-16. EVIDENCE TAMPERING.
- The alteration, destruction, hiding, or fabrication of evidence with the attempt to obstruct or interfere with legal proceedings or investigations.
- Punishable by up to a 2-week ban, or up to a 3-rank demotion.
ARTICLE 2-0-17. JURY TAMPERING.
- The illegal attempt to influence a Juror, or unauthorized or improper interaction with a Juror, outside formal proceedings.
- Punishable by up to a 2-week ban, or up to a 3-rank demotion.
ARTICLE 2-0-18. PERJURY.
- The act of violating or falsifying an oath to state the truth, either in writing or in speech when concerning a proceeding. This is also classed as lying under oath.
- Punishable by up to a 2-week ban from the game, an exile from all divisions, or a demotion in all divisions up to to the lowest rank.
ARTICLE 2-0-19. FORGERY/FALSIFICATION OF DOCUMENTS.
- The act of altering or tampering with evidence, documents, messages, or any other type of alterable material in order to justify wrongdoing, frame someone, escape charges or Justice, or to cause an unwarranted action.
- Punishable by up to a 2-week ban, up to a 3-rank demotion, or up to a 2-month blacklist.
ARTICLE 2-0-20. COERCION.
- The act of using forceful or intimidating action to force an individual to act in an involuntary manner.
- Punishable by up to a 7-day ban or a 7-day ban along with a 1-rank demotion from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-21. BRIBERY.
- The act of offering, giving, accepting, or soliciting any valuable incentive with the intent to persuade an individual to commit an unlawful or improper act.
- There must be a malicious or corrupt intent.
- Punishable by up to a 15-day ban, a demotion to E5 along with up to a 2-week rank lock, or an exile from the related division along with up to a 5-day discord mute.
ARTICLE 2-0-22. MISUSE OF CLASSIFIED INFORMATION.
- Leaking confidential information you have been entrusted with, to those who do not possess the authority to access the contents of such information.
- Punishable by up to a 10-day suspension, up to a 5-rank demotion along with up to a 3-day ban, an exile from the related division, or a 90-day blacklist from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-23. ILLEGAL INVESTIGATION.
- An investigation conducted without any legal authority or while violating laws such as legal procedures or rights.
- Only the DHS, the NCIS and the United States Congress may conduct investigations.
- Divisional leadership may conduct internal investigations within their division with approval from the respective O10 or above. If the investigation involves outside factors it must be handed to the DHS immediately.
- Punishable by up to a 10-day suspension from the related division, up to a 4-rank demotion or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-24. DEPRIVATION OF RIGHTS UNDER COLOR OF LAW.
- Whoever, under color of any law, statute, ordinance, regulation, or custom, willfully subjects any person to the deprivation of rights, privileges, or immunities secured or protected by the Constitution or laws of the United States, or subjects them to different punishments or penalties based on race, color, or citizenship, commits a federal offense.
- Punishable by up to a 7-day suspension, demotion to a rank of your choosing, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-25. DERELICTION OF DUTY.
- Dereliction of duty is the intentional or negligent failure to fulfill one's duties properly.
- Punishable by up to a 2-rank demotion, up to a 3-week suspension, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-26. MALFEASANCE.
- Malfeasance is intentional dishonest, fraudulent, or improper conduct, or intentional misuse of power by high-ranked or public officials.
- Punishable by up to a 2-week suspension, a demotion up to the lowest rank in the related division, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-27. NEPOTISM.
- A form of malfeasance done to benefit friends or family through favoritism or undue advantages.
- Punishable by up to a 2-rank demotion, up to a 2-week suspension from the related division along with up to a 3-rank demotion in the related division, or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-28. OBSTRUCTION OF JUSTICE.
- The deliberate and illegal interference with government or judicial proceedings in the pursuit of justice.
- Punishable by up to a 2-week ban or an exile from the related division.
ARTICLE 2-0-29. CONTEMPT OF COURT.
- Contempt of the Court is when a person is causing the court to take longer on deciding verdicts or such other manner by being disrespectful or interruptive.
- Punishable by up to a 2-week ban from the game, or a 2-rank demotion.
- In cases of direct contempt, in front of the presiding judge, the presiding judge of the case may hold the violator in contempt and impose immediate punishment to it, which must be enforced once the case reaches a conclusion.
ARTICLE 2-0-30. CONTEMPT OF CONGRESS.
- Contempt of Congress is the disobedience or interference to lawful Congressional orders or mechanisms. This can include but is not limited to the refusal to comply with subpoenas, the disruption of Congressional proceedings or the interference with witnesses, documents or evidence relevant to Congressional proceedings or inquiries.
- Upon referral by the President of the Senate or the Speaker of the House, the Attorney General must ensure that violations are prosecuted in the name of the United States through the US Attorney’s Office.
- Punishable by up to a 3-week suspension from the related division, up to a 2-rank demotion, or up to a 2-week ban from the game.
ARTICLE 2-0-31. TREASON.
- Committing acts of war against the United States or intentionally harboring / aiding a traitor or an enemy of the United States.
- Punishable by a removal from up to all divisions.
ARTICLE 2-0-32. BREACHING NATIONAL SECURITY.
- The unauthorized access, disclosure, or manipulation of sensitive data.
- Punishable by up to a 1-week suspension from the related division, 2-week ban, or an exile from all divisions along with a 1-week ban.
