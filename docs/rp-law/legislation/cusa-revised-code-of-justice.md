SUBCHAPTER 1: GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article-01: Definitions:
- “Attorney General” refers to the standing head of the Department of Justice.
- “Chief of Staff” refers to the current head of all Staff members in CUSA
- “President” refers to the current ranked President of CUSA.
- "Vice President" refers to the currently ranked Vice President.
- “Chairman and Vice Chairman” refer to the two highest ranks and standing owners of CUSA.
- “Ownership Team” refers to any member of the CUSA Ownership Team members.
- “Commanding Officer” refers to the standing head of a division in CUSA.
- “Local Judge” refers to a ranked Local Judge in the Department of Justice group.
- “Federal Judge” refers to a ranked Federal Judge in the Department of Justice group.
- “Enforcement Division” refers to all members of enforcement divisions of which includes the Military Education Department & Military Police.
- "Chief Justice" refers to the standing head of the Supreme Court.
- "Speaker of The House" refers to the presiding officer of the United States House of Representatives.
- "Local case" refers to any court case that contains articles from Subchapter 6 and no articles from Subchapter 7 as well as not being presided over by a Supreme Court Justice.
- “Federal case” refers to any court case that contains articles from Subchapter 7 and is not presided over by a Supreme Court Justice.
- "Supreme Court trial" refers to any court case that is presided over by at least one Supreme Court Justice.

Article-02: Persons Subject to the USCJ:
- All members of the “[CUSA] United States Military” are subject to the United States Code of Justice.
- All personnel within groups that are allied to the “[CUSA] United States Military '' are also subject to the United States Code of Justice.
- Any Discord servers that are in a valid alliance or in partnership with "[CUSA] United States Military" are additionally subject to the United States Code of Justice.

Article-03: Citizen’s Right to Trial:
- All members of CUSA are granted a right to a fair and just trial before a court issued punishment or verdict is delivered based on accusations and evidence.
- Only the Attorney General, the Chairman, the Vice Chairman, and the President of the United States may bypass this protocol before directly punishing military personnel.
- All members of CUSA  also retain the right to appeal a punishment that has been delivered inside or outside of court via summary judgement or an in-game trial. They may appeal case verdicts to the Supreme Court of The United States directly.
- The only exception are punishments issued by the Ownership team.

Article-04: Territorial Applicability of the USCJ:
- The United States Code of Justice applies to all roblox games under the ‘Experiences’ tab of the “[CUSA] United States Military.”
- The United States Code of Justice also applies to Discord servers that are used in relation with the “[CUSA] United States Military,” such as Branch, Divisional, or Department servers.
- The United States Code of Justice also applies to Discord servers used in collaboration with the community, "[CUSA] United States Military".

Article-05: Attorneys and Legal Officers:
- Licensed Attorneys from the Department of Justice are authorized to prosecute and defend on behalf of the United States Government, as well as represent individual citizens.
- Licensed members of the Judiciary Committee are authorized to prosecute or defend on behalf of Members of the House of Representatives or the Senate.
- In all cases, both the prosecutor and the defendant must confirm the identity of their legal representatives.

Article-06a: Investigation and disposition of matters pertaining to the capability of DOJ
- Federal Judges may only be appointed by the Supreme Court of The United States, a Criminal Justice Instructor +, or the Attorney General of the United States if deemed fit.
- Federal Judges may also be appointed via a Supreme Court vote.
- The Attorney General of the United States reserves the right to investigate any member of the Department of Justice should there be reasonable suspicion of the corruption of a member or a member of the Department of Justice being deemed unfit for service.
- Federal Judges shall be independent from any outside influences and cannot be ordered or commanded by anyone but the Chief Justice of the United States, the Attorney General, the President, or the Chairmen.

Article-06b: Rights of the Citizens
- The Right to remain Silent.
- The Right to choose a legal counsel or consultant of their choice.
- The Right to know the reason and amount of time for their arrest.
- The Right to choose between a Bench Trial or a Jury Trial when being prosecuted.
- The Right to be given a fair and unbiased Trial under a Jury of their peers (If requested)
- The Right to see the evidence(s) of the prosecutor before the trial which the Judge delivers.
- The Freedom of the Press.
- The Right to protest the seated Government in a non-violent way.
- The Right to know the reason and be provided with the evidence of their punishment, demotion, exile or blacklist. Excluding evidence that is deemed classified.
- The right to appeal a punishment issued by the Ethics Committee or Homeland Security. This may be reviewed by the Supreme Court or their overseers.
- The Right to state that the seated government is unethical.
- The Freedom of Expression.
- The Freedom of Religion.
- The Right to not be forced into any other religion.
- The Right to refuse to proceed with unethical commands.
- The Right to ask for the declassification of documents.
- The Freedom of Speech.
- The Right to Bear Arms.
- The Freedom of Political Thought.
- The Freedom of Assembly.
- The Freedom to Petition.
- Right to a Speedy Trial

Article-06c: Applicability of Court Martials
- The Department of Justice holds the power to court martial any member pertaining to CUSA and all affiliated groups of “CUSA: United States of Military”
- The Immunity Clause will be voided if someone is subjected to court martial.
- Staff Members will be exempt from Court Martials unless permission is obtained by a Vice Chairman/women+
- Court Martials must be approved by the Secretary of Justice, the Attorney General, or by a Supreme Court vote.
- Court Martials must be presided over by a jury of all available Supreme Court Justices.
- Court Martial verdicts will be reviewed by either the President of the United States or the Attorney General.
- The Department of Justice holds the power to order the demotion and/or temporary suspension of a military official while under investigation and until court proceedings are over if deemed necessary.
- During a Court Martial, the Department of Justice will be stated as the Government of the United States.
- Only Federal Attorneys may represent each side.
- If in the absence of any Federal Attorneys, the Chief Justice of the United States may deem anyone ranked Local Licensed Attorney (and above) as either side's attorney.
- Any member of the “[CUSA] United States Military”  (besides staff) and their affiliates, are able to be subject to a Court Martial.

Article-6d: Legality and applicability of USCJ amendments and fixes
Amendments to the United States Code of Justice (USCJ) can only be enacted through legislation passed by Congress and signed into law by the President.
- The Amendment must pass both chambers of Congress by a majority vote.
- Once passed by Congress, the amendment is sent to the President for signature.
- If the President signs the amendment, it becomes law and amends the USCJ.
- If the President vetoes the amendment, it is returned to Congress.
- Congress can override the veto with a two-thirds majority vote in both the House and Senate, after which the amendment becomes law without the President’s signature.
- Once passed, the leadership of the Department of Justice is responsible for implementing the amendment in the USCJ.
- The Supreme Court is the only Government body which is able to strike down legislation if it is determined to be unlawful.
- Once all is completed and the amendment is approved by the Supreme Court and Congress and all is given the green light, you may then give the document to a Department of Justice HI-COM with proof of approval for that amendment to be put into action.
- Amendments may be vetoed by the President if it's approved by both Congress and the Supreme Court.
- A two-thirds majority vote is required to override the President's veto on the amendment.

SUBCHAPTER 2: APPREHENSION AND RESTRAINT

Article-07: Apprehension
- Apprehension shall be defined as taking an individual who has violated the US Code into custody.
- Anyone authorised to apprehend someone may do so under the reasonable belief that an offence has been committed, and the individual apprehended has committed it.
- The only personnel who are authorised to apprehend someone are enforcement divisions and units; Military Education Department, the Military Police, the Specialized Protection Unit and the Marine Corps Military Police. Enforcement divisions and units are exempt from providing proof of the reason for the arrest.
- The Department of Homeland Security and the Secret Service are allowed to apprehend someone if an offence has been committed in their guarded areas (DHS base, Headquarters), The Military Police and Military Education Department are not allowed to go in the Department of Homeland Security’s base, and only may enter the Headquarters if their request to apprehend someone inside of it is approved.
- Apprehension is not applicable if the context of the violation is being tried upon in court.
- Valid reasons for apprehension are doing a lawful arrest, issuing a warning or following divisional procedure such as checking the individual's profile. It is explicitly illegal to detain in ways that cause harm, such as waiting for others to aim their guns before releasing the detainee.

Article-08: Restraint of Citizen Charged
1. Any person subject to this chapter, and awaiting trial, may be ordered into arrest or confinement. There is no mandatory procedure nor protocol for this to take place.
2. This shall not be applicable if the person is accused of a charge that warrants only a Local case.
3. The seated Judge may rule that the defendant may be temporarily suspended from his/her position.
4. The seated Judge may enact this article without the prosecution requesting it.
5. The suspended person's position may not, under any circumstances, be permanently taken by another member.
6. When and if a person is ordered into arrest or confinement, the following steps must be taken by the apprehender.

- The individual getting apprehended is to be informed of his rights.
- The individual is to be informed of the reason for his apprehension

7. This article shall only be used when clear evidence is presented to support the suspension, and the reason for the suspension must be directly relevant to the case.
8. This article may also be enacted when the defendant's duties interfere with the timing of the trial.
9. The Judge shall not abuse this permission for personal benefit and shall always firstly think whether the suspension of the defendant is helpful for the current case or if it connects the case in any way. For example suspending a defendant from his divisional duties because he may interfere with the court proceeding with his duties.

Article-09: Legal Authority of Enforcement divisions:
- The Military Police shall be tasked with enforcing the laws in any United States Military Property.
- United States Military property includes the whole perimeter of the United States Military base.
- MP’s (Military Police) have the authority to use lethal force against intruders and internal attackers.
- Military Police may only use lethal force against intruders when there is a reasonable suspicion that the accused has committed, or is committing, an federal or civil offence.
- To detain/arrest a suspect there must be reason and having a lack of reason would result in punishment to them within their division.
- They are only permitted to arrest outside of USM property if this is to protect the military base from armed intruders.
- They are not permitted to arrest outside of USM property for any other reason. Failure to adhere to this results in harsh punishment; typically a physical or administrative punishment.
- Members of the Military Education Department are tasked with enforcing the laws within the USM property of Fort Tedder.
- The Military Education Department may detain and arrest civilians as their jurisdiction is over soldiers and citizens alike.
- To detain/arrest a suspect there must be valid reason and lack of reason will result in punishment to them within their division. They are not permitted to arrest civilians outside of USM property, violation will mean punishment.
- Both Law Enforcement agencies may not arrest any government or federal members.
-  Members of the Law Enforcement Agencies are not to be interfered with by anyone outside of  their division during an arrest unless they are abusing their powers. Officers not on their divisional team have absolutely no power over them.
- Only MP and MED personnel can legally arrest civilians. Any and all other divisions have no jurisdiction over citizens except when arresting them for trespassing. However, if any personnel or citizens break federal law, the Department of Homeland Security have jurisdiction to arrest these individuals wherever they may be, including divisional bases. The Secret Service may only do so when the said individual has endangered the VIP they are protecting.

SUBCHAPTER 3: NON-JUDICIAL PUNISHMENTS

Article 10: Overview
- Punishment from the commanding or executive officer of a division shall be used only in lieu of a summary court-martial and the punishment following. A non judicial punishment may not be issued for special or general courts-martial.

SUBCHAPTER 4: TRIAL JURISDICTION
Article-11: Classification of a Trial
- The 3 types of Court Trial in CUSA are:
a. State Trial (Local Judges)
- b. Federal Trial (Federal Judges)
- c. Supreme Court Trial (Supreme Court Justices)
- Any and all trials are strictly prohibited to happen outside of the main game, all allowed locations for a trial to take place will be publicly stated by the Department of Justice. In all cases, trials will be open to the public. However, the presiding judge may choose a closed trial for only authorised personnel if there are compelling justifications for it, and such decisions are subject to review by the Supreme Court.
   a) Local Trial
- A Local trial consists of a singular Local Judge along with a Co-Judge if seen fit by the standing Judge and/or 7 Jurors. This can be changed if the defendant wishes for a bench or jury trial. When found guilty by the bench (Judge, Co-judge) the defendant may go to retrial with a Jury.
   b) Federal Trial
- A Federal Trial consists of a singular Federal Judge with a Co-Judge if seen fit by the standing Judge or 7 Jurors. Can be changed if the Defendant wishes to go to Bench Trial. If found guilty by the bench (Judge, Co-judge) the defendant may not go to retrial with a Jury, the sentence still stands.
c) Supreme Court Trial
- A Supreme Court Trial consists of up to 9 Supreme Court Justices of the United States on the bench, or a singular Supreme Court Justice member. Supreme Court cases will never be judged by a jury only by the bench. All verdicts are final unless the Attorney General, President, or Ownership team says otherwise in an official announcement to the Department of Justice and/or the CUSA public. The verdict of a trial of which is defined as a case of which must/or will be handled by a member of the Supreme Court must be approved by the Chief Justice before punishment of the verdict may proceed.
  d) Federal Agency Procedure
- Federal Agencies such as the Secret Service and The Department of Homeland Security have their own procedures to deal with certain legal issues. As long as these procedures do not violate any articles under the Code of Justice it is classed as a Non-Judicial Punishment.

Article-12: Charges
- When one is requesting a court case, a list of the articles a defendant is being charged with must be established.
- The list shall be reviewed by a Judge alongside the evidence to determine the validity of the said request. Reasoning: This is in conjunction with the redefinition of Article 5, where the label of Judge being "Legal Officer" is being removed.

Article-13: Proceedings before Trial
- For a Local Case, a Local Judge or Federal Judge must be found, date and time of trial confirmed as well as the assignment of attorneys for those that wish to have one. A body of jurors will also have to be summoned if the defendant wishes for one.
- For a Federal Case, a Federal Judge must be found, date and time of trial confirmed, attorneys for those that wish to be represented by one and a 6 person jury if requested by the defendant.
(Juror’s will be replaced immediately if it is found that they’ve failed to appear for the trial. The appointed Judge may prosecute the replaced Juror if necesarry as it could be labelled as Contempt. )
- For a Supreme Court Justice Case, a time and date confirmed as well as an attorney for the defendant. If the defendant and/or prosecution wishes for an attorney they will be provided for one, and if the defendant wishes for a bench, the Supreme Court member who accepted the case will request 2 or more members of the Supreme Court to be alongside him on the bench.

Article-14: Prohibition of Self Incrimination
- Article V of the United States Constitution protects the accused/witness from being forced to incriminate themselves in a crime. However, staying silent without a prior exclamation of exercising this right, the right is not invoked.

Article-15: Duties of Counsel
- The trial counsel shall be prosecuting for either the Plaintiff or on behalf of the United States Government.
- The Defense counsel shall be defending the defendant.
- The Prosecuting counsel shall be the standing attorney for the prosecution.
- Both attorneys are expected to perform to their best ability, failing to do so will result in actions taken by the American Bar Association with punishments accordingly.

Article-16: Accessory Before the Fact
- Accessory Before the Fact shall be defined as the conspiracy, aid, or influence of another individual committing a crime, without attendance at the setting of incidence.
- An accessory shall be subject to the punishment of the crime they assisted in.

Article-17: Accessory After the Fact
- A person who knowingly and purposely assists in covering up a crime committed by another individual or the criminal’s escape from law enforcement or punishment is guilty of being an accessory of that crime after the fact.
- Not working with or assisting law enforcement to catch a criminal is not considered assistance under Clause 1.
- An accessory shall be subject to the punishment of the crime they assisted in.

SUBCHAPTER 5: TRIAL PROCEDURE

Article-18: Unlawful Command influence
- Unlawful Command Influence is where a member of the military, usually a high ranking officer or Government official, uses or appears to use their authority to influence the goings of military judicial proceedings when they have not obtained any prior approval from a member of the Department of Justice's High Command.
Article-19: Challenges / Objections
- Challenges are motions made (such as motion to dismiss and other motions) that will challenge the ongoings of court.
Article-20: Oaths
- In the beginning stages of a trial, the prosecutor/claimant and defendant/plaintiff must be asked: “Do you swear to tell the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth?” by the seated judge. Both defendant and plaintiff must respond with a statement that is along the lines of: “I swear to tell the truth, the whole truth and nothing but the truth so help me God.”
- Failure to abide by this oath during the duration of the trial will result in perjury charges along with the case being deemed a mistrial.
Article-21: Statute of Limitations
- The statute of limitations is the time limit for filing charges against the defendant.
- Charges that are older than 4 months may not be used during the hearing of a court case.
Article-22: Double Jeopardy
- Double Jeopardy is the illegal punishment of a person twice for the same offence.
- If multiple of the same criminal offenses are committed in two separate instances with different evidence, double jeopardy does not apply, as it is not the same offence.
- Federal Articles may be prosecuted for, even if divisionally punished, without violating Double Jeopardy as they are severe actions that violate the societal fabric of CUSA.
Article-23: Prohibition of Ex Post Facto Punishment
- No person may be judicially punished for an action defined by an article in the CoJ when at the time it was committed, there was no article or article definition that deemed that action as illegal.
Article-24: Pleas of the accused
- The defendant must state whether they plead guilty or not guilty.
- Pleading guilty to an offence means that you have admitted your guilt for that offence. (See Article-32 for more information).
- Pleading not guilty means that you do not agree with the allegations against you and the case will proceed as normal.
Article-25: Court Procedure
- During court there are five separate sections being, Opening Statement followed by the Evidence Statement, Closing Statement, Final Verdict, and Dismissal.
- The Opening Statement consists of a presentation of the defendant’s crimes from the prosecution. The defence team presents a statement to instil doubt into either the seated judge or the jury.
- The Evidence Statement consists of a presentation of witness statement’s or other forms of evidence such as photo proof or audio/video recordings, both sides can partake in this statement.
- Closing Statement consists of a statement provided by the prosecution, mainly covering how their evidence has been shown to implicate guilt. However, the defence is also able to make a statement on how their arguments disprove guilt.
- The verdict is administered at the end of the trial and is the given sentence by the judge if the defendant has plead not guilty, and is proven guilty. If the defendant pleads guilty they are to be provided the punishment listed in the plea agreement. And if the bench finds the defendant not guilty, no punishment is delivered.
- The final stage is Dismissal, it is simply where the punishment is enacted and everyone in the stands is asked to leave the court as the trial has ended.
Article-26a: Objections & Motions
- Objections and Motions are allowed to be given during every statement.
- Objections and motions may be approved or denied depending on the opinion of the seated judge.
- If one of the parties in a case feels as if they were mistreated, they may ask the judge to reconsider by using the Objections and Motions Reversal Act.
- If the OMRA is denied, the party may go to the Supreme Court for a final ruling on it.
Article-26b: Countersuing
- Countersuing defines situations such as, if you were the defendant in a case, and you request a court case against your prosecutor while being sued, it would be considered countersuing, that would be against this article and it would be illegal.
- If the defendant of the initial case is found not guilty of all charges, they may sue the prosecutor referencing this case without violating Countersuit.
Article-27: Refusal to attend Court
- Deliberately missing court hearings is evading justice and is committing contempt of the court. (see article 40).
- Avoiding contact with the prosecution and/or judge purposely throughout a 72 hour timeframe and/or leaving the Discord server or group.

Article-28: Contempt
- Contempt is the offence of being disobedient to or disrespectful to a court of law.
- Committing contempt could lead to legal charges.
Article-29: Depositions
- A deposition is a sworn out-of-court testimony given by a witness that is relevant to a case. Depositions are usually done before court by a representing side's attorney.
- A deposition is required during summary judgements and must be presented to the judge along with the prosecutor's or defendant's statement if there are witnesses involved in the case.
Article-30: Votings and Rulings
- A ruling is a decision made by the judge or jury, normally on the sentence. Rulings can be appealed under some circumstances of which may include, mistrial, bias, and so on.
- A voting is how the jury makes their decision in a group and decides what will go forward to the court.
Article-31: Votes required for conviction, sentencing, and other proceedings
- In a jury, the majority will rule (More than 50%) and their decision will be kept in mind when the judge(s) are making decisions.

Article-32: Plea agreements
- A plea agreement, also known as a plea bargain is an agreement in criminal law proceedings, whereby the prosecutor provides a concession to the defendant in exchange for a plea of guilty or nolo contendere. This concession is often a reduced sentencing. Nolo contendere is a plea by which a defendant in a criminal prosecution accepts conviction but pleads neither guilty or not guilty.
- If the defendant wishes for a plea deal, they will usually be sentenced with lesser punishments once the verdict is reached.
- During a plea deal, no decided punishments between them shall exceed a 2 week ban, 3 weeks rank lock, a blacklist of any sorts, or any other punishment that exceeds the reasoning of their trial.
- A plea agreement may include agreements where additional violations of the Code of Justice will result in an already set punishment. This can also be restricted to only applying to violations related to duties in a specific division. The agreement can still not result in a blacklist.

Article-33: Record of trial
         1. The trials prosecutor, defendant, judge/side judges and attorneys will be recorded alongside the verdict.

Article-34: Time Constraints
1.) A case with both sides’ 3 statements may not elapse beyond a maximum duration of 3 hours.
2.) Any opening or closing statement may not elapse beyond a maximum duration of 15 minutes. Each side’s evidence statement may last up to an hour or 5 minutes of explanation per article for shorter cases (under 10 articles).
3.) Deliberate extension of a case by either side to force the opposite into compromise by time constraint shall be prohibited. -An attorney found with intention to prolong a court for their side’s benefit may be punished appropriately by the American Bar Association. -A case the judge or ruling party allows to elapse beyond the above time constraints will be deemed a mistrial. The Bar Association therefore has the right to investigate the case, and if necessary, punish responsible individuals.
4.) The judge or ruling party has the right to terminate any side’s statements if they exceed the given time limits.
5.) An accepted recess will not count toward the time constraint.

SUBCHAPTER 6 : LOCAL TRIAL ARTICLES

Article-35: Aggravated Assault
- Aggravated Assault is a serious and violent form of assault. The defendant should have had the intent to cause serious bodily harm to another person to be charged with this. Weapons are sometimes included.
Article-36: Assault
- Assault is physically trying to inflict harm, threatening to inflict harm, or otherwise placing an individual in fear of physical harm.
- When oral or verbal, assault is the act of stating a specific physical threat of harm or death to the listener where the listener has a reasonable belief that such threat will be carried out.

Article-37: Assaulting an Enforcement Officer
- Assaulting a MED or MP member is the same crime as assault, but the assaulted is an enforcement member. Sentences could range from simple punishments to harsher punishments, of which could include arrests or warnings.
- This does not apply to any rank-bought personnel of their respective division.
Article-38: Resisting Arrest
- Resisting Arrest is a criminal charge against an individual who has committed at least one of the following acts:
a) Fleeing a division that has the power of detaining and punishing an individual via warning or arresting them, for example a MED or MP member is attempting to detain and warn/arrest you and you are fleeing from them.
b) Threatening an enforcement officer with physical violence while being arrested.
c) Physically struggling to free oneself from being restrained.
d) Attacking an enforcement officer while being arrested (assault).
Article-39: Open Carrying a Firearm
- Carrying a firearm partly concealed eg. down is legal for USM and divisions. It is not permitted for civilians.
- Pointing guns at other players or the discharge of a firearm on base without a valid reason is illegal and will have legal action.
Article-40: Contempt of the Court
- Contempt of the Court is when a person is causing the court to take longer on deciding verdicts or such other manner by being disrespectful or interruptive.
Article-41: Delaying Activities Of Enforcement Officers
- Delaying Activities of Enforcement Officers is when someone purposely and knowingly delays a member of an Enforcement team from carrying out their duties. This can also be classed as obstruction of justice and can be considered contempt if it occurs during a court case (see Article-40 & 42).
Article-42: Refusing Orders of Enforcement Officers
- Failure to obey lawful orders from an Enforcement Officer in situations wherein that officer has jurisdiction.
Article-43: Escaping from Lawful Custody
- Escaping from lawful custody means breaking free from an enforcement officer's legal control or from a division with cuff jurisdictions, often after being arrested or detained by law enforcement.
- This article also applies to individuals that managed to escape from law enforcement such as MED and MP while on pursuit.
Article-44: False Imprisonment
- False Imprisonment is the act of wrongfully imprisoning a person. This is usually a crime that can be committed by any personnel with access to cuffs.

Article-45: False Information
- Giving false information to anyone can lead to consequences such as False Imprisonment and can delay investigations. Some examples could be, falsely informing an Enforcement Division officer/agent, of a false arrest time if they ask you as they may have forgotten the arrest time themselves, of which would cause False Imprisonment. Or providing False Information about an investigation that could possibly make the investigation be delayed.

Article-46: Criminal Negligence
       1. Criminal Negligence is the act of injuring or causing damage to another, without intent, due to acting in a negligent manner that puts individuals in harm when it is clear that such damages are possible.

Article-47: Manslaughter
      1. Manslaughter is an unlawful killing that doesn't involve malice aforethought—intent to seriously harm or kill, or extreme, reckless disregard for life. Thus, while manslaughter is a serious crime, the punishment for it is generally less than that for murder.
Article-48: Voluntary Manslaughter
      1. Voluntary Manslaughter is the killing of a human being in which the offender committed in the “heat of the action” under circumstances that would inflict harm on the killer or another.

Article-49: First-Degree Murder
      1. Unlawful premeditated killings committed with an intention to kill.
      2. Unlawful premeditated killings committed with an intent to cause serious injury where the killer was aware that his or her conduct involved a serious risk of causing death.
Article-50: Second-Degree Murder
      1. Second-degree murder is defined as intentional murder that is not premeditated, is intended to only cause bodily harm or displays an obvious lack of concern for human life.

Article-51: Third-Degree Murder
      1. Third Degree Murder is where the defendant kills someone accidentally or unintentionally.
Article-52: Attempted Murder
      1. The unlawful and intentional attempt to kill someone, that was unsuccessful.

Article-53: Abuse of Tools
- Using tools for purposes that are not required given the circumstances is illegal. For example, shooting someone and cuffing them for no reason. Cuffing is the most common and can lead to damages being paid. Divisions that have teams must say why they are arresting and read their rights (See Article-06b).
Article-54: Obscene Behaviour in Public
- Obscene Behaviour in Public is the act of doing inappropriate things in public.
- The word "inappropriate" includes:
- Sexual behavior in public.
- Disruptive behavior in public.
- Words or expressions that are disrespectful, discriminatory, or offensive.

Article-55: Obtaining Rank by False Statement
      1. False statements such as lying about previous divisions, positions and getting a rank using those false statements is illegal.

Article-56: Discrimination
      1. Discrimination refers to unfair treatment or giving unequal opportunities and benefits, due to certain qualities such as sex, religion, or race.

Article-57: Defamation of Character
       1. Defamation of Character is the act of maliciously spreading false information as fact to a third party that causes damage to someone's reputation.

Article-58: Threats to Kill
      1.  Threats to Kill charges are paired with assault as threatening is a type of assault.
     2. When a person threatens another with serious offences to usually obtain personal profit.
Article-59: Disorderly Conduct
- Disorderly conduct is when one's behavior disturbs the peace, or safety of the general public or a specific group of people. Examples may include spamming, overt trolling, random shooting, or otherwise disrupting the good order and discipline of the CUSA community.
- Disorderly Conduct is a misdemeanor and will be treated and punished less seriously than a felony.
Article-60: Harassment
-  Harassment refers to behavior that is intended to disturb, upset, or intimidate someone. It can take many forms, including verbal, physical, or written actions. Some common examples of harassment include:

 - Unwanted comments or gestures
- Threats or intimidation
- Repeated, unwanted contact or communication
- Bullying or aggressive

Article-61: Retaliation
      1. Retaliation is taking harmful and wrongful action against someone because they have done or are about to take actions against you in a lawful manner. It may involve seeking retribution or revenge. Example: A division HI-COM threatens a lower rank with consequences because they suspect the lower rank of reporting them for their wrongdoings.

Article-62: Violation of the intimacy of private life
      1. The violation of the intimacy of private life is the act of, sharing/leaking private information or direct messages without the consent of the person. (You may only use this article if it's related to CUSA.)

Article-63: Trespassing on Military Property
- The act of trespassing on non-federal properties owned by divisions/military such as SOKOM, USMC, MP, MED, etc.
- Trespassing is when you enter a property illegally that is owned by the Military.

Article-64: Mass Murder
- The unlawful killing of three or more individuals in one location at one particular time.

SUBCHAPTER 7 : FEDERAL TRIAL ARTICLES
Article-65: Obstruction of Justice
- Obstruction of Justice is the crime of stopping government officials from doing their duty/job.
- It can also be defined as restricting a member of a Law Agency from performing their duties, for example restricting a Federal Agent from the Secret Service or Department of Homeland Security from properly doing their duties, or preventing a member of the Department of Justice from doing theirs.

Article-66: Nepotism
- Nepotism is favouritism over friends and family normally for a position/rank, of which is illegal and otherwise known as false obtainment of a position/rank. Other forms of nepotism could include, letting someone in a division without a tryout as they are a family member or friend, picking a friend or family member over someone else, like removing the warning for them because it’s your friend or family member.

Article-67: Political Assassination
- Political Assassination is the planned killing of a politician, or killing prompted by political motives.
- Attempting to ruin the political career of a person via fake evidence, or other unjust means, whether or not it is successful.

Article-68: Conspiracy
- An agreement between two or more persons to commit an offence at some point in the future. It must be agreed upon by the persons involved in order to be considered as conspiracy.
- For example: Conspiring to Murder by plotting or planning to kill an individual.

Article-69: Sabotage
- Sabotage is the act of intentionally disrupting or interfering with someone else's efforts, usually to gain an advantage.

Article-70: Espionage
- Also known as spying, Espionage is the act of obtaining secret or confidential information from undisclosed sources. A person who commits espionage is known as a spy. Government authorised espionage is legal in investigations where needed.

Article-71: Falsification of Documents
- Falsification of Documents is altering messages, documents, evidence, or other alterable material to justify wrongdoing, mislead another to cause an unwarranted action, escape due justice, or place someone in unnecessary danger of injustice.
- If a fabricated document is provided in court, the standing attorney for the person(s) who fabricated the document must provide that information to the judge otherwise it will result in a mistrial and the removal of the attorney.

Article-72 Forgery
- Forgery is the action of copying, or imitating instruments, usually governmental documents, to be used to make people think they are genuine.

Article-73: Discharging a Firearm on Federal Property
- Discharging a Firearm is unlawful, with some exceptions, however firing on federal property is punished further as it puts Federal Agents at risk.

Article-74: Illegal Investigation
- Conducting an investigation is crucial for cases and arrests, among other things. They are to be conducted by the primary investigative team in CUSA, The Department of Homeland Security. Only HLS, Ethics and NCIS can conduct investigations. Any investigation taken by a division outside of Homeland Security, Ethics or the NCIS is considered unlawful. In order for an investigation to be considered legal, it must be approved by the HLS Chief of Staff, and/or the Secretary of Homeland Security, and the President of The United States of America. Investigations without probable and justifiable cause is also illegal.
- Governmental investigations must be approved with the Secretary of Homeland Security’s, the President of The United States’ explicit permission or the Congress Ethics.
- Divisions are authorised to proceed with internal investigations if they have substantial reasoning and proof. It must be approved by their HI-COM, or division head, the President of The United States, the Attorney General, or the Department of Homeland Security Head.

Article-75: Abuse of Power
- Using rank or authority, or threatening the use of such authority to influence people in unlawful ways. For example, demoting someone for simply not liking them.

Article-76: Abuse of Position
- Using the power of one’s position you have been trusted with, in order to benefit one’s self.

Article-77: Political Corruption
- Using political power to influence the ongoings of the world to benefit yourself, not the majority of the population.

Article-78: Illegal Removal by the President
- Removing Congress or personnel with the cabinet rank without proper reason and justifying it is illegal. Taking away Congress or Cabinet and replacing them with friends/family is also Nepotism, and can lead to impeachment.

Article-79: Treason
- Treason is the act of attacking a state authority or one’s own country that they owe allegiance to. This can also be summarised as betraying one’s own country, usually for the benefit of an enemy. In CUSA, betraying the community of CUSA by selling or leaking classified information to potential enemies, can be considered Treason.

Article-80: Conspiracy to Commit Acts of Terrorism
- Conspiring, an act of two or more parties agreeing to commit an unlawful act, but in this instance, it is to inflict terror. Raiding the base with the intent to cause fear and spread hate is an example.

Article-81: Bribery
- The act of offering, giving, receiving, or soliciting anything of value to corrupt a person or influence a person in unlawful ways. This can be anything such as ranks, money, or power.

Article-82: Perjury
- The intentional act of swearing a false oath or falsifying an affirmation to tell the truth in writing or spoken matters concerning a proceeding. This is also known as lying under oath.

Article-83: Group Admin Abuse
- Group Admin Abuse is using permissions in groups to alter anything without good intent or permission. For example: Timmy was a Captain in Marines, he is bad and his division leader demotes him. Bob then changes all of the members ranks that he could out of rage. This is an example of GAA.
- The head of a division/department may appoint their assistant head/co-leader of their division/department without any prior approval and if they do so, are protected under law for that one action. (They are not given immunity, it’s that they cannot be sued for the appointment/demotion of their assistant head)

Article-84: Game Admin Abuse
- Game Admin Abuse is the wrongful use of commands/permissions for unlawful acts. For example, Stuart uses the ;m command to get all the citizens to join a raid. This is illegal.
- This action should be handled with staff, this will only appear in a case if it’s accompanied by another article.
- An individual may not be prosecuted for this article if the conducted rank alter is reasonable and/or has evidence to back it up.

Article-85: Discord Admin Abuse
- Using the power trusted in you in a discord server for wrongdoings such as mass-banning people' for no apparent reason is an instance of DAA.

Article-86: Impeachment
- Impeachment is the process by which a legislative board charges a public officer and removes them of their role.
- If someone who is able to be impeached is being sued, it is not counted as impeachment unless the prosecution specifically adds Article-86 to their list.
- Only the House of Representatives may bring up charges of impeachment.

Article-87: Incompetency
- Incompetence is the inability to do things successfully, responsibly and sensibly.

Article-88: Insubordination
- Insubordination is the act of disobeying your superiors lawful commands with purpose.
- This article does not apply if the command is unethical, unlawful, or against divisional or departmental rules.

Article-89: False Accusations
- Accusing someone of things they have not done or unsupported by facts.
- Can be paired with Article-42.

Article-90: Trespassing on Federal Property
- Definition of Federal Property- Private Property that is under the use and ownership of the United States Government and affiliated departments. These include “The Government Building”, “Homeland Security Headquarters” and “The Courthouse”.
- An individual is not allowed to enter the priorly mentioned places unless a specific event is occurring that allows the general population to enter.
- Definition of Trespassing- Trespassing is under CUSA law, meaning it is a misdemeanour in most cases, however when one trespasses on a Federal Property it becomes a criminal offence. Trespassing is the act of deliberately and illegally entering private property without the expressed permission of the property owner and/or operator or a personnel superior.
- The entering of Federal Property without permission is illegal and will be prosecuted. Permission can be given under few circumstances such as “whitelisting” where permission is pre given by the Federal Government e.g. The President. Other circumstances include invitation by a person holding the power to allow that such as The President or Vice President along with other high ranking government officials. Another circumstance would be to attend a special event. (ex. A court trial)
- Enforcement Officers and divisional personnel stationed on the premises may use handcuffs to remove trespassers from the premises of Federal Property without warning.
- Enforcement Officers can’t arrest someone that trespassed in the HLS Headquarter.

Article-91: Coercion
      1.    Coercion involves compelling a party to act in an involuntary manner by the use of threats, including threats to use force against someone.
It involves a set of forceful actions which violate the free will of an individual in order to induce a desired response. These actions may include extortion or blackmail. Example: Threatening someone to declassify divisional documents under penalty of being demoted.

Article-92: Extortion
- Any person who unlawfully communicates threats to another person or uses actual force with the intention thereby to obtain anything of value or any acquittance, or advantage is guilty of extortion. Extortion can be a result of blackmail.
- For example, taking someone hostage and demanding money. Another example: The commanding officer of a division resorted to extortion,  unlawfully threatening a lower-ranking individual with demotion unless they handed over the desired item or information.
Article-93: Blackmail
- The action, treated as a criminal offence, of demanding payment or another benefit from someone in return for not revealing compromising or damaging information about them, or taking some form of threatening action to oneself.
Article-94 Impersonation
-  Impersonation is the act of imitating persons and pretending to be them for unlawful purposes, to benefit themselves, or to influence another into taking action that they would not have taken if there was no impersonation.
Article 95: Entrapment
        1. Luring an individual who was not otherwise predisposed into committing a crime in order for this person to be punished. For example, a divisional member telling someone to step inside their base then arresting them for trespassing. Telling someone to commit an unlawful act with the intent of reporting or prosecuting the individual for that offense would also invoke this article.
Article 96: Misuse of Classified Information
        1.  Leaking classified information that you have been entrusted with, by sharing it to individuals who have no access to it/are not supposed to have or allowed to have access to it.
Article 97: Deprivation of Rights Under Color of Law
        1.  People act Under Color of Law when they wield powers invested in them by divisions or departments. Such as MED personnel on patrol duty, or DoJ judges presiding over a case.
Willfully depriving a person of a right or privilege protected by the
Constitution or the Code of Justice while acting Under Color of Law is strictly prohibited by this article.
Article 98: Witness Tampering
        1.  The act of pressuring, threatening, or inciting fear in a witness to force them to refrain from testifying or speaking about an act that they have witnessed, to force them into testifying untruthfully, or to change the outcome of the case.

Article-99: Malicious Prosecution
- The act of initiating a case or using the influence of a case against someone with no evidence or completely irrelevant evidence to the litigant's knowledge, or to warrant threats, harassment, damages, or unjust leverage while there is no proper reason for a lawsuit.

Article-100: Assaulting a Government Official
- Assaulting a Government Official is an act of assaulting or harming a government official. This is similar to assault and aggravated assault except that it is done towards a government official.

Article-101: Contempt of Congress
- Contempt of Congress is the disobedience or interference to lawful Congressional orders or mechanisms. This can include but is not limited to the refusal to comply with subpoenas, the disruption of Congressional proceedings or the interference with witnesses, documents or evidence relevant to Congressional proceedings or inquiries.
- Upon referral by the President of the Senate or the Speaker of the House, the Attorney General must ensure that violations are prosecuted in the name of the United States through the US Attorney’s Office.
