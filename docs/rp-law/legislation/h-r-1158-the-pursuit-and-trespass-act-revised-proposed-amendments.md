## Source Text

CONGRESSIONAL LAW PROPOSALS
H.R. 1158 "The Pursuit and Trespass Act"

Section I: Short Title

-
- This bill shall be titled the “The Pursuit and Trespass Act”, it may be cited as such during formal reference

- It may be referred to by code, or common acronym ‘PTA’

Section II. Contents

-
- The below terms shall apply to all members of CUSA Divisions carry the Detain or Cuff tool:

- They will be granted the authority to enter local divisional property in pursuit of a suspect or trespasser.

- When said divisional property is being guarded by (active beyond reasonable doubt) designated personnel with access to detains or cuffs, the aforementioned pursuit will be discontinued, and the trespasser becomes the responsibility of the divisional base it has trespassed.

- Should there be reasonable doubt that personnel of the divisional base will not apprehend the trespasser within acceptable time, it is acceptable to enter a divisional base that is being guarded, such as entering from a different side to the guards.

- Only Enforcement Officers of rank O1+ shall be permitted to enter federal property (the Courthouse, HQ) in pursuit of a suspect when no federal agents are available. No non-enforcement personnel may enter.

- Federal Officers of rank O1+ have the authority to order any Enforcement Officer to vacate the premises
immediately if deemed unnecessary and disruptive.

- Upon successful apprehension of the suspect, the personnel shall promptly vacate the premises.

- A member of personnels failure to do so in under 5 minutes may be considered trespassing and may be held accountable.

- They may be arrested by cleared divisional agents for the appropriate time suggested by the Department of Justice Arrest Procedure or according to respective valid HICOM instructions.

Section III. Reasoning

-
- To increase the efficacy of law enforcement divisions such that capturing a criminal is the primary objective during pursuit without undue preoccupation about whether entry into a building is permissible or not - allowing agents to enter divisional bases relaxes concerns on potential repercussions for trespassing themselves

- To allow federal property to be specifically out of bounds to enlisted enforcement agents, giving full responsibility to federal agents and enhancing the roleplay experience

- To ensure enforcement agents acknowledge the maximum time allowed within a foreign base in pursuit of a suspect before being considered one themselves

- To enforce limitations on enforcement agents’ pursuit of suspects on federal property such that they may be reported by any public member to respective divisional HICOM for violation of the aforementioned lines

- To eliminate the number of trespassers within divisional bases, especially in alternate servers.

Section IV. Waivers

-
- This bill may be waived by the Ownership Team in rare exceptions.

- All permissions enacted within this bill are null regarding the entry of the Department of Homeland Security base; an offender who trespasses into this area is automatically the responsibility of Homeland personnel – all unauthorised personnel that enter this area in pursuit may face exile from their division.

Section V. Proposed Amendments.
-
- In Section 2.1.1, it is stated that any divisional member with the cuff or detain tool is permitted to enter a foreign divisional base, under the condition it is to capture a fleeing suspect or trespasser. Previously, this was limited to purely Enforcement Agents of the MED and MP divisions.

- In Section 2.1.1.2, , it is stated that the divisional member may enter a guarded base, under the condition that there is reasonable evidence that they will not arrive at the location of the trespasser or suspect in time.
