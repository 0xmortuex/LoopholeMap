SUBCHAPTER 1: GENERAL PROVISIONS

Article 01: Definitions
- “Attorney General” refers to the head of the Department of Justice.
- “Chief of Staff” refers to the head of all Staff members in CUSA
- “President” refers to the current President of CUSA
- “Chairman and Vice Chairman” refer to the two highest ranks and owners of CUSA
- “Divisional Commanding Officer” refers to the head of a division in CUSA
- “Local Justice” refers to a Local Judge
- “Federal Justice” refers to a Federal Judge
- “Enforcement Officer” refers to all members of enforcement divisions; MED, MP, DoJ, SS and DHS.

Article 02: Persons Subject to USCJ
- All members of the “[CUSA] United States Military” owned by RenewedJourney are subject to this document of the US Code.
- All personnel within groups that are allied to the “[CUSA] United States Military '' owned by RenewedJourney are also subject to this document of the US Code.

- All members of CUSA are granted a right to a fair and just trial before a punishment or verdict is delivered based on accusations and evidence.
- Only the Attorney General, the Chairman, the Vice Chairman, and the President of the United States may bypass this protocol before directly punishing military personnel.
- All members of CUSA  also retain the right to appeal a punishment delivered with or without a trial, with the only exception to this being the punishments issued by the President of the United States.

Article 04: Territorial Applicability of the USCJ
- The United States Code of Justice applies to all roblox games under the ‘Games’ tab of the “[CUSA] United States Military” owned by RenewedJourney.
- The United States Code of Justice also applies to Discord servers that are used in collaboration with the “[CUSA] United States Military”, owned by RenewedJourney, such as Branch, Divisional, or Department servers.

Article 05a: Attorneys and Legal Officers
- All Attorneys under the employ of the Department of Justice will receive their certification from either the Supreme Court Justice or the Attorney General of the United States. This certification can also be revoked by the Supreme Court Justice, President, or the Attorney General for valid reasons.
- Only certified DoJ Attorneys may prosecute on the behalf of the United States Government.
- Legal Officers shall be defined as anyone who has passed Law School and is currently seated as a Judge.
- In civil matters in which the United States Government is not a defendant or claimant in a case, citizens may represent themselves or fellow citizens in court proceedings although it is advised against as there are professional, certified legal officers and attorneys available to the public.
- Legal officers may set up their own law firm and become private attorneys to the public at a charge.

Article 05b: Investigation and disposition of matters pertaining to the capability of the DOJ

- The Attorney General of the United States reserves the right to investigate any member of the DoJ should there be reasonable suspicion of the corruption of a member or a member of DoJ being deemed unfit for service.
- Federal Judges shall be independent from any outside influences and cannot be ordered or commanded by anyone but the Attorney General, President, Chief of Staff, Vice-Chairman or Chairman.

Article 05c: Rights of the accused
- The Right to remain Silent
- The Right to an attorney (If they do not have one, an attorney will be provided for them)
- The Right to know the reason for their arrest.
- The Right to choose between a Bench Trial and or a Jury Trial
- The Right to a fair and unbiased Trial
- The Right to be given a fair and unbiased Trial under a Jury of their Peers (If Requested)

- The Department of Justice holds the power to order the deranking of a military official while under investigation and until court proceedings are over if deemed necessary.

SUBCHAPTER 2: APPREHENSION AND RESTRAINT

Article-06: Apprehension
- Apprehension shall be defined as taking an individual who has violated the US Code into custody.
- Anyone authorized to apprehend someone may do so under the reasonable belief that an offense has been committed, and the individual apprehended committed it.

Article-07: Restraint of Citizen Charged
- Any person subject to this chapter, and awaiting trial, may be ordered into arrest or confinement. There is no mandatory procedure nor protocol for this to take place.
- This shall not be applicable if the person is accused of a charge that warrants only a summary Local case.
- When and if a person is ordered into arrest or confinement, the following steps must be taken by the apprehender -
- The individual getting apprehended is to be informed of his rights.
- The individual is to be informed of the reason for his apprehension.

Article-08: Punishment prohibited before trial
- No member of the CUSA Group may be punished without a proper trial which culminates in a conviction. He may be arrested or given a minor punishment for infractions against discipline. If deemed necessary, members of the CUSA group can be deranked temporarily.
- Non-judicial punishments may be issued by the Court, Judge or the division the accused is part of. This may only be issued with the consent of either the accused or the Divisional Commanding Officer of the division/branch he/she is part of.
- The President/Vice President of the United States may bypass this protocol.

Article-09: Legal Authority of MED and MP
- The Military Police shall be tasked with enforcing the laws herein any United States Military Property. USM property includes the whole perimeter of the base. MP’s have the authority to arrest and use lethal force against intruders and internal attackers. To detain/arrest a suspect there must be reason and lack of reason will result in punishment. They are not permitted to arrest outside of USM property and violation of this will result in punishment.
- Members of the Military Education Department are tasked with enforcing the laws within the USM property of Fort Tedder. The Military Education Department is prohibited from detaining and arresting civilians as their jurisdiction is only over soldiers. To detain/arrest a suspect there must be valid reason and lack of reason will result in punishment. They are not permitted to arrest civilians within USM property violation will mean punishment.
- Both Law Enforcement agencies may arrest anyone, and may not be questioned in the process unless the issue is concerning malpractice.
4,  Members of the Law Enforcement Agencies are not to be interfered with during an arrest. Officers not in their division have absolutely no power over them, except for the Attorney General, President, Vice Chairman and Chairman.

SUBCHAPTER 3: NON-JUDICIAL PUNISHMENTS

Article-10: Overview
- Punishment from the commanding or executive officer of a division shall be used only in lieu of a summary court-martial and the punishment following. A non judicial punishment may not be issued for special or general courts-martial.

Article-11: Punishment Prescribed
- All non-judicial punishments must not exceed the sentencing which would be done in a normal summary court trial.
- A Non Judicial Punishment must be a strenuous activity such as a physical challenge, or basic general hard labor.

SUBCHAPTER 4: TRIAL JURISDICTION

Article-12: Classification of a Trial
- The 4 types of Court Trial in CUSA are:
- State Trial (State/Local Judge)
- Federal Trial (Federal Judge)
- Supreme Court Trial (Supreme Court)
- Federal Agency Jurisdiction

- State Trial
- A state trial consists of a singular Local Judge and an 8 person jury.
- This can be changed if the defendant or Plaintiff wishes to go to Bench Trial.
- When found guilty by the bench the defendant may go to retrial with a Jury.

b) Federal Trial
- A Federal Trial consists of a singular Federal Judge or a 12 person jury.
- Can be changed if the Defendant wishes to go to Bench Trial.
- If found guilty by the bench the defendant may not go to retrial with a Jury, the sentence still stands.

c) Supreme Court Trial
- A Supreme Court Trial consists of up to 12 Supreme Court Justices of the United States on the bench.
- Will never be judged by a jury only by the bench.
- All verdicts are final.
- Must be approved by the President and the Defendant unless the President is the Defendant.

d) Federal Agency Procedure
- Federal Agencies such as the Secret Service and The Department of Homeland Security have their own procedures to deal with certain legal issues.
- As long as these procedures do not violate any articles under the Code Of Justice it is classed as a Non-Judicial Punishment. (See Article 11)

SUBCHAPTER 5: PRETRIAL PROCEDURES

Article-13: Charges and Specifications
- A written text containing each specification, together with the charge under which it is placed must be submitted to the judge before trial takes place.

Article-14: Procedure Before Trial
- For a Local Case, a Local Judge must be found, date and time of trial confirmed as well as the assignment of attorneys for those that wish to have one. A jury will also have to be summoned if the defendant/plaintiff should choose for one to be present.
- For a Federal Case, a Federal Judge must be found, date and time of trial confirmed, attorneys for those that wish to be represented by one and a 12 person jury if requested by the defendant.
(Juror’s will be replaced immediately if it is found that they’ve failed to appear for the trial. The appointed Judge may prosecute the Juror if necessary as well.)
- For a Supreme Court Justice Case, the Attorney General must be present, a time and date confirmed as well as an attorney for the defendant.

Article-15: Prohibition of Self Incrimination
- The Fifth Amendment To the United States Constitution protects the accused/witness from being forced to incriminate themselves in a crime. However, staying silent without a prior exclamation of exercising this right, the right is not invoked.

SUBCHAPTER 6: TRIAL PROCEDURE

Article-16: Duties of Counsel
- The trial counsel shall be prosecuting for either the Plaintiff or on behalf of the United States Government
- The defense counsel shall be defending the accused
- Both attorneys are expected to perform to their best ability, failing to do so will result in their removal from the Department of Justice

Article-17: Unlawful Command Influence
- Unlawful Command Influence is where a member of the military, usually a high ranking one, uses or appears to use their authority to influence the goings of military judicial proceedings.

Article-18: Challenges / Objections
- Challenges are things such as motion to dismiss and other things that will challenge the ongoings of court.

Article-19: Oaths
- In the early stages of the beginning of a trial, the prosecutor/claimant and defendant/plaintiff must be asked: “Do you swear to tell the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth?” and they need to respond with something along the lines of: “I swear to tell the truth, the whole truth and nothing but the truth so help me God.” Not staying true to this oath will result in perjury charges. (See article 65)

Article-20: Statute of Limitations
- The statute of limitations is the time limit for filing charges against the defendant.
- Any applicable evidence will remain valid up to a year after a criminal offense has occurred.

Article-21: Double Jeopardy
- Double jeopardy is the prosecution or punishment of a person twice for the same offense. If these offenses were at different times this is not applied.

Article-22: Pleas of the accused
- The defendant must state whether they plead guilty or not guilty when asked in court.
- Pleading guilty to an offense means that you have accepted you have committed that offense.
- Pleading not guilty means that you do not agree with the allegations against you.

Article-23: Court Procedure
- During court there are five separate sections being, Opening Statement followed by Cross Examination, Closing Statement, Final Ruling, and Dismissal.
- The Opening Statement consists of a presentation of the defendant’s crimes from the prosecution. The defense team presents a statement to instill doubt into either the seated judge or the jury.
- Cross Examination consists of a presentation of witness statement’s or other forms of evidence such as photo proof or audio recordings. (Clarification: Both teams can partake in this stage)
- Closing Statement consists of a statement provided by the prosecution, mainly covering how their evidence has been shown to implicate guilt. However the defense is also able to make a statement on how their arguments disprove guilt.
- Final Ruling occurs at the end of the trial and is the given sentence by the judge if the defendant has plead not guilty, and is proven guilty. If the defendant pleads guilty they are to be provided the punishment listed in the plea agreement. And if the bench finds the defendant not guilty, no punishment is delivered.
- The final stage is Dismissal, it is simply where the punishment is enacted and everyone in the stands is asked to leave the court as the trial has ended.
- Note: Objections, Motions and Challenges are allowed to be given during, Opening Statement, Cross Examination and if necessary the closing statement.

Article-24: Refusal to attend court
- Deliberately missing court times is evading justice and is committing contempt of the court. (see article 25).

Article-25: Contempt
- Contempt of court or often referred to as contempt is the offense of being disobedient to or disrespectful to a court of law.

Article-26: Depositions
- A deposition is a sworn out-of-court testimony given by a witness that is relevant to a case. Depositions are usually done before court by a representing side's attorney.

Article-27: Votings and Rulings
- A ruling is a decision made by the judge, normally on the sentence. Rulings can be appealed under some circumstances.
- A voting is how the jury makes their decision in a group and decides what will go forward to the court.

Article-28: Votes required for conviction, sentencing, and other proceedings
- In a jury, the majority will rule and their decision will be kept in mind when the judge(s) are making decisions.

Article-29: Findings and Sentencing.
- Judges and the Jury can look over findings and make a non-biased well informed decision on the discipline required for the charges. If the findings of a case are too little or of little use then the judge/jury/side judges will make decisions whether to discipline at all.

Article-30: Plea Agreements
- A plea agreement, also known as a plea bargain, is an agreement in criminal law proceedings, whereby the prosecutor provides a concession to the defendant in exchange for a plea of guilty or nolo contendere. This concession is often a reduced sentencing. Nolo contendere is a plea by which a defendant in a criminal prosecution accepts conviction but pleads neither guilty or not guilty. Presented during the Opening Statement.

Article-31: Record of Trial
- The trials prosecutor, defendant, judge/side judges and attorneys will be recorded alongside the verdict.

SUBCHAPTER 7 : LOCAL TRIAL ARTICLES

Article-32: Aggravated Assault
- Aggravated Assault is a serious and violent form of assault. The defendant will have had to have intent to cause serious bodily harm to a person. Weapons are sometimes included.

Article-33: Assault
- Assault is the crime of trying to or attempting to inflict harm or threaten. Assault is often paired with battery, which is the actual infliction of harm.
- Harassment is a form of assault. Harassment is unwanted behavior that may be found offensive, annoying or hindering.

Article-34: Assaulting an Enforcement Officer
- Assaulting a Police Officer is the same crime as assault, but the assaulted is a police officer. Sentences are more harsh on this.

Article-35: Resisting Arrest
- Resisting Arrest is a criminal charge against an individual who has committed at least one of the following acts:
a) Fleeing an enforcement officer while being arrested
b) Threatening an enforcement officer with physical violence while being arrested
c) Physically struggling to free oneself from being restrained.
d) Attacking an enforcement officer while being arrested (assault).

Article-36: Blackmail
- the action, treated as a criminal offense, of demanding payment or another benefit from someone in return for not revealing compromising or damaging information about them, or taking some form of threatening action to oneself.

Article-37: Nepotism
- Nepotism is favoritism over friends and family normally for a position/rank. It is illegal and otherwise known as free-ranking.

Article-38: Open Carrying a Firearm
- Carrying a firearm partly concealed eg. down, it is legal, pointing guns at other players will have legal action.
- Open Carrying and Discharge of a firearm on base is also illegal

Article-39: Contempt of the Court.
- See article 25.

Article-40: Obstruction Of Justice
- Obstruction of Justice is the crime of stopping government officials from doing their duty/job.
- It can also be defined as restricting a member of a Law Agency from performing their duties.

Article-41: Delaying Activities Of Enforcement Officers
- Delaying entry of Police officials is a crime and will be punished. This is also classed as obstruction of justice. (See article 41).

Article-42: Refusing Orders from Enforcement Officers
- Failure to Obey charges are misdemeanors so the sentences will most likely be lower than serious felonies.

Article-43: Escaping From Lawful Custody
- Escaping Lawful Custody, for example: breaking out of jail or handcuffs, is illegal and will increase a sentence.

Article-44: Extortion
- Extortion is the wrongful use of actual or threatened force, violence or intimidation to gain money or property from an individual or entity. For example, taking someone hostage and demanding money. Extortion can be a result of blackmail (see article 37).

Article-45: Federal Extortion
- Federal Extortion is the crime of extortion and it becomes federal when any wireless communication is used. For example: Discord.

Article-46: False Imprisonment
- False Imprisonment is the act of wrongfully imprisoning a person. This is usually a crime committed by government officials.

Article-47: False Information
- Giving false information to anyone can lead to consequences such as False Imprisonment and can delay investigations.

Article-48: Falsification Of Documents
- Falsification of Documents means altering or fabricating documents, such as messages, formal documents and agreements. Another example is law firm certificates.

Article-49: Forgery
- Forgery is the action of copying, or imitating instruments to be used to make people think they are genuine.

Article-50: Misdemeanors Definition
- A misdemeanor is a minor wrongdoing that is less serious than a felony and is less punished.

Article-51: Intentionally Causing Harm
- Intentionally causing harm is the act of harming someone on purpose to inflict damage.This charge is often paired with batter, assault and aggravated assault. This can sometimes lead to First Degree murder under the subcategory of unlawful killing with the intent to cause serious injury.

Article-52: Manslaughter
Manslaughter is an unlawful killing that doesn't involve malice aforethought—intent to seriously harm or kill, or extreme, reckless disregard for life. Thus, while manslaughter is a serious crime, the punishment for it is generally less than that for murder.

Article-53: Voluntary Manslaughter
- Voluntary Manslaughter is the killing of a human being in which the offender committed in the “heat of the action” under circumstances that would inflict harm on the killer or another.

Article-54: First Degree Murder
- unlawful killings committed with an intention to kill.
- unlawful killings committed with an intent to cause serious injury where the killer was aware that his or her conduct involved a serious risk of causing death.
Article-55: Second Degree Murder
- Second-degree murder is defined as intentional murder that is not premeditated, is intended to only cause bodily harm or displays an obvious lack of concern for human life.

Article-56: Third Degree Murder
- Third Degree Murder is where the defendant kills someone accidentally or unintentionally.

Article-57: Obscene Behavior In Public
- Obscene Behavior in Public is the act of doing inappropriate things in public.

Article-58: Obtaining Rank with False Statement
- False statements such as lying about previous divisions, positions and getting a rank using those false statements is illegal.

Article-59: Discrimination
- It is against the law to discriminate against anyone because of their sex, religion, sexuality or disability or groups of people.

Article-60: Perjury in a Local Case
- Perjury is lying under oath in court and in this instance is in a local case. Perjury will disrupt the entire case and you will be convicted if found guilty.

Article-61: Rioting Resulting In Death
- Rioting or raiding if weapons are involved is a criminal offense.
- Rioting is a violent disturbance of peace by a crowd
- Raiding is rioting with weapons involved.
- If these actions have resulted in the death of a person, it is Rioting Resulting in Death.

Article-62: Defamation of Character
- Defamation of Character is the act of spreading false information that has or may damage their reputation.

Article-63: Threats To Kill
- Threats to Kill charges are paired with assault as threatening is a type of assault.

Article-64: Disorderly Conduct
- Disorderly Conduct is a misdemeanor and will be treated and punished less seriously than a serious felony. See article 51 for Misdemeanor Definition.

Article-65: Harassment
- Harassment is the act of actively pressuring a person when asked to stop. It is similar to the violation of a restraining order and will be treated as such.

SUBCHAPTER 8 : FEDERAL TRIAL ARTICLES

Article-66: Political Assasination
- Political Assassination is the planned killing of a politician or killing, prompted by political motives.

Article-67: Conspiracy
- Conspiracy is an agreement between two or more persons to commit at some time in the future. For it to be defined as conspiracy, the people must agree on it.
- For example, Conspiracy to Murder is the plotting/planning to kill a being

Article-68: Sabotage
- Sabotage is the act of hampering, deliberating, subverting or hurting the efforts of another, usually for personal benefit.

Article-69: Espionage
- Espionage or spying, is the act of obtaining secret or confidential information from undisclosed sources. A person who commits espionage is known as a spy. Government authorized espionage is legal in investigations where needed.

Article-70: Discharging a Firearm on Federal Property
- Discharging a Firearm is unlawful, some exceptions, but firing on federal property is punished further as it puts Federal Agents at risk.

Article-71: Illegal Investigation
- Conducting an investigation can be crucial for cases, arrests and other things. They are conducted by the primary investigative agency in CUSA, Investigations may only be undertaken by the Department of Homeland Security at the request of DoJ High Command. Any investigation undertaken by an agency other than the DHS is considered unlawful. In order for an investigation to be considered legal, it must be approved by the Director of HLS and The President of The United States of America, investigating without justifiable cause is also illegal.

Article-72: Abuse of Tools
- Using tools for purposes that are not required in the circumstance is illegal. For example, hitting someone with a baton for no reason, firing a taser at someone for no reason and cuffing someone for no reason. Cuffing is the most common and can lead to damages being paid. Divisions that have teams should say why they are arresting the accused and read their rights.

Article-73: Abuse of Power
- Using ranks or authority or the threat of using authority to influence people in unlawful ways. For example, threatening to demote someone if they don't host when the then threatener does not have the power to do so.

Article-74: Abuse of Position
- Abuse of Position is using the power of the position you have been trusted with to benefit you.

Article-75: Political Corruption
- Using political power to influence the ongoings of the world to benefit yourself and not the majority of the population is seen as corruption.

Article-76: Illegal Removal of Cabinet By President
- Cabinet can only be removed if deemed unworthy or unfit to be cabinet. Removing a member of the Cabinet to replace them with friends or family is Nepotism (See article 38) and is also a form of Political Corruption. Presidents could be found guilty of impeachment if the accusations of this are true.

Article-77: Illegal Removal of Congress
- Removing Congress without proper reason and justifying the action is illegal. Taking away Congress and replacing them with your friends/family is Nepotism and you could be impeached.

Article-78: Treason
- Treason is the act of attacking a state authority or one's country that they owe allegiance to. This typically includes acts such as participating in wars against one's native country.

 Article-79: Conspiracy to Commit Acts of Terrorism
- Conspiring (See article 69), is an agreement between two or more people to commit an unlawful act, in this case the unlawful act is of terror.  Raiding on the basis of spreading fear and messages of hatred is an example.

 Article-80: Bribery
- Bribery is the unlawful act of offering, giving, receiving or soliciting of anything of value to influence the actions of a person. For example ranks or money.

 Article-81: Perjury
- Perjury is the intentional act of swearing a false oath or falsifying an affirmation to tell the truth.  In writing or spoken, concerning matters to a proceeding.

 Article-82: Rank Corruption
- Rank Corruption is the unlawful use of authority/power to influence those lower in the chain of command than you

Article-83: Group Admin Abuse
- Group Admin Abuse is using permissions in groups to alter anything without good intent or permission. For example: Bob was a Captain in Marines, he is bad and his division leader demotes him. Bob then changes all of the members ranks that he could out of rage. This is an example of GAA.

 Article-84: Game Admin Abuse
- Game Admin Abuse is the wrongful use of commands/permissions for unlawful acts. For example, Stuart uses the ;m command to get all the citizens to join a raid. This is illegal.

 Article-85: Discord Admin Abuse
- Using the power trusted in you in a discord server for wrongdoings such as banning loads of people for no apparent reason is an instance of DAA.

 Article-86: Impeachment
- Impeachment is the process by which a legislative board charges a public officer and removes them of their role.

Article-87: Incompetence
- Incompetence is the inability to do things successfully, responsibly and sensibly.

Article-88: Insubordination
- Insubordination is the act of disobeying or refusing your superiors commands.

Article-89: False Accusations
- Accusing someone of things they have not done or unsupported by facts.

Article-90: Trespassing on Federal Property
- Definition of Federal Property- Private Property that is under the use and ownership of the United States Government and affiliated departments. These include “The Government Building”, “Homeland Security Headquarters” and “The Courthouse”.
- Definition of Trespassing- Trespassing is under Tort law, meaning it is a misdemeanor in most cases, however on Federal Property it becomes a criminal offense. Trespassing is the act of deliberately and illegally entering private property without the expressed permission of the property owner or a personnel superior.
- The entering of Federal Property without permission is illegal and will be prosecuted. Permission can be given under few circumstances such as “whitelisting” where permission is pre given by the Federal Government e.g. The President. Other circumstances include invitation by a person holding the power to allow that such as The President or Vice President along with other high ranking government officials. Another circumstance would be to attend a special event. (ex. A court trial)
- Enforcement Officers may use handcuffs to remove trespassers from the premises of Federal Property without warning. They may also use lethal force if no other alternative is viable.
