CONGRESSIONAL LAW PROPOSALS
H.R. 0354 "War & Peace Powers Act"

Section I: Short Title

-
- This bill shall be titled the “War & Peace Powers Act”, it may be cited as such during formal reference

- It may be referred to by code, or common acronym ‘WPPA’

Section II. Contents

-
- The following Congressional resolution limits the Presidential Office’s ability to initiate or escalate military actions abroad.

- War may only be declared with a majority Congressional approval, with Ownership and/or presidential authorization if the opposite wishes to initiate a campaign against an enemy group

- The Office must be notified at least 48 hours before campaign commencement into hostilities or situations of imminent hostilities, and armed forces are prohibited from remaining for longer than 60 days

- This period can be extended for a maximum of 30 days if the Presidential Office determines it is necessary to complete safe withdrawal of troops (w/o consultation)

- Any military engagement beyond the initial 60-day period or 30-day extension must be ratified by the Congress where the POTUS seeks explicit authorization for continued involvement

- Requests submitted pursuant to Subsection 2.1.1&2 must be transmitted to the standing Speaker of The House for announcement of voting within the Office

- The same terms apply to any ultimatum to be delivered to a hostile force; the Office must be notified

- Congress will have the authority to terminate the use of armed forces in hostilities by passing a concurrent resolution without Presidential approval necessary if deemed unbeneficial to CUSA

- The President may veto a concurrent resolution passed by Congress to terminate the use of force - the Office however can override the veto with a ⅔ vote.

- Although the President is Commander in Chief of all divisions, thereby with authority to issue orders; only the Congress may ratify a declaration of war, set and provide appropriate military funding

- The following resolution limits the Presidential Office’s individual ability to negotiate terms of peace.

- The Office must be notified 48 hours in advance before terms of peace are delivered to the opposition or national representatives are sent to agree to terms abroad

- To declare a ceasefire, the Presidential Office must consult the Office per Subsection 2.3.1, and gain majority approval & Ownership authorization.

- The Presidential Office may draft terms of peace to be reviewed by Congress, and transmitted to the Speaker of The House for announcement of internal voting.

- Surrender of all armed forces may only be ordered in an absolute emergency, not requiring Congressional or Ownership approval.

- The following resolution limits the Presidential Office’s individual ability to form alliances with other Groups or Militaries.

- The Office must be notified 72 hours in advance to review the terms of an alliance, requiring majority, judicial (Supreme Court of Justice), and Ownership approval.

- All proposed long-term alliances (defined as over 60 days in length) will abide by the above terms

Section III. Reasoning

-
- Its inception will ensure that the constitutional powers of Congress & the POTUS are carefully balanced to prevent unilateral initiation of hostilities & unnecessary involvement

- To allow the Office to assert its role in military decision-making, such that a President may not initiate potentially damaging war campaigns without bipartisan approval

- To prevent any incompetence in the Presidential Office to have far-reaching consequences

Section IV. Waivers

-
- This may be waived only by the Ownership team in rare exceptions where war, peace, or alliance is necessary beyond significant doubt

- In the case of temporary alliance (under 60 days), the Office requires notification at least 36 hours before the set announcement time for the announcement of internal voting by the Speaker of The House where a majority & Ownership approval is required
