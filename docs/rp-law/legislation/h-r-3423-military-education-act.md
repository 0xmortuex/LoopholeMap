TITLE I—GENERAL PROVISIONS
SEC. 101. DEFINITIONS.
- “Public training/tryout” refers to all training events for USM personnel (such as ETs, OTs) and public tryouts hosted by divisions/departments/branches for positions within their ranks. Internal divisional events are not public, and thus are not affected by Subtitle A.
- “Special event” shall refer to events that are not ETs, OTs or tryouts. An example of a special event would be a Question and Answer event.
SEC. 102. WAIVERS.
Internal divisional trainings may waive SEC. 111, (b) (i), and instead follow internal documentation.

Subtitle A—Adjustments to Trainings and Tryouts
SEC. 111. EVENT COMMENCEMENT.
- All public trainings and tryouts are to commence at the parade squares, unless explicit permission has been given by a member of the Ownership Team, the President or Vice President to host a mass tryout/selection.
- Public trainings and tryouts may not exceed 20 attendees, in exception to mass recruitment events.
- Once the cap is reached, additional attendees may not be admitted.
- Those who fail to comply with the host’s orders to leave shall be dealt with by the Military Education Department, upon the host’s request.
- This does not affect internal divisional trainings or events.
- Special events hosted by members of government or military officials (with permission from the President) do not need to begin in the parade squares.
- Hosts shall wait a minimum of 5 minutes after initial in-game event announcement (both USM officers and divisional officers) before the event shall begin.
- This requirement may be waived if the event has at least 20 attendees.
- Permission to begin is withheld should the criteria outlined below not be satisfied;
- 3 eligible attendees are within the box awaiting event commencement.
- As aforementioned, at least 5 minutes have passed between initial event announcement and event commencement.
- Failure to abide by the aforementioned criteria is in doing so improper hosting and thus holds the hosting officer liable for their misconduct.
- Aforementioned articles still hold true regardless of event start, in exception to specialised events such as Congressional Bill Suggestion Events, medal ceremonies etc.
SEC. 112. FACES.
- The faces section of any event (in exception to divisional events hosted by divisional officers for their own personnel) shall not extend any longer than 5 minutes.
- In between each face command the host shall ensure a 2-3 second duration is effective.
- Article b, SEC. 112. is to be standardized across all trainings and tryouts.
- Non-pursuance with the aforementioned articles in SEC. 112. is in doing so improper hosting and thus the host shall be held liable for their misconduct.
- Face commands shall comprise of any tricks the host deems appropriate, unless the trick can be considered difficult to spot on Roblox.
SEC. 113. STRIKES.
- Strikes may be issued only after the event host has outlined the rules in full and the attendees have clearly stated they have understood.
- Strikes must be given a valid reason upon issuance.
- Improperly issued strikes may be considered improper hosting thus holding the host liable for such an incident.
- A ‘two-strike system’ shall explicitly refer to a system in which if an attendee acquires 2 strikes, they are dismissed. The same applies to three-strike systems, etc.
- Strikes must be clearly stated; ‘Strike one - Speaking out of PTS.’ for example. An acronym such as ‘S1’ is considered invalid as the meaning may not be understood by newer recruits.
SEC. 114. DISMISSALS.
- For a dismissal to be valid, an appropriate reason is to be provided. This must be clearly stated.
- Event hosts may only dismiss an individual when;
- The attendee has acquired the required number of strikes.
- The attendee is an obvious troller or is causing a major disturbance and immediate removal is a necessity.
- The attendee has acted in a way that contradicts an order necessitating immediate removal from the event (e.g.: the attendee fails the safe chat check, the attendee fails to ping the host in the CUSA server etc.).
- The attendee has shown clear signs of being AFK and has been unresponsive for a prolonged period without any approval by the Host.
SEC. 115. SUPERVISION.
- ETs and OTs may be supervised by members of the Military Education Department that serve in the Intelligence Department.
- Hosts may not decline a MED Intelligence agent’s request to supervise, unless they do not meet the requirements stated in this section.
- Hosts may also be supervised by any member of MED High Command with a paygrade of O6 and higher.
- Military Education Department Intelligence members may supervise other divisional tryouts to ensure compliance with the standards to the faces, formations and obstacle course sections.

Subtitle B—Extended Qualification Implementation.
SEC. 121. IMPLEMENTATION OF CUFF EVALUATION.
- All divisions and departments, from enactment of this bill, shall be required to implement adequate training and evaluation events for members entrusted with cuff or detain tools.
- A certificate with no expiry may be issued by High Command to a division/department member following successful completion of cuff evaluation. Certificates will apply only to that specific division. For example, a cuff certificate in the Special Operations Command will not be applicable to the Military Education Department.
- If any individual within a division makes use of cuffs without first receiving a certificate, they have contradicted official law and are subject to immediate and strict reprimand by either High Command, through the courts, or both.
- All detainments and arrests done by individuals who lack a cuff certificate are invalid.
- The evaluation process must take place before they receive their cuffs or as their first training if all ranks receive cuffs.
- Should divisional high command fail to introduce the necessary evaluations, the highest rank within the division will be held accountable and be subject to legal action.
SEC. 122. IMPROVEMENT TO CLASSROOM SESSIONS.
- Upon enactment of this bill, the ‘Enlisted Training Guideline’ and ‘Officer Training Guidelines’ documents shall be updated to include the following:
- Both events, in their classroom phases, will now be expected to provide:
- A short summary of all divisions and departments (excluding Navy units),
- An explanation of common key guidelines in the United States Arresting Procedures,
- Basic information on the United States Code of Justice,
- And an explanation of key rights, as outlined in the constitution or the Code of Justice.
- Section 122 may be waived in the circumstance that the military officer is not in the CUSA communications Discord server.
- Should these documents not be updated or replaced within 2 weeks of bill enactment, or should changes made not include the required provisions in this section, Congress reserves the right to pursue legal action to ensure the provisions are met.

TITLE II—MILITARY COMMISSION ON EDUCATION
SEC. 201. FOUNDATION.
- There shall be a Commission titled as “Military Commission on Education”, or “MCE” for short.
- All decisions by the MCE shall be concluded through a voting among all its members, with a voting period between 24 and 48 hours as determined by its Chairperson.
- The Department of Defense leadership shall be able to oversee all procedures of the MCE for insights, without any interference in their procedures. Under no circumstances may the Department of Defense leadership influence the MCE’s procedures or structure.
- Any attempt to loophole this by trying to directly or indirectly influence the MCE shall be subject to prosecution by the US Attorney’s office for malfeasance and/or other charges under the United States Code of Justice.

Subtitle A—Structure
SEC. 211. LEADERSHIP.
- The Secretary of Education shall preside over the MCE as its Chairperson.
- The Chairperson of the MCE shall appoint a Vice Chairperson if needed, with a simple majority approval of the seated members of the MCE.
- The Vice Chairperson must be an already seated member of the Commission and shall assist the Chairperson in all their needs in regards to the Commission and preside in the absence of the Chairperson.
- The Chairperson of the MCE shall appoint a Secretary, with a simple majority approval of the seated members of the MCE.
- The Secretary must be an already seated member of the Commission.
- The Secretary shall be tasked with recording the official result of any voting and act as the source of truth for the public on all procedures conducted within the MCE.
- The Secretary shall be tasked with record-keeping and public transparency by maintaining a comprehensive record of all proceedings and preparing them for public release by the Chairperson.
- The Secretary shall provide the United States Congress and the Department of Defense leadership with weekly reports on all procedures of the MCE.
- The procedures included in the report shall consist of a record on all conducted votings and their results.
- Such reports shall not include any information on classified internal military proceedings but only policy standards meant to be implemented across all divisions or general organizational matters to the MCE that do not relate to any divisions.
- The Chairperson and Vice Chairperson may delegate small tasks to the Secretary.
SEC. 212. MEMBERSHIP.
- Each member of the Joint Chiefs of Staff has 1 seat reserved to the MCE, that they may assign to any senior officer they deem fit.
- Senior officer refers to a high ranked MIDCOM member of their division or a Brigadier General or above in the United States Military.
- The Military Education Director has 1 extra seat reserved that he may assign to any senior officer he sees fit or he may take it for himself.
- The Chairperson of the MCE may permanently suspend any seated member for consistent disturbance or disregard to rules.
- His reserved seat may be re-assigned by the Joint Chiefs of Staff member that initially appointed him.
- Each member shall have 1 vote for any voting matter.
- Ties may be resolved by the Chairperson, or are to be considered as rejected.

Subtitle B—Purpose
SEC. 221. DIVISIONAL PROGRAMS.
- The MCE may draft specific guidelines or details to existing educational programs or directives, whether set in place through legislation or executive orders as long as such does not directly contradict existing legislation.
- This provision has the purpose to expand and specify what is not directly specified by legislation and does not serve as a loophole to change the meaning or application of existing legislation but only expand on what it leaves open.
- The MCE may create new educational directives or guidelines to be implemented in all divisions, sub-divisions, or units, unless it directly contradicts existing legislation.
- Guidelines drafted and approved by the MCE can be binding for all military divisions, sub-divisions and units.
- The MCE shall not discuss classified, internal, and division specific policies or regulations.
- All matters discussed and processed within the MCE shall be of the nature so that they may be released to the public.
SEC. 222. USM PROGRAMS.
- The USM Enlisted and Officer Training guidelines are to be under direct oversight of the MCE.
- The MCE may amend the training guidelines in accordance with existing legislation and must ensure the guidelines comply with existing legislation.
